# Political science source review

All 11 initial flags reviewed: **10 closed** (4.5,4.12,4.13,5.1,5.4,5.6–5.10); **5.5 retained**, with a specific inaccessible-original gap. The models are illustrative reconstructions unless explicitly identified as original data.

**4.5 Plott:** Complete original article transcription, [A Notion of Equilibrium and Its Possibility under Majority Rule](https://paperzz.com/doc/8992263/a-notion-of-equilibrium-and-its-possibility-under-majorit...), printed pp.787–806; §II.B–C pp.790–791 and Appendix Theorems1–2 pp.797–801. The familiar pair symmetry characterization assumes exactly one zero gradient; coincident ideal points need separate treatment. Corrected the claim and replaced a small numerical yolk-radius threshold with an open-half-plane majority test at each ideal point. A tiny but positive yolk no longer certifies equilibrium. Coincident ideals are allowed by the numerical test; its angular tolerance is disclosed through the numerical interpretation.

**4.12 Black:** Complete 12-page original, [On the Rationale of Group Decision-making](https://www.scribd.com/document/131579325/On-the-Rationale-of-Group-Decision-Making), Economica15 pp.23–34, §§I–IV, particularly §II median/transitivity proof and §III cyclic examples. Replaced the invalid “one strict local maximum” check, which accepted a second flat plateau after a valley, with strict increase/decrease on the two sides of a unique peak. Cross-side indifference is permitted. Nonconforming curves can permit cycles; they do not force cycles. The source discussion now identifies the actual original figures/sections.

**4.13,5.1,5.7 Downs:** Complete [An Economic Theory of Political Action in a Democracy](https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~lebelp/DownsEcThDemocJPE1957.pdf), JPE65 pp.135–150, all scanned pages read by OCR. §VI pp.142–145 imposes nonleaping parties and extremist abstention; its multiparty example can stabilize at separate modes. Corrected a contrary attribution and distinguished the unrestricted-position, compulsory-turnout reconstruction: two parties at a median form an equilibrium for any voter distribution, including a bimodal one. §VII pp.145–149 supplies the instrumental voting/information argument. The exponential accuracy model and its logarithmic optimum are our reconstruction; the logarithmic formula does not hold for arbitrary convex information costs. Information axes now include the actual optimum instead of clipping it.

**5.1 Gelman–Silver–Edlin:** Complete [What is the probability your vote will make a difference?](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/published/probdecisive2.pdf), Economic Inquiry50 pp.321–326, methods and discussion. The approximately one-in-60-million national average is weighted by turnout in an October24,2008 forecast, using 10,000 correlated national/state simulations, not an observed election frequency. Their social-benefit argument can rationalize voting despite small pivotal probability. The displayed elementary binomial calculation is identified as a reconstruction; tie-as-decisive payoff convention is explicit.

**5.4,5.10 Condorcet:** Complete [1785 Discours préliminaire](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/condorcet/Essai_application_discours_preliminaire/discours_preliminaire.pdf), 175-page primary transcription. Original pp.xxiv–xxv give competence above/below one-half and the limiting majority result; pp.lvi–lxi discuss the eight possible three-candidate pairwise outcomes, two cyclic, and the 60-voter example: 23ABC/17BCA/2BAC/10CAB/8CBA yields A>B33:27, B>C42:18, C>A35:25. Independent equal-competence Bernoulli notation is a modern reconstruction. Removed the overbroad claim that pairwise majority estimates nothing. Heterogeneous competence convergence now requires a fixed positive mean margin above one-half, rather than a merely positive margin that may vanish with electorate size. Hoeffding is credited as a modern bound.

**5.6 Feddersen–Pesendorfer:** Complete [Convicting the Innocent](https://www.haverford.edu/sites/default/files/FeddersenPesendorfer-ConvictingTheInnocent.pdf), APSR92 pp.23–35, model pp.23–24, unanimity pp.25–27, supermajorities pp.28–30, example pp.30–31 and appendices pp.32–34. Also read the complete [2026 correction by Petersen](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/correction-to-feddersen-and-pesendorfer-1998/42500E77CE1431F58A2F611E24DB5388), DOI10.1017/S0003055426101762, including its proof. It repairs the first squeeze inequality in the original Proposition2 proof while preserving the positive unanimity error limit. Corrected the solver to allow guilty-signal voters to mix when the prior is low/the conviction threshold high; the former solver assumed they always convict. Readouts show both signal-conditional strategies, pure limiting corners and both errors. The prior slider extends the original symmetric-prior model. Claims concern the responsive equilibrium selection, rather than every unresponsive equilibrium or a universal finite-jury comparison.

**5.8 Page–Shapiro:** Complete author book excerpt [Individual Ignorance and Collective Wisdom](https://ropercenter.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/2018-07/35029.pdf), Public Perspective July/August1992 p.29, original page image read in all three columns. It explains cancellation of random individual/survey errors and explicitly assumes that available information is accurate/helpful. The card's two-accuracy Bernoulli voting model is identified as our illustration, not their empirical design or a historical-priority claim. Correlation can obstruct concentration without introducing marginal bias. The exact count chart now includes its whole support; it previously clipped high-accuracy outcomes.

**5.9 Feddersen–Pesendorfer:** Complete [October1995 author version of The Swing Voter's Curse](https://faculty.econ.ucsb.edu/~tedb/Courses/UCSBpf/readings/curse.pdf), subsequently AER86(1996), and complete [Fey–Kim August2000 comment](https://www.rochester.edu/college/faculty/markfey/papers/Error2.pdf), subsequently JET107(2002), including Proposition1's proof repair. Original voter counts are random and informed voters perfectly informed; our fixed noisy informed votes are not optimized. Consequently the display gives uninformed best responses conditional on those votes, not a claimed equilibrium of the entire original Bayesian game. Strictly negative odd-count voting gains require one-half<q<1; at q=1 and at least three informed voters the gains are zero. The 500-step exponential-weights curve now reports unilateral regret and identifies unconverged profiles instead of declaring equilibrium without checking.

**5.5 retained — Ladha1992:** DOI10.2307/2111584/JSTOR2111584 returned only an access/JavaScript shell; searches yielded bibliographic citations and summaries rather than the complete original. Original conditions on correlated juries therefore remain unverified. The explicitly separate common-leader reconstruction was checked independently. Corrected its threshold boundary: at rho=1−1/(2p), the limit is (1+p)/2, rather than p, because the leader-wrong conditional electorate has competence exactly one-half. This mathematical correction does not close the original-source flag.
