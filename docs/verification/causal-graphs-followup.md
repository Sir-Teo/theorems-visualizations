## Corrections and reasoning

**3.2: finite-table Markov factorization.** State a finite DAG, normalized conditional tables, and arbitrary versions on parent configurations of probability zero. No positivity assumption is needed for finite tables. Local Markov plus the chain rule in a topological order gives the product. Conversely, sum descendant factors in reverse topological order. No remaining non-descendant's factor involves the selected variable, so the remaining joint is its conditional table times a function of its non-descendants, yielding local conditional independence. This also proves normalization by reverse leaf summation. Fitting the same law does not identify its causal graph, and a deleted graph edge does not universally guarantee an irreducible error. The count is conditional-table parameters, not necessarily identifiable free parameters of a particular boundary law. A sample-size slider with step 100 actually reaches both 100 and 200,000; unobserved parent strata use a disclosed 1/2 convention.

**3.3: autonomy is a property of functions.** The old general definition allowed dependent exogenous noises but asserted Markovness of the observed DAG automatically. Now distinguish general acyclic SCMs from the independent-noise Markovian special case and augmented latent-cause graphs. For example, two parentless assignments with identical exogenous noise need not be independent. The old caption also falsely promised that every unedited finite regression estimate stayed unchanged after an upstream edit. Holding the structural function fixed does not hold its finite regressor matrix fixed. Bars remain honest through-origin estimates for the zero-mean simulation, while ink dots mark the structural coefficients. Plot bounds include every bar and true dot. Only edited nodes and descendants can change values under the same exogenous realization; descendants need not all change.

The tests independently solve the actual 3,000-row fits with modified Gram–Schmidt QR, rather than the helper's normal equations, in 81 coefficient worlds. One concrete fixed-noise comparison changes a from 0.8 to −1.5 while keeping b=0.9,c=0.7,d=0.6. The unedited fitted b changes by approximately 0.0025354 and fitted d by −0.0342143. Fitted c in this particular comparison stays fixed because partialling out Z removes the change in M along Z; we do not claim every unedited fit must move. Population recovery follows from independence of each disturbance and its parents plus nonsingularity.

**3.4: blocked paths imply independence; open paths permit dependence.** Restrict the elementary independence statements to isolated Markov triples. In a larger graph every path must be blocked. State the additional faithfulness assumption for the converse, and distinguish independence as a conditional-law property from independence at one particular value. The independent-Gaussian models with strength r have the following exact correlations, derived directly by expanding each variable in its three independent noises:

| Model | corr(A,C) | corr(A,C given B) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chain | r² / sqrt(r⁴+r²+1) | 0 |
| Fork | r² / (1+r²) | 0 |
| Collider | 0 | −r² / (1+r²) |

A finite interval of B is not exact conditioning. For chain/fork models, writing A=αB+R_A and C=γB+R_C leaves independent Gaussian residuals, but a finite slice has covariance αγ Var(B given slice), generally nonzero. Captions no longer promise that the selected empirical slope disappears. All sampled standardized points fit the axes. Too few selected units get an explicit unavailable-correlation message instead of NaN.

An independent positive-probability binary example further checks the pointwise caveat: independent fair A,C and likelihood P(B=1 given A,C)=(0.1,0.2,0.2,0.4) yield independence conditional on B=1, but dependence given B=0. The law is faithful to the collider: A–B and C–B are dependent both marginally and conditionally on the remaining root, while A and C are marginally independent. Thus faithfulness does not promise dependence in every stratum.

**3.9: exact Bayes and impossible events.** With leak ℓ, l_be=1−(1−ℓ)(1−s_B)^b(1−s_E)^e. Algebra gives l_00 l_11−l_10 l_01=−(1−ℓ)s_Bs_E. Independent nondegenerate priors multiply that determinant by a positive factor; posterior normalization divides by P(alarm)². Both active strengths and ℓ<1 therefore give negative association. A zero strength gives a factorizing posterior, possibly with a zero margin and undefined odds ratio. Deterministic AND after its effect fixes both causes to 1, rather than inducing the formerly claimed positive dependence; the replacement positive example uses likelihoods (0.1,0.2,0.2,0.9), whose determinant is positive.

When both strengths and leak are zero, no alarm-conditioned table exists. An alarm plus no quake can also be impossible even when an alarm alone is possible. These cases display undefined conditional probabilities and no invented zero bars. The prior remains available. Mosaic rectangles now have their actual probability areas, including tiny/zero cells; removed fixed insets that could invert small rectangles. Missing columns never cause division by zero. Probability-one bar labels fit the expanded axes.
