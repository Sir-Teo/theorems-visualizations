# Stochastic processes: source verification, 18 October 2026

Scope: the six existing source flags in this cloud checkout, plus the shared branching-process edge case exposed by that review. Three flags are closed; three remain with explicit missing evidence. Closures certify the whole item; a checked formula alone does not clear historical or board-provenance gaps.

## Primary evidence read

- [Watson & Galton (1875), pp. 138–144, complete scan](https://math.uchicago.edu/~shmuel/Modeling/Galton%20Watson.pdf): the problem, iterated generating functions, surname-count question, and the erroneous root selection on p. 143 were read in full. The original binomial example is independently recomputed in the tests. This paper does not settle the separate Bienaymé/Steffensen historical claims.
- [Pólya (1921), Mathematische Annalen 84, pp. 149–160, complete GDZ original](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN235181684_0084/LOG_0016.pdf): all sections read from the scan/OCR, including §2 lattice probabilities/integral, §3 asymptotics, §§4–5 the first-return generating-function argument, §6 two-walker encounters, and §7 special numerical formulas. The scan has no usable text layer. It establishes the dimension dichotomy but does not supply the displayed later three-dimensional return constant or its later historical attribution.
- [Kolmogoroff (1936), Mathematische Annalen 112, pp. 155–160, complete GDZ original](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN235181684_0112/LOG_0015.pdf): all four sections read. Detailed balance is equation (9), p. 159; the necessary and sufficient cyclic product condition is equation (10), with the discrete-time case requiring only a unit time interval. Equation (10) was also visually inspected to verify the formula after OCR.
- [Anantharam & Tsoucas (1988), A Proof of the Markov Chain Tree Theorem, UMD TR 88-97, complete author report](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/bitstreams/e2a2bec3-3f01-4ac6-aee4-4a0803491a0e/download): complete report read, including the irreducible theorem, last-exit tree construction and reverse transition argument, and the reducible extension. In-tree orientation and product weights agree with the implementation. Its introduction reports the Hill attribution; Hill's original itself remains unread.
- [Hasbro A4756 official instructions (2021), complete two-page sheet](https://instructions.hasbro.com/api/download/A4756_en-us_chutes-and-ladders-board-game-instructions.pdf): both pages read, including LET'S PLAY and WIN. They establish the start off-board, ideal six-sector spinner, exact-finish/overshoot-stays rule and the 80→100 ladder. They do **not** depict all jump endpoints. A product-box image was not accepted as board evidence.
- [Paul & Tatiana Ehrenfest (1907), Physikalische Zeitschrift 8, pp. 311–314, complete original scan from Leiden](https://www.lorentz.leidenuniv.nl/IL-publications/sources/Ehrenfest_07b.pdf): all seven sections and notes read. §6 defines numbered balls, sampling with replacement and moving the selected ball between urns; §§3–7 set out the recurrence/reversal objections and the limited purpose of the urn example. The current account now follows this concrete argument rather than calling recurrence physically irrelevant.
- [Levin & Peres, Markov Chains and Mixing Times, 2nd ed., author PDF](https://pages.uoregon.edu/dlevin/MARKOV/markovmixing.pdf): complete relevant §1.5 (stationarity and reciprocal mean return), §2.1 (first-step equations), and §2.3 (hypercube and Ehrenfest projection) read. The text explicitly warns of period two in the non-lazy walk. This supports the modern mathematical reconstruction rather than original-paper historical priority.

PDFs and OCR are temporary under `/tmp/stoch-primary`; URLs and sections above form the durable evidence trail. Publisher previews, bibliographic metadata and search summaries are not full original-paper readings.
