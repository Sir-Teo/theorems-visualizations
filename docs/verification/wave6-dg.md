## Mathematical and visual choices

The shape matrix is computed from analytic first/second derivatives as I⁻¹II, then converted to an orthonormal tangent basis for its symmetric eigenproblem. Coordinate shears leave the physical point fixed and exhibit why coordinate columns need not produce an ordinary symmetric matrix. Normal reversal changes S and H while preserving K. Centering and scaling a nearly scalar symmetric matrix avoids losing tiny anisotropy in the shared eigensolver.

Taylor examples display an actual cubic remainder. Meusnier's circles lie on the sphere, have a shared tangent, and distinguish total curvature from its signed normal component. The torus correctly separates the zero conditions for K and H. Finite Gauss-image integrals count signed multiplicity, including cancellation across a fold, and the convergence chart plots their ratio minus pointwise K. The patch view is translated to its probe and magnified by 1/h; this display transform is disclosed, and the area calculation uses the original patch. Its normal image remains on the whole unit sphere.

Point types use exact model eigenvalue signs and distinguish planar from parabolic points. Higher-order terms demonstrate the limits of degenerate quadratic data. Dupin contours are explicitly sampled, clipped to a declared finite window, and accompanied by an analytic uniform error bound. Catenoid principal/asymptotic curves are explicit solutions, not undocumented numerical traces. Graph and revolution scenes compute their actual fundamental forms.

Spheroid equality is determined by exact axis equality, with a factored curvature gap retaining neighboring nonzero values. Four ellipsoid markers come from analytic coordinates; a numerical probe gap does not certify a root count. The compact-surface example uses an enclosing sphere and actual inward curvatures. Its planar-disk mode shows the boundary hypothesis failing: the inward radial direction there is tangent, not a surface normal. Irrelevant semiaxis controls are hidden. The selected umbilic is an integer control; continuous parameters retain raw unsnapped values.
