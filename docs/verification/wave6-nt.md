## Mathematical scope

All rational, determinant, mediant, quadratic-state and Pell calculations use BigInt integers. Negative rational inputs use floor division. Square radicands are visibly excluded, nonsquare inputs need not be squarefree, and general (a+√d)/b states allow negative intermediate denominators. Repeated integer states certify periods. No floating-point tail comparison decides a cycle or a record.

Best approximations explicitly distinguish second-kind |qα−p| from first-kind |α−p/q|; the original outline’s claim about all records was corrected. The exact first-kind example 4/3 for √2 is not a convergent. Legendre’s criterion is derived using the second-kind theorem, and the Pell proof uses it to establish minimality and completeness. Positive and negative norms, one versus two periods, trivial solutions and signed solutions are distinguished. Display abbreviations preserve the full underlying integers.

Euler digits are independently certified against a 240-term factorial enclosure. Small quadratic errors use exact norms, avoiding catastrophic subtraction. Gauss experiments use common digits of exact 256-bit intervals, retain a >10 tail bin and report the retained sample count. Khinchin/Lévy experiments certify only a finite common prefix of a seeded 2048-bit interval. They do not assert randomness or an infinite expansion for a rational machine number. Uniform first digits differ from the stationary law; ensemble convergence and individual almost-sure frequencies are separate. K is bracketed with an explicit tail bound and outward-rounded display endpoints. √2 and e are documented exceptions, and no typicality claim about π is made.
