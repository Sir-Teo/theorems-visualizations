# Imaging source review: chapters 3–4

Reviewed the twelve open checks against the full relevant source passages, with independent numerical and browser checks. Two flags are closed (3.4 and 4.9); ten remain for the precise original-paper/history gaps below. Modern textbook corroboration does not silently stand in for an unread historical original.

## Full sources consulted

- Bracewell and Riddle, *Inversion of Fan-Beam Scans in Radio Astronomy*, Astrophysical Journal 150 (1967), 427–434, complete paper: https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1967ApJ...150..427B. Section II pp. 429–431 proves the Fourier-slice identity and reconstructs through filtering/backprojection; pp. 431–434 distinguish finite angular approximation and sampling. Its authors explicitly cite Bracewell 1956 as earlier work. The 1956 CSIRO original returned 403, so the earlier date is corroborated by this contemporary primary account, rather than falsely recorded as a directly read paper.
- Kak and Slaney, *Principles of Computerized Tomographic Imaging*, author-hosted full chapters: https://www.slaney.org/pct/CTI_Ch03.pdf (§3.2 pp.56–59 Eqs.7–23; §3.3 pp.63–65 Eqs.25–35; pp.72–73 Eqs.61,65); https://www.slaney.org/pct/CTI_Ch06.pdf (§6.2 pp.211–218 Born/Rytov including Eqs.85–86; §6.3 pp.218–223 Eqs.92–106); https://www.slaney.org/pct/CTI_Ch07.pdf (§7.1 pp.275–283 Eqs.4,12, §7.2 pp.283–284, §7.3 p.284, §7.4 pp.285–290 Eq.32, bibliography pp.295–296). Read the relevant complete arguments and scope discussions, not abstracts. Kak is a coauthor of the original SART method; the book describes its full bilinear/Hamming pipeline, which this panel does not reproduce.
- Budinger, *Time-of-Flight Positron Emission Tomography: Status Relative to Conventional PET*, JNM 24 (1983),73–78: https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/24/1/73.full.pdf. Read the complete article body pp.73–77 and its history/references. Eq.1 gives cΔt/2 (300 ps→4.5 cm); Eqs.6–7 separate √(D/Δx) SNR gain from D/Δx effective-count gain, for matched efficiency and spatial resolution. Nonuniform distributions and changed detector efficiencies limit the comparison.
- Cormack, Nobel lecture, full historical/technical account pp.551–556: https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/cormack-lecture.pdf. Independent inverse problem, summer 1963 two-day Co 60 experiment with DavidHennage, aluminium/Lucite phantom, later discovery of Radon. This does not establish which experimental figure belongs specifically to the 1963 versus 1964 papers.
- Hounsfield, Nobel lecture, full early development passage pp.568–572: https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/hounsfield-lecture.pdf. Gamma-ray nine-day experiment, X-ray nine-hour experiment, preserved/fresh brain tests and clinical prototype. The lecture's first-patient year differs from commonly repeated chronology; removed the unsupported year from this panel, and do not treat the lecture as a substitute for the contemporary 1973 paper's exact scanner specification.
