## Corrections

4.1 now requires self-adjointness, lower boundedness, nonzero admissible form-domain states and the applicable fermion symmetry. It separates an energy bound from a state bound requiring a spectral gap, handles ground degeneracy, and qualifies excited discrete levels/multiplicity. Hydrogen energy and normalization use closed radial integrals for all three families; overlap quadrature alone is numerical. The Gaussian curve covers the actual slider maximum1.5. Previously rational p4 displayed the actual14.5714hartree readout beside a **clamped0.44marker**, and high densities were clipped: all energy values, endpoint markers and radial peaks now fit without falsification. Radial finite-window/whole-domain normalization is explicit.

4.2 requires independent basis functions/positive-definite Gram matrix, and nested spaces for interlacing. The 700-point finite-difference comparison is consistently called a numerical reference, not exact. When the user requests level3 with N1, the old code silently plotted level1 against the level3 reference; now the unavailable Ritz state is omitted and explained. All displayed convergence curves determine bounds; the bump is a rescaled shape. The secular panel contains **all N roots** and inserts each root into its sampled graph, avoiding missed close zero crossings. Its determinant is evaluated from the equivalent eigenvalue product, independently checked against direct determinants. Reference inequalities and finite interlacing checks illustrate, and do not prove, the theorem.

4.3 explicitly uses an infinite-mass nucleus, nonrelativistic atomic units, normalized spatial product and antisymmetric spin singlet. Effective screening is a trial parameter rather than a universal literal charge. The admissible optimum requires Z>5/16. The H⁻ upper bound above −0.5 cannot prove no binding; every contrary claim was corrected. Unsupported high-precision numerical ground energies were removed as described above. Axes now contain all kinetic/nuclear/repulsion curves and both density peaks at every permitted input. Parameter axes have margin around endpoint markers. No numerical integration is presented as a binding theorem.
