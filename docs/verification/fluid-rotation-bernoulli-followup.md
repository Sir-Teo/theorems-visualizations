## Corrections and independent derivations

**3.6, Rankine vortex.** Radial Euler balance and vertical hydrostatic balance give the pressure and surface without an invalid common Bernoulli constant through the rotational core. Local fluid spin is half vorticity; it differs from orbital rotation outside the core. For nonzero circulation the velocity derivative is undefined at the ideal core edge; at zero circulation it is zero everywhere. A finite paddle's material orientation is not predicted by local spin alone. Circulation controls now cover both signs and zero, and the actual velocity-peak handle follows the signed profile. The displayed model time is animation time divided by12.

The previous surface was clipped onto a floor and called an air core. The complete analytic curve is now visible, including arbitrarily deep values within the finite control range. A below-floor prediction is explicitly invalid connected-liquid continuation, rather than a solved dry core. Only the wet region is filled. Pressure/depression use disclosed chosen density, gravity and floor inputs. Dynamic velocity/surface bounds include all extrema, and the wheel plus spoke fits the top view.

**3.11, Taylor–Proudman.** Reduced pressure includes gravity and centrifugal potential. The statement is a steady, leading geostrophic balance with nonzero uniform rotation; slow unsteady use additionally needs a small time derivative. Taking its curl directly gives height independence of all three components. Impermeability makes vertical flow vanish only on columns connected to the stated boundary. A stagnant column and its circulation are not uniquely selected by the theorem.

The rotating illustration explicitly chooses a height-independent zero-circulation cylinder exterior and stagnant interior. With unit Coriolis parameter the exterior streamfunction is `psi=y(1−1/r²)`, reduced pressure is `−psi`, and the interior pressure can be zero, continuous at the cylindrical edge. These chosen fields and the exact nonrotating sphere potential comparison are checked independently, not falsely attributed to the MIT column figure. The sphere comparison obeys the plane-floor condition and has no finite lid. Curves in a horizontal slice are not3D trajectories where vertical velocity is nonzero.

Previously solid points were counted as zero-speed fluid, stagnant fluid was not always zero, and nonrotating spread was asserted nonzero even at special positions. Readouts now distinguish solid, stagnant fluid and other fluid, exclude solid points from speed spread, and use the actual field. Streamline integration stops at zero speed. Only the stated colormap saturates; numerical speeds remain unclipped.

**4.3, unsteady Bernoulli and the U-tube.** The theorem requires a connected region with a single-valued potential. Its spatial constant may depend on time, and subtracting its time antiderivative is the correct gauge change. The acceleration line integral is `Qdot integral(ds/A)`, rather than a spatial integral of the potential's time derivative itself.

The original length control actually specified inertance, could produce negative arm heights, retained inconsistent time after edits and doubled unequal half-cycles for its period. It now specifies the actual total path length at equilibrium: bottom-link length0.5m, left area1m², right area`a`, equal rest heights `he=(L−0.5)/2`. For left displacement `x`, continuity gives `h1=he+x`, `h2=he−x/a`, `xdot=−Q`, and actual inertance is `I(x)=h1+0.5+h2/a=I0+c x`, with `c=1−1/a²`. Bernoulli gives `Qdot=(k x+c Q²/2)/I(x)`, `k=g(1+1/a)`.

The energy per chosen density is `E=I(x)Q²/2+k x²/2`; differentiating using the two equations cancels exactly. With turning amplitude `A`, its first integral gives the full period

`T=2/sqrt(k) integral[-pi/2,pi/2] sqrt(I0+c A sin(theta)) dtheta`.

The equal-arm solution is exactly harmonic within the ideal1D model; unequal arms have unequal half-cycles and a finite-amplitude period below the linearized period by concavity. The displayed measured period uses first and third zero crossings. Amplitude is a fraction0–0.9 of `he min(1,a)`, keeping both arms wet at both turning points. Zero amplitude is stationary and has no measured period; the quadrature then gives the natural-period limit. Native parameter edits reset state, history and animation time together. Tube widths scale with areas, while horizontal length is explicitly schematic. Entrance/bend corrections, viscosity, capillarity and unequal gas pressure are excluded.
