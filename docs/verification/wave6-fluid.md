## Models and scope

- Newtonian stress uses the symmetric velocity gradient, with positive pressure compressive and the explicit convention Aᵢⱼ=∂ⱼuᵢ. Uniform translation and rigid spin do not create viscous stress. Opposite faces have opposite tractions; a physical Mohr circle uses equal coordinate scales. Local spin is half the vorticity.
- No-slip and impermeability are distinct boundary assumptions. The exact flat-wall finite-Navier-slip comparison satisfies both wall laws. It does not claim that the bulk equations prove no-slip or that a curved wall has the same scalar condition.
- The Navier–Stokes example is an exact two-dimensional periodic Taylor–Green solution. The full pressure gradient, nonlinear advection, time change and viscous acceleration are evaluated at the selected point. It is not a numerical cavity simulation or a general existence theorem. Density is1kg/m³.
- The Reynolds-number comparison uses exact impulsive-plate profiles at corresponding dimensionless ages. Geometry, initial/boundary conditions and other relevant groups must also match. No universal turbulence threshold follows from Re alone.
- Buckingham Π uses the rank-three dimension matrix of five positive variables. Both basis products can be selected with actual buttons. Integer dimension coefficients are accumulated as compensated repeated signed terms: a valid0.1-scaled drag basis remains exactly dimensionless, while a tiny nonzero viscosity exponent is not hidden by a tolerance. Unit changes do not supply a drag law.
- Combined Couette–Poiseuille flux uses the sum of wall speeds. Signed wall and pressure powers and exact interior extrema are retained. Wall-on-fluid traction signs are explicit. Circular-pipe flow is a steady fully developed laminar model; its chosen controls keep Re≤312.5. Darcy64/Re is distinguished from a zero-flow undefined value.
- Energy compares periodic decay with moving-wall driven Couette flow, both per unit fluid volume. The boundary power is retained. Pointwise strain dissipation is not confused with μ|∇u|² or a vorticity-squared identity that needs boundary assumptions.
- The Rayleigh ideal jump has an incompatible wall/time corner for nonzero speed and singular initial wall loading. These values are unavailable rather than invented finite numbers. Zero drive is identically at rest. The erfc evaluator retains logarithmic tails after velocity underflow. Its scaled Gaussian quadrature has a uniform omitted-integral tail bound exp(−79)/2; the adaptive Simpson estimate is not a certified roundoff enclosure. Physical profile sampling follows the true diffusion width, including subpixel widths. Wall-load logarithms avoid forming an underflowed product.
- The oscillating plate is a steady periodic solution, distinct from from-rest startup. Velocity has physical units; the envelope uses the imposed amplitude rather than the instantaneous wall velocity. Exact eighth-turn phases retain zero wall speed and zero traction. Signed instantaneous work can return energy to the wall.
- Lamb–Oseen flow is the positive-age diffusion of point circulation, with a regular axis and stable exponential differences. The nonzero speed maximum excludes the trivial root. Finite-window circulation differs from the whole-plane value. Nonzero circulation has infinite whole-plane kinetic energy.
- Burgers flow uses imposed three-dimensional strain with two transverse contractions a/2 and axial extension a. Its exact transient Gaussian width is separately identified; the zero-strain limit freely diffuses and has no finite steady core. Meridional arrows and field coordinates are explicitly rescaled. The unbounded background strain is not a closed finite-energy system. No general vortex-stability theorem is inferred from introductory source pages.
