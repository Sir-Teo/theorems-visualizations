## 4.6: exact radial model and global radius comparison

The former 48×48 box integration truncated a broad surround; at k=1 it therefore failed the whole-plane uniform-cancellation identity. Freely moved large disks/annuli could extend past the visible/integrated box. The old sampled preferred-size marker omitted both radius0 and the exact endpoint6, and its ON-size explanation did not cover OFF polarity.

The replacement states a bounded **concentric** authored family. With fixed centre sigma .8, surround sigma `.8*ratio`, ratio1.5–5, weight k0–1.2 and radius0–6, unit-integral 2D Gaussian disk mass is exactly `F_sigma(r)=1-exp(-r²/(2sigma²))`. Signed disk drive `D=F_c-kF_s`; annulus drive `D(r+1.5)-D(r)`; whole-plane drive `1-k`. Uniform cancellation at k=1 is exact before the baseline and leaves rate20 afterward. The displayed field is a finite view, not the integration domain. Distances/strengths, 20-spikes/s baseline and60 gain are authored. Polarity changes the signed drive; `max(0,20+60*s*D)` explicitly rectifies the rate, so ON/OFF rates need not mirror each other. Actual Kuffler timing/adaptation/irregularity are not falsely replicated.

For r>0,
`D′(r)/r=exp(-r²/(2sc²))/sc²-k exp(-r²/(2ss²))/ss²`.
Because `ratio²>k` everywhere offered, k>0 gives one positive stationary radius
`r*²=2[log(ratio²)-log(k)]/[1/sc²-1/ss²]`.
The log difference avoids overflow even at the smallest positive double k. The derivative is positive before r* and negative afterward; k=0 is monotone. ON drive's maximum is at the interior stationary point if present or an endpoint; OFF rate's maximum is at the minimum drive, hence an endpoint. Comparing all these finite candidates gives the global maximum on[0,6] and retains exact numerical ties. Rectification does not create a higher candidate. The curve includes zero, six, the selected radius and the stationary point when interior. The annulus/uniform response remains separate from the disk tuning curve and its probe.

Full rate bounds follow from `-k<=D<=1`: every offered authored rate is in[0,92]. ## 4.8: exact ramp convolution and limited perceptual inference

The old item reversed the Taylor regime, asserted exact finite zero plateaus despite Gaussian tails, asserted extrema exactly at the knees, and remapped normalized filter output into a fake perceived-brightness strip. It used edge-padded finite sampled convolution without declaring that approximation.

The replacement is explicitly a **Model**. Its input rises from .25 to .8, with authored centre3.5–6.5 and width0–7. Positive-width knees are `a=c-w/2`, `b=c+w/2`. Centre sigma .04–.4, surround/centre2–8, k0–1.2 and probe−3–13 are declared relative coordinates. For `J_sigma(z)=z Phi(z/sigma)+sigma phi(z/sigma)`, exact blurred ramp is
`F_sigma=.25+.55[J_sigma(x-a)-J_sigma(x-b)]/w`.
At w=0, exact step convolution is `.25+.55 Phi((x-c)/sigma)`; the input plot retains the complete vertical step and the midpoint input convention is .525. The filter response is `F_c-kF_s`. No edge replication or finite integration cutoff enters production. The separate encoded-gray strip depicts only the input and makes no calibrated display/brightness claim.

The Gaussian CDF uses the existing read-only runtime erfc; independent tests use direct Gaussian integration and do not call that CDF/hinge. Very short intervals (`w<1e-4*sigma`) use positive eight-point Gauss–Legendre averaging of Phi to avoid cancellation, with error order `(w/sigma)^16`. Otherwise the analytic hinge difference is used. Symmetry avoids subtracting a large linear term inside the hinge. Tiny endpoint roundoff is constrained to the theoretical[.25,.8] range, and both branches are compared with independent direct integrals. Overall response lies within[−.71,.8], so the initial conservative frame retained all offered signals and probe glyphs. The final response frame uses the tighter analytical envelope below.

The exact far-field response limits are `(1-k)*.25` and `(1-k)*.8`, rather than two finite plateau measurements. For balanced k1, write `g=J_sc-J_ss`: g is even, negative and increases with positive distance. Thus the response is antisymmetric about c, negative to its left and positive to its right. Its derivative at either knee of a positive-width ramp is `.55/w [Q(w/ss)-Q(w/sc)]>0`, so the knees are not extrema; the dip occurs before the lower knee and bump after the upper. For k0 the Gaussian blur is monotone. The step limit retains strong signed edge lobes, which are explicitly distinguished from distinct perceived Mach bands. No exact global extrema are invented from a restricted sample search.

Knee derivatives use survival tails directly rather than subtracting two nearly equal half-probabilities. A genuine extreme underflow remains for width7/sc.04/ratio2: w/ss=87.5, so the positive analytical derivative is below double precision's representable range. The model/public readout identifies that condition; very small signals can also round to zero. This is not misreported as a mathematical zero derivative.
