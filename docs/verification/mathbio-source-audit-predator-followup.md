## Corrections and independent derivations

3.7 now states an existence of arbitrarily small orbit-changing perturbations, rather than asserting every perturbation destroys a centre. Its focus classification is restricted to the actual ε range, where ε²−4(1−ε)<0. “Hyperbolic” is the persistence condition for isolated cycles. Neither an unproved distant attractor nor finite-time extinction is asserted for the saturation example. This finite phase viewport explicitly permits trajectories to leave it.

3.8 distinguishes the selected I/III models and the different units of an encounter coefficient when physical densities are used. The II/III renewal simulation counts only completed search-plus-handling intervals in the fixed 120-unit window; I is accurately described as a separate capped-Poisson construction. Finite-window bias is declared. The risk function includes its exact N→0 limit, and the risk-axis maximum is analytic (a or √(a/h)/2), removing near-zero clipping. The selected intake axes also accommodate a zero random count with marker-radius room.

3.12 no longer equates a cubic normal form with the full system or promises a subcritical jump destination. At a=1, μ=b−2, u=x−1 and v=−(x+y−1−b), the critical field is u′=−v−2uv−u³−u²v, v′=u. In z=u+iv, g20=i, g11=0, g02=−i and g21=(−3+i)/4. Kuznetsov's formula gives Re(c1)=−3/8 in these coordinates. Thus the leading physical x half-range is √(4μ/3); the author's exercise uses a different eigenvector normalization and gives l1=−3/4 at a=1, consistent in sign and after coordinate scaling. The previous distant b=2.6 fitted coefficient was removed. The local asymptotic is drawn only through b=2.2; numerical half-ranges use b=2.05,…,3.20, transient 800 and observation 80. These dots are finite-time estimates, not theorem proofs. Two complete 300-time-unit RK4 trajectories now set their own full phase extents instead of terminating at the old viewport. The gold vertical parameter guide replaces a misleading zero-amplitude dot.
