## Inherent-ambiguity proof audit

The proposed language is the union of block words with nonnegative counts and i=j or j=k. The application of marked pumping is sound, with an important tree formulation to make explicit in the final prose.

Take an arbitrary candidate grammar and its marked-tree pumping constant p. Write K=p+p!. In a^p b^p c^K, mark the a positions. Preserving block order under every pump forces each nonempty pumping factor to be unary; a factor crossing two letter blocks would create a reversed boundary when repeated. At least one factor contains a marked a. Since all pumped words must satisfy one of the two equalities and the original b/c counts differ, the only possible pair is a^t and b^t with1≤t≤p, and no pumped c. Pumping1+p!/t copies gives the common diagonal word and retains such an a/b loop. Starting instead from a^K b^p c^p and marking c retains a b/c loop in another parse of the same diagonal.

The outer a/b loop subtree contains no c, not merely its two side factors: its nonempty second factor is b^t and therefore its entire yield ends before the c block. The b/c subtree similarly contains no a. If both obtained parse trees were identical, the two outer loop subtrees could not nest, because each has a positive symbol absent from the other. Ordered-tree subtree intervals would therefore be disjoint. Pumping both once more produces counts(K+t,K+t+u,K+u), which satisfy neither equality. Thus the parses are distinct in every candidate grammar. A finite natural-union-grammar widget illustrates this consequence without proving a statement about every grammar.

The coordinator source receipt starts the marked-tree proof with CNF. For this application, do not silently transfer ambiguity back through an arbitrary language-equivalent conversion. A direct arbitrary-tree version suffices: let b=max(2,maximum RHS length), let k be the number of variables and choose p=b^(k+1). Follow the child with the most marked descendants. Bottom marked branching points bound the outer marked yield; repeated variable labels among k+1 such points give a side context with at least one marked leaf, in that actual candidate grammar’s tree. The final5.6 draft now supplies this direct arbitrary-arity argument, followed by the factorial/disjoint-subtree contradiction. It was reread and independently accepted; no ambiguity-preservation assumption remains.
