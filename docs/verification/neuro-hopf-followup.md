## Material correction and independent derivation

The old ε=.08 caption described a stable cycle born at Hopf and a canard explosion, and equated observed firing frequency with the local Hopf frequency. Its local Hopf is **subcritical**. At either candidate voltage, set s=εb and ω²=ε−s². With v−v*=x and w−w*=sx+ωy, the nonlinear terms are f=−v*x²−x³/3 and g=−s f/ω. Substitution in the source Eq.(6.7) gives

`ell1 = -1/8 + s*v*²/(4*omega²) = (2*b-1-eps*b²)/(8*(1-eps*b²))`.

It is +.07230185497 for ε=.08 and −.01388888889 for ε=1. Both equilibrium crossings are transverse. The two exact Hopf currents are .33128133745/1.41871866255 and .73338236143/1.01661763857 respectively. At ε=.08,I=.34 the observed large oscillation has rate .0213712 while the local unstable branch tends to .0438483, a concrete counterexample to the old identification. No finite simulation certifies a global cycle branch or a canard explosion. The chapter summary now names Hopf/SNIC as two important mechanisms and does not claim they exhaust neuronal onset.

The revised theorem gives stable versus unstable branch sides and distinguishes local amplitude/frequency from a stable global firing attractor. The finite detector's actual criteria are visible. Failure returns **unresolved**, not zero frequency; actual last-quarter amplitude is retained even for a decaying/tiny run. Counts, the preceding-quarter amplitude and window length are displayed. Both real eigenvalues are shown separately when the discriminant is nonnegative; the old readout silently represented them as a repeated real part±0i. Views include the full computed eigenvalue curves, traces, rates and actual amplitudes. No equilibrium stability calculation is promoted to an exhaustive global cessation/onset result. Existing shared helpers and all dependency arrays remain unchanged.
