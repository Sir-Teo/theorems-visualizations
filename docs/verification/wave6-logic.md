The interface’s bounded input range is not a purported universe; results can exceed it. Von Neumann ordinals are displayed through their exact predecessor relation without attempting astronomical code allocation. Closure means the least transitive **superset**, distinguishing TC(A) from TC({A}).

Separation evaluates a few specified predicates. Replacement checks totality and uniqueness of the displayed relation, including the vacuous empty-domain case; a failed premise is not a nonexistence proof. Russell’s general Boolean contradiction does not use Foundation, while R_A=A is expressly limited to the HF examples.

Kuratowski pairs include equal coordinates; function graphs distinguish totality from uniqueness and injectivity. The Foundation display checks every nonempty subset of a four-vertex relation and separately identifies extensionality collisions. An arbitrary labelled relation is not automatically a model of ZF, and finite checks do not certify infinite well-foundedness. Graph arrows point from member to containing set.

Quotient representations use exact equality tests and embeddings, not literal inclusion of the raw constructions. Cut membership includes all negative rationals in the √2 example; exact comparisons determine membership, while the boundary coordinate is rounded. Finite samples are never called Dedekind cuts.

The historical axiom toggles track direct licenses for the displayed presentation, not independence. Finite choice and fixed finite singleton chains do not require their corresponding general axioms.
