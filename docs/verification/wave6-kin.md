## Models and limits

- A declared two-state Eyring model changes the common transition level without changing the state free-energy difference. Both rate constants scale together and retain detailed balance. Exact closed-system relaxation shares one equilibrium. Drawn paths are selected functions, not molecular calculations; real multistep catalysts need not have a common measured rate multiplier. Raw baseline exposure retains a positive clock even when physical seconds underflow.
- The Michaelis workshop holds substrate constant and removes product. Exact two-state startup and integrated product are computed before taking the stationary limit. This is an open chemostatted mechanism, not an unlimited closed batch. KM is generally not a dissociation constant, and saturation alone does not identify a mechanism. Finite plotted endpoints use the actual transient, not an invented exact stationary endpoint.
- Seven positive synthetic observations receive the same bounded additive noise before either fit. Direct raw-rate least squares and unweighted reciprocal regression minimize different objectives. The direct fit profiles amplitude over a compact transformed concentration, compares numerical stationary candidates and both limiting boundary models, and reports resolution diagnostics. It is not a certified global optimizer, confidence interval, or universal estimator ranking. Signed reciprocal regression parameters are retained and infeasible positive Michaelis parameters are explicitly undetermined.
- Catalytic efficiency factors a chosen Smoluchowski encounter coefficient, association probability and reaction-before-release probability. The conversion is 1000 NA from m³/s to L/mol/s. KM is reported in mol/L for these rate-constant units. No empirical enzyme ranking or universal diffusion ceiling is claimed; zero association has no finite saturation concentration.
- Competitive, uncompetitive, pure noncompetitive and general mixed inhibition derive from one conserved enzyme-population law. Pure noncompetitive means equal competitive/uncompetitive binding excesses. Zero substrate leaves relative rate loss undefined; raw logarithms distinguish tiny positive inhibition from zero. Hidden controls are inactive in the displayed classification, and native tests must exercise controls when their mode makes them visible.
- The Hill law is a declared response function. Its slope is not a molecular site count. The independent-identical-sites model separately computes expected number bound with per-site Hill slope one. Zero dose has no finite logarithmic point; tiny positive doses outside the drawn window are identified rather than placed at zero.
- Thermal activity combines chosen Arrhenius catalysis and rapid reversible two-state unfolding. A finite positive-temperature maximum is derived only when it exists; an enthalpy inequality alone is insufficient. No universal biological temperature optimum or irreversible deactivation model is inferred.
- Reaction-limited Langmuir–Hinshelwood kinetics uses competitive adsorption of A and B on one site pool. Its rate optimum varies A at fixed B pressure, not fixed total pressure. The local site-balance proof supplies the still-planned adsorption prerequisite without pretending that prerequisite body was read.
- Eley–Rideal has only A adsorbed while gaseous B strikes it. The compared LH branch lets B compete for sites. Chosen ER and LH coefficients have distinct units; their arbitrary amplitudes are not experimental evidence that one mechanism is faster. Raw pressure logarithms retain positive rates when pressure products underflow.
- Temperature-programmed desorption integrates a positive Boltzmann dose along a linear ramp. First-order loss and its implicit peak condition are source-backed; zero/second order are explicitly declared extensions. Adaptive quadrature reports convergence and an error estimate, not a rigorous roundoff enclosure. Zero-order exhaustion has an open left-limit signal and a separate post-exhaustion branch; an unresolved exhaustion boundary has no invented point value. Positive orders do not exhaust at finite time, even when linear inventory/rate underflow. Loaded inventory is restricted to0.05–1; a separate physical toggle selects empty inventory. Observation-window limits are explicit.
- The 1913 Michaelis–Menten Paper anchors a modern pre-equilibrium/stationary-binding comparison. The original German body and original invertase data were not read, fitted or reconstructed. At zero catalytic ratio binding remains meaningful while both product rates vanish; no0/0 rate normalization is assigned. The complete frozen historical bibliography and introduces edge are preserved, with no invented DOI or URL.
