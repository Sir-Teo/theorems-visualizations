## Mathematical model and proof

The source spans are deliberately replaced by independent uniforms **only in the authored comparison**. Their printed CI/HPD meanings are not identified with a uniform distribution; no posterior shapes, dependence structure, extinction-cause probability or inference from dating data is reconstructed.

Let A∈[a0,a1], E∈[e0,e1] be ages increasing into the past, wa=a1−a0, we=e1−e0. A>E means the blue dated presence precedes the red loss. For wa,we>0, conditional on a, the probability E<a is0 belowe0, (a−e0)/we inside[e0,e1], and1 abovee1. Integrating this piecewise linear CDF overA yields the triangular/rectangular area. In the middle segment [l,h]=[max(a0,e0),min(a1,e1)], its contribution before division bywa is (h−l)((h−e0)+(l−e0))/(2we), with zero contribution whenh≤l. The upper segment length is max(0,a1−max(a0,e1)).

The unconditioned positive moment M=E[(A−E)+] integrates (a−e0)^2/(2we) in the middle and a−(e0+e1)/2 abovee1. The middle antiderivative is factored into positive local differences to avoid subtracting large cubes. The conditional gap is M/P(A>E) only when that probability is positive, otherwise null and explicitly displayed as undefined.

A point versus a span uses the corresponding one-dimensional integral. Two points have P(A>E),P(A<E),P(A=E) equal to the appropriate0/1 indicators. Continuous distributions have zero tie mass even when their endpoints touch. Red mass inside the **closed** blue span is overlap length/we for a nondegenerate red interval, and a closed-membership indicator for a red point. Thus an isolated blue point has zero red-continuous mass but includes an equal red point with probability1. The finite binary evaluation is disclosed; “analytic” refers to the integral, not formal interval arithmetic.
