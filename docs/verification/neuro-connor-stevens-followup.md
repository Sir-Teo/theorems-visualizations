## Corrections

The old entry said adding A-current guarantees type I/SNIC and removing it restores type II, and described the blue finite curve as beginning at exactly zero frequency with unbounded latency. Those claims do not follow from a finite step-response sweep or all offered conductances. They are replaced with the dependence on kinetics, input, initialization and conductance. Unsupported “first quantitative prediction after Hodgkin–Huxley” priority is removed. No exact minimum frequency or bifurcation classification is inferred from the grid.

Changing gA also changes EL substantially, from about−17 to−67.86490mV. The comparison explicitly identifies this **joint A/leak change**, not a pure channel-block manipulation. The common rest is solved as an actual scalar equilibrium and each leak is derived from it, replacing an incompletely relaxed initial state for some variants. Rest V=−67.97472585536181 agrees with an independent root solve. Source rate factor3.8, sodium/potassium shifts and doubled n time constant are retained. The empirical A-activation target slightly exceeds1 at high spike voltages (e.g.1.01326 at60mV); it remains **unclipped** and is not claimed to be a literal probability everywhere.

The selected400ms trace and620ms grid protocols are distinguished. All threshold events are now interpolated at integration resolution, independent of the plotting stride. Every.1ms is plotted instead of the old.4ms, which could miss narrow spike peaks. Finite rates require at least three events after120ms, rather than converting a missing interval to zero or calling a transient interval a steady firing rate. First-spike latencies beyond the observation are unresolved, not proof of no eventual spike. Only positive resolved latency points reach the log panel. Views include all computed rates/latencies and both actual voltage traces. The purple observable is conductance gA*a³*b rather than ionic current.
