# Epidemiology: source review, 18 October 2026

Four source checks were open. This review closes epi-4.4, epi-4.5 and epi-4.12 after reading the relevant original evidence, and preserves epi-3.13 because the complete Cornfield 1951 paper remains inaccessible. Historical data are distinguished from the illustrative models.

## Evidence read

- [Snow (1855), On the Mode of Communication of Cholera, complete text and scanned tables](https://www.academia.dk/MedHist/Biblioteket/Print/snow_1855.html): complete relevant Broad Street, Hampstead, map explanation and attack-date sections, and the complete relevant intermingled-company comparison, inquiry methods and first-four/first-seven-week results. Visually inspected Tables I and IX. Table I has 571 dated fatal attacks plus 45 of unknown onset date; Table IX gives 40,046 houses/1,263 deaths and 26,107 houses/98 deaths. The ratio recomputed from counts is about 8.402; 315/37 uses rounded table rates and gives a different value.
- [HistData dataset documentation](https://rdrr.io/rforge/HistData/man/Snow.html) and [Peter Li's complete source vignette on duplicate and missing records](https://ftp.gwdg.de/pub/misc/cran/web/packages/HistData/vignettes/Snow_deaths-duplicates.html): Dodson/NCGIA digitisation, distributed by Tobler and HistData, has 578 records but only 575 distinct coordinates, with three duplicate pairs. The proposed replacements are interpolations; the visualization preserves the named original dataset and discloses its limitation. Stacked symbols do not supply exact addresses or a population denominator.
- [Semmelweis (1861), original German text in TextGrid/DTA](https://www.textgridrep.de/browse/4bn26.0): complete relevant admission/transfer discussion, original pp. 2–4, and Kolletschka, chlorine, mortality and patient-to-patient transmission discussion, pp. 53–60. [Digitized German original-text reprint in Gesammelte Werke](https://mek.oszk.hu/12600/12641/pdf/12641_2.pdf), pp. 99–101 and 130–135: visually checked the six-year comparison and post-intervention tables. The downloaded 332-page PDF is the collected-works reprint, not a 332-page scan of the 1861 first edition. It gives 1,989/20,042 and 691/17,791; the printed second-clinic summary percentage is arithmetically wrong, so rates are recomputed. Chlorine washing began approximately mid-May; liquid chlorine preceded cheaper chlorinated lime. Admission turns were unequal and transfers biased recorded mortality, so this was not random allocation.
- [Doll–Hill (1950), complete ten-page original](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC2038856?pdf=render): complete relevant investigation/sampling and smoker-definition sections, pp. 740–742, and Table IV, visually inspected. Male non-smokers numbered 2/649 cases and 27/649 controls; the displayed smoking odds ratio is 647×27/(2×622), not an odds ratio of non-smoking.
- [Doll–Hill (1956), complete eleven-page original](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC2035864?pdf=render): complete relevant cohort definitions, follow-up, age-standardisation and amount-smoked sections, pp. 1071–1074; Table V and its footnotes visually inspected. Rates 0.07/0.47/0.86/1.66 are per 1,000 men aged at least 35 per year, standardised to the 1951 UK male population, over November 1951–March 1956. Amount is grams of tobacco, with one cigarette equivalent to 1 g; past smokers use their most recent amount. These are not exclusive current-cigarette categories.
- [Doll et al. (2004), complete nine-page original](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC437139?pdf=render): complete relevant participant definitions, survival-curve methods and cessation results, including Figs. 3–4 and Table 5. The study's 34,439 men and gains of approximately 3/6/9/10 years at cessation around 60/50/40/30 agree with the item. The original explicitly discusses reverse causality and interpolation around cessation at age 60; the visualization's between-age interpolation is illustrative.
- [Cornfield et al. (1959), complete seventeen-page original-paper reprint](https://statlit.org/CP/Cornfield/2009-Int-J-Epidemiol-Reprint-1959-Cornfield.pdf), doi:10.1093/ije/dyp289: complete relevant constitutional-hypothesis discussion and Appendix A. The binary-confounder proof assumes no direct exposure effect and yields a rate-ratio bound; its use for a case–control odds ratio additionally requires a rare-outcome approximation. This is a different paper from the still-unread 1951 original.

Temporary originals and readable text are in `/tmp/epi-primary`. BMJ and PMC direct requests returned access/challenge pages; Europe PMC supplied the complete original PDFs. Cornfield 1951's publisher route exposes a bibliographic record/first-page preview, not the complete seven-page article. That flag remains open.

## Corrections

| Item | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| epi-3.13 | Retained. Fixed the sampled table: rows now truly represent exposed/unexposed and columns cases/controls, with 100 in each column. The old transposition contradicted the labels. Bayes reconstruction is independently checked. Replaced the universal “baseline risk <5%” shaded region with the exact baseline threshold for OR within 10% of the chosen RR; rare disease must hold in both exposure groups. Expanded the logarithmic graph to include the interior OR maximum at the risk cap, not just its right endpoint, and reports zero-cell sample ratios as undefined or unbounded. and disclosed the 98% exposed-risk cap. The original paper's exact derivation, applications, tables and priority claims still require a complete reading. |
| epi-4.4 | Closed. Corrected the map/intervention chronology and the interpretation of deaths per unit area: a spatial concentration is not a dose–response result without population or consumption denominators. Original interviews, exceptions, dates and water-company counts support the account. Disclosed the mapped dataset's three duplicate coordinate pairs and the difference between mapped records and the 616 fatal attacks in the chronological table. |
| epi-4.5 | Closed. Corrected unequal admission turns, transfer-related recording bias, liquid chlorine versus later chlorinated lime, and the unsupported global “first intervention” priority claim. Fixed the model's hidden recalibration: per-examination transmission risk is calibrated once at the defaults and remains fixed when the controls change, so examination count and contaminated share now affect predicted mortality. The graph expands for high-risk settings instead of clipping at 16%. The model is not an estimate of historical examination risk or intervention efficacy. |
| epi-4.12 | Closed. Corrected tobacco-dose units and follow-up dates; explicitly distinguishes rate ratios, the case–control odds ratio and the assumption used to compare it to Cornfield's bound. Corrected the numeric prevalence implication (about 4.22%, rather than under 4%). The graph now includes the confounder bound at 1% prevalence and the complete case–control interval. Cessation readouts identify the interpolation. |

The adjacent unflagged paper summaries also contained directly contradicted wording. Corrected Snow’s map from “every death” to ascertained addresses, the workhouse from “spared” to its reported five deaths, and removed the unsupported claim that a single later sewer project ended London cholera. Corrected Semmelweis’s June–December rate from about 2% to 56/1,841 (3.04%), the admission-turn account, “published only in 1861” to the book’s publication date, and the change-point caption from “true break” to the optimum of the descriptive two-rate model. No additional source closure is claimed for those unflagged summaries.
