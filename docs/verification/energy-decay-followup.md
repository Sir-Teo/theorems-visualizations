Re-derived the heat integral analytically using the 0.8-power antiderivative. Stable `expm1/log1p` differences avoid unnecessary cancellation. Calculated 1-year-operation heat is 1.5747% at 1 hour and 0.6616% at 1 day; these are this fit's predictions, not universal measurements.

Corrected the water energy balance. The former curve consumed mass linearly with the fraction of `m(cp ΔT+hfg)` supplied, so it evaporated water while still warming it. The revised curve keeps the full inventory until `m cp×60 K` has been admitted, then reduces mass only by excess heat divided by latent heat. Constant `cp=4186 J/(kg K)` is a declared approximation. The existing IAPWS saturation reference gives `hfg=2256.47 kJ/kg` at 100°C. No exact 1-atmosphere pressure is falsely implied by that 100°C reference.

Warmup and exhaustion times are found from the integrated energy. Exhaustion ends the curve at its actual time; otherwise the model explicitly stops after 100 h and reports remaining water. It no longer labels an iteration/time guard as “gone,” nor truncates its plotted data at 100 h while expanding the axis beyond that limit. For example, 100 MW, 10 days operation, cooling lost 100 days after shutdown, and 600 t water admit only about 2.127 GJ in the next 100 h: the inventory has not reached boiling and all 600 t remain. The sensible requirement is about 150.7 GJ.
