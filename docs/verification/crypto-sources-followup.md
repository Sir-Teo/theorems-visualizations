## Primary sources actually inspected

- **Goldwasser–Micali, “Probabilistic Encryption,” JCSS 28(2), 270–299 (1984), DOI 10.1016/0022-0000(84)90070-9.** [Author's full original scan](https://people.csail.mit.edu/silvio/Selected%20Scientific%20Papers/Encryption/Probabilistic_Encryption.pdf): 30 pages; printed page = PDF page +269. OCR was generated for all pages, checked against rendered pp.281 and283. Read §4.4, pp.281–282 (key generation, bit encryption/decryption); §5.1/Theorem5.1, pp.282–286 (polynomial security and adjacent-message reduction); §5.2/Theorem5.2, pp.286–290 (original semantic notion and implication); §§6.2–6.3, pp.292–298 (QRA, average-case reduction and revealing a random pseudosquare). The bibliography confirms the STOC1982 predecessor. The paper has Figures1–2 on p.283. Its Theorem5.2 states polynomial security **implies** semantic security; the former claim that this theorem proves the modern private-key simulation equivalence was removed. A separate modern formulation with explicit joint auxiliary sampling and both reductions is supplied in3.4.
- **Boneh–Shoup, *A Graduate Course in Applied Cryptography*, v0.6, January2023.** [Authors' full PDF](https://crypto.stanford.edu/~dabo/cryptobook/BonehShoup_0_6.pdf), linked by [author landing page](https://toc.cryptobook.us/): 1130 pages; printed = PDF −16. Read §2.2 for the modern IND games; Theorem3.2 proof pp.57–59, especially uniformly random challenge position and signed telescoping; §4.4.3 Theorems4.4–4.7, pp.134–137, full lazy coupling/difference-lemma proof; §5.4.2 Theorem5.3 proof pp.191–193, §5.4.3 Theorem5.4 proof pp.195–198, and §5.5.2/Theorem5.6 pp.203–204 for random-IV and nonce-based modes, including the counter-overlap counterexample. The AES state-size clarification also agrees with §4.3's 16-byte state description. These are the authors' actual constructions/proofs, rather than an uninspected Katz–Lindell pointer.
- **Mantin–Shamir, “A Practical Attack on Broadcast RC4,” FSE2001, LNCS2355 (proceedings2002), pp.152–164.** Full original article read within the [359-page proceedings scan](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ljupco-Kocarev/publication/220942382_Cryptanalysis_of_SBLH/links/0deec53a9c28f923e9000000/Cryptanalysis-of-SBLH.pdf), despite its misleading SBLH filename. Article PDF pages160–172 correspond to printed152–164. Specifically §2.1/Fig.1 p.154, §3.2/Theorem1/Fig.2 pp.156–157 and §3.3/Theorem2 p.158. The theorem assumes a uniform **initial** permutation; its forcing event is before the first PRGA round, not at the start of round2. The `2/N` approximation uses the approximately uniform complement. The paper's 200-stream experiment reports success exceeding0.64, rather than high-confidence identification. Full cached original text was accessible via the web reader; direct curl of the proceedings and the RFC-linked UConn copy returned403/timeout, so **no local PDF hash is claimed** for this source.
- **RFC6229 §2, “Test Vectors for the Stream Cipher RC4” (May2011).** [Official full text](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6229.txt), all14 keys and all18 16-byte stream slices per key transcribed programmatically into the dedicated vector JSON. The source reports comparison of three implementations. **RFC8439 §§2.1–2.4**, [official full text](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8439.txt): complete quarter-round and block vectors, counter/nonce layout, feed-forward and little-endian serialization; nonce/counter restrictions. Full round specification was checked against the existing shared implementation.
- **Heys, “A Tutorial on Linear and Differential Cryptanalysis.”** [Full author's tutorial, academic mirror](https://cs.bc.edu/straubin/crypto2017/heys.pdf), 33 pages. Read §§2.1–2.4 pp.3–5, including Tables1–2 and final whitening; §§3.2–3.3 pp.7–10 for the independent-variable piling-up argument and S-box biases; §3.6 pp.17–18 for dependence/linear-hull caveats; §§4.1–4.5 pp.19–29 for DDTs, characteristics, scoring and data caveats. Rendered Table1 and the bit-permutation table inspected. This is a primary tutorial construction; the atlas's two-layer experiment is explicitly an original simplified model, not its four-round attack or its empirical data.
- **Bogdanov et al., “PRESENT: An Ultra-Lightweight Block Cipher,” CHES2007, 450–466.** §3 S-box table was read in the complete [author-uploaded paper text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221291660_PRESENT_An_Ultra-Lightweight_Block_Cipher), identified as Gregor Leander's uploaded original. The16 entries exactly match the shared B S-box. [Official IACR original URL](https://www.iacr.org/archive/ches2007/47270450/47270450.pdf) returned403, as did direct PDF download; **no original-PDF hash is claimed**. No hardware/security claim from the paper is being reproduced: the only imported component is this table.
- **FIPS46-3 (25October1999)** [official PDF](https://csrc.nist.gov/files/pubs/fips/46-3/final/docs/fips46-3.pdf), 27 pages, printed = PDF −5. Actual DES specification pp.8–19: IP/inverse, Feistel updates, E/P/S tables, key selection/shifts and reversed-key decryption. The [official publication page](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/46-3/final) records withdrawal on19May2005. **EFF's July1998 [original project FAQ](https://w2.eff.org/Privacy/Crypto/Crypto_misc/DESCracker/HTML/19980716_eff_des_faq.html)** independently supplies the Blaze known-answer example, July13–15 search, 56-hour result,24.8% space, average88,803,604,509 keys/s and budget below$250,000. The search-rate calculator now distinguishes the mean work model from the realized run.
- **NIST SP800-38A (2001)** [official full PDF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-38a.pdf), 66 pages, printed = PDF −7. §§6.2 pp.10–11 and6.5 pp.15–16: CBC/CTR operations; AppendixB pp.18–19: unique counters across all messages, incrementing and nonoverlap; AppendixC p.20: IV unpredictability before plaintext choice; AppendixD p.21: integrity consequences. Security reductions are sourced separately to Boneh–Shoup; the NIST specification is not represented as a proof of the toy cipher's security.

## Limits preserved

Number-theoretic hardness, general pseudorandomness and production cipher security are assumptions or source theorems, never consequences of these samples. Reproducible sample RNGs and tiny block/modulus sizes are explicitly teaching-only. The original Biham–Shamir/Matsui cost claims, secret DES-design narrative and precise term-priority claims were removed rather than treated as verified from later citations. Two originals were available as complete primary text in the web reader but not as downloadable bytes; that distinction and the absence of their local hashes are recorded above. No original-paper queue item is being silently accepted from a secondary summary.
