For the capacity proof, unequal above-cost prices cannot be an equilibrium: a strictly capacity-bound low-price seller can raise its price slightly, while otherwise the higher-price seller has zero sales and can profit by undercutting. At a common price above the capacity-clearing candidate an undercut increases sales; below it, a small price increase retains capacity sales. Thus p0=max(c,a−2k) is the only candidate. Below p0 there is no profitable deviation; above it the concave residual-profit quadratic has vertex (a+c−k)/2, subject to the branch interval. Comparing with tie profit yields k≤(a−c)/3 or k≥a−c. This explicit linear result is not generalized to other rationing or demand rules.
