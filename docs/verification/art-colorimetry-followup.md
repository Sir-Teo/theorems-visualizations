## What the original sources support and what changed

**4.5.** Smith–Guild p.74 separates objective matching coordinates from physiological primaries; pp.75/77–78 define reference stimuli and hypothetical standard observer; pp.80–89 discuss linear specification, units and the coordinate transformation. The signed inverse-basis equation requires independent primaries and fixed matching conditions. Negative coefficients move to the test field, leaving two nonnegative mixtures with equal raw XYZ. This does not certify appearance across observers/adaptation.

The old bunched-primary equal-energy normalization could give a negative physical primary column. The two authored spectral systems now normalize each positive column by its own positive Y. The historical standard matrix is explicitly the rounded relative-unit matrix of p.88 Eq.2, not radiant watts or an exact interpolation of the current table at its reference wavelengths. The local widget uses the official table, displays all401 curves over380–780nm and both actual signed-side XYZ values/residuals, and labels display patches as proxies. The source's1:4.5907:0.0601 luminosity ratio is stated to rounded precision; M's relative units are not silently rescaled.

**4.14.** The original has more qualifications than an unqualified modern linearity slogan: three impression descriptors and continuity pp.69–72, complement argument pp.72–78, separate substitution premise p.78, geometric sum pp.79–81, and a fourth intensity assumption explicitly treated as less firmly grounded pp.82–83. The final p.84 note itself calls for revising the hue placement. Obsolete homogeneous-Purpur language is not adopted as modern spectral physics.

The widget proves the bounded modern construction B=max(A)−A in nonnegative linear sRGB powers: A+B is scaled D65 white. This is not a universal theorem about spectral complements. It compares actual mixture XYZ against an equal-Y neutral and proves that adding common C preserves the raw difference. The former clamp/round of computed B and arbitrary chromaticity match threshold are removed. Nine continuous controls, explicit black/undefined chromaticity and a common patch scale preserve this declared model.

**4.16.** Source p.74 distinguishes color specification/physiology, p.75 states hypothetical standard observer, pp.77–78 define XYZ cardinal stimuli, pp.87–89 derive imaginary unitary stimuli and luminosity-isolated Y, and pp.92–93 give transformations/units. This paper describes a standard, not physical emission of imaginary primaries. The current471-row data are distinguished from its rounded original tables.

The former implicit historical transformation is replaced by the explicitly authored P(t)=(1−t)M+tI. Every row of M sums to1, preserving the equal-energy reference. Its determinant is positive for all real t∈[0,1]: the four Bernstein-style expansion coefficients det(M), sum of principal2-minors, trace(M),1 are all positive. All471 actual samples are transformed, every signed extremum remains visible, and XYZ at t=1 returns the original rows exactly. No intermediate basis is called historical or physically emitted. Rowwise nonnegativity also covers the declared linearly interpolated locus; there is no empirical sign tolerance.
