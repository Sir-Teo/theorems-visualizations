## Definite inherited defects and replacement

The inherited default symmetry button moved one ideal to(-3.3,-0.9), outside its[-3.2,3.2] view. It equated a sampled-direction/numerically optimized yolk with the true yolk, applied a continuum4r bound to finite-grid uncovered points with an unspecified resolution allowance, and promised collapse to a single equilibrium even when that point was absent from its fixed grid. The actual grid admits pairwise ties, while the inherited sophisticated-agenda helper resolved a missing edge in favor of the challenger and the text presented a tournament theorem. Those agenda conclusions are removed.

The new widget has three ideals, bounded by[-2,2]² and snapped to eighth units. For a nondegenerate triangle, its exact mathematical yolk is the incircle; for collinear/coincident ideals it is the zero-radius disk at the median ideal. The authored proof is included: side-length-weighted signed distances force maximum side distance at least2Δ/perimeter; the incenter achieves it, and every other median line through a vertex lies between the two side directions, at distance no greater than the inradius. Collinear median lines all pass through the median ideal. Coordinates, orientation signs and all vote comparisons are small exact dyadic/integer calculations; square roots/incenter coordinates are floating point and separately checked.

A different panel builds a9×9 or17×17 grid over[-3,3]². The explicitly declared finite relation requires both a direct strict-majority win A>B and inclusion of B's outgoing wins in A's. It retains tied votes, shows uncovered points for exactly that graph, and exposes every missing-win witness for selected A/B. This is an authored extension of the tournament definition, not an assertion that finite ties meet Miller's tournament hypotheses or McKelvey's continuous-covering hypotheses. The continuous4r reference appears only on the true-yolk panel; no bound is imposed on finite uncovered points. The off-grid symmetry preset has a zero-radius full-plane equilibrium missing from both offered grids; an uncovered finite policy can therefore lie outside the continuous reference even if it is the unique strict finite-grid winner. On-grid and coincident-majority presets provide the complementary collapse cases.

Ideals and selected A/B are actual draggable handles. A/B snap only to actual graph policies. The finer grid is the initial state so every coarse saved handle is also valid before the grid control restores; fine-grid selections replay exactly. A native check initially exposed view autoscaling during an ideal drag, which moved the target's data coordinates under the pointer. The left viewport now stays fixed during a drag and updates after release. The failed initial smoke is retained as `polsci-yolk-native-smoke.log`; the corrected narrow smoke is `polsci-yolk-native-smoke2.log`.
