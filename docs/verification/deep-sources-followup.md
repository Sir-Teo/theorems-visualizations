## Corrections and independent reasoning

**4.5.** Updates consistently start at t=1, using gradients at θ(t−1); zero-step Adam bias denominators disappear. AdaGrad's nonincreasing *normalizer multiplier* is distinguished from its actual step. Bias corrections normalize exponential weights and give unbiased stationary moments; they do not exactly debias arbitrary changing gradients. RMSProp credit is corrected, and an unverifiable absolute claim about never being published is removed. The displayed Adam quantity was |Δ/g|, which can be undefined or enormous when current g is zero but momentum remains. It now shows the actual diagonal denominator multiplier η/(√s+ε), applied to the appropriate gradient or momentum. Loss and multiplier axes include every displayed finite value, with the loss floor 10⁻¹⁶ disclosed. The path window is explicitly clipped. Tested closed forms are AdaGrad's η/√t and RMSProp's η/√(1−0.9ᵗ) for a constant gradient, independent geometric sums for changing Adam moments, and the exact quadratic GD powers.

**4.8.** Added the source's missing d_y≤d_x condition and exact data/eigenvalue hypotheses. The old convex-in-product argument ignored hidden-layer bottlenecks. A valid example X=I₂, Y=diag(3,2), hidden width 1 has best product diag(3,0): both factor gradients vanish, while the unrestricted product gradient diag(0,−2) does not. The corrected outline follows the source's ordinary-LS/rank-p/perturbation cases. Scalar differentiation is independent of the matrix proof: g_i=(P−c)∏j≠i w_j, H_ii=(∏j≠i w_j)², H_ij=(∏k≠i w_k)(∏k≠j w_k)+(P−c)∏k≠i,j w_k. At the two-weight origin Hessian eigenvalues are ±c. For D≥3 **all matrices zero** makes all first/second derivatives vanish, while suitable products lower/raise the loss nearby.

**Primary-proof edge correction.** Kawaguchi Appendix C.1 leaves the final matrix arbitrary after setting the hidden matrices to zero and then asserts zero Hessian throughout that set. At D=3 this is too broad: scalar weights (0,0,z) have H₁₂=−cz. The atlas uses the sufficient **all-weight-zero** construction, which proves the corollary's existence conclusion without the overstatement. Analytic and finite-difference tests cover both cases.

The 30 GD runs now report final threshold counts separately from first crossings; neither a finite plateau nor reaching a small value proves convergence or the theorem. All 1200 steps remain on the horizontal scale. Large-loss/nonfinite stopping is identified as a numerical budget, not a mathematical divergence certificate. D=3 paths are explicitly two-coordinate projections; sampled paths always include their final point.

**5.8.** Time continuity and spatial Lipschitz alone do not justify the stated O(1/L) rate. Bounded second derivative along the relevant trajectories is now explicit. With h=1/L and K the global spatial Lipschitz constant, Taylor gives local defect ≤Mh²/2, then e_(j+1)≤(1+Kh)e_j+Mh²/2 and e_L≤M(eᴷ−1)/(2KL). The same inequality holds with Euclidean vector norms. For the actual tanh field, finite weights and bounded tanh/tanh′ give explicit bounds F on ‖f‖, K on ‖D_x f‖, and T on ‖∂_t f‖; therefore ‖x″‖≤KF+T uniformly. Independent sampled finite differences check these bounds.

Continuity alone can give a much slower rate: the uniformly convergent series f(t)=Σ(j≥1) cos(2π·2ʲt)/j² is continuous and has integral zero. On L=2ᵐ Euler's time grid, its left Riemann sum is Σ(j≥m)1/j²≥1/(4m), which is not O(1/L). Tests verify the frequency-alias identity on finite partial sums; the infinite statement follows from uniform absolute convergence. The ODE flow has a continuous inverse by solving the reversed-time globally Lipschitz equation; Gronwall gives continuous dependence both ways. A finite Euler step can collapse all inputs (f=−x,h=1), so flow invertibility is no longer assigned to every finite ResNet. The sufficient block-injectivity condition hK<1 is shown with its actual computed bound.

The former “exact flow (RK4)” labels are replaced by an 800-step numerical reference. Both grey paths and endpoint rings follow the toggle; all displayed trajectories/errors fit their axes. The 400→800 endpoint difference is a disclosed diagnostic, not a certified bound. Independent DOP853 calculations for every point at amplitudes 0.5, 1.1 and 3 agree with 800-step RK4 within **5.99×10⁻¹⁴, 1.54×10⁻¹² and 2.39×10⁻¹⁰**, respectively. The observed 64→128 Euler orders are **0.9977, 0.9973 and 1.0039**. These measurements illustrate the proven assumptions; they do not prove convergence by themselves. The depth control is an integer slider so all values 1–128 are directly reachable.

**5.10.** The toy uses ε=0 with positive data covariance, not a hidden positive ε while claiming exact invariance. The singular zero vector is excluded and marked; the plot includes origin/ray context and all finite trajectory data. Actual logistic loss is no longer clipped below 0.05, and effective rates/norms fit their axes. Heat-colour saturation ranges are disclosed. Every batch's own scale-invariant loss remains orthogonal even as minibatches or gain parameters change; those alone do not create cross terms. Positive fixed ε and frozen inference statistics generally break exact scaling. The corrected decay recurrence R₊=(1−ηλ)²R+η²‖g‖² is displayed for BN both with and without decay. The equilibrium expression has denominator 2λ−ηλ² and is conditional on an approximately stationary angular-gradient magnitude, not a guaranteed steady state. Both exact normalized direction updates and the arctan turn-angle formula are independently checked.
