## Corrections

A slow outward current and a small parameter do not alone guarantee bursting, monotonic spike count per burst or a particular bifurcation pair. The finite frozen runs no longer certify a homoclinic termination or divergent period. Calcium activation is nonzero at rest; z changes with the actual balance−μICa−z, not an assertion that no calcium enters between spikes. The slow variable is scaled and calcium-like, without empirical concentration units.

Full events are now detected at integration resolution and interpolated; the former2ms plot sampling could miss brief positive crossings and rounded every event. The explicit250ms-gap grouping rule is distinguished from a dynamical classification. Single groups can be tonic activity, single spikes are retained, boundary groups are excluded from the interior mean, and a missing mean is unavailable. The fixed12000ms warm-up and8000ms observation are stated without claiming a settled attractor.

The z and voltage views include all recorded full states and all computed frozen data. At I=60 the z range is near3, far beyond the old.4–1.1/.3–1.2 ranges; this tonic regime is now visible. Each diagram spans49 frozen values over the actual selected range. Gaps in observed spiking bounds remain gaps, and missing finite launch results do not rule out another attractor or a slower cycle. Frozen equilibria are solved by the previously audited monotonic-current-branch method: algebraically the fixed-z potassium term is an extra leak conductance to EK, avoiding the old coarse root scan. Stable equilibria, saddles and unstable equilibria have distinct markers. The sampled-cycle criterion, initial condition, observation interval and threshold are stated; these are observed finite spiking extrema rather than proven stable-cycle branches. Real animation markers follow the actual recorded time.
