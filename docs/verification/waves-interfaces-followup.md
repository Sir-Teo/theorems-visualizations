## Corrections and derivations

| 4.5–4.6 | The radius/contact construction gives reflection and Snell without inserting those laws. The TIR explanation was reversed: the transmitted wavelet becomes **larger** than the distance to the moving contact point. Its displayed radius ratio now divides by the incident radius, not by contact distance. Zero incidence has its physically simultaneous-contact picture; critical incidence uses the grazing limit. Views include the emitted wavelets. Stationary, isotropic phase-normal assumptions are explicit, without the false blanket exception for birefringent wavevector phase matching. |
| 4.7 | The propagating power expression is distinguished from the complex TIR branch. Complex reflection amplitudes are displayed in full. Equal indices give no interface, including the limiting grazing sample, avoiding the legacy helper’s spurious zero transmittance. Zero reflected power no longer draws a solid reflected ray. |
| 4.9 | Mean power is explicitly normal flux into a semi-infinite lossless second medium. Presets and the critical-angle button retain the computed angle rather than rounding across the threshold. Reversed index choices explicitly report no critical angle. |
| 4.10 | A single evanescent field has no normal mean flux; the finite gap contains both exponentials and carries power. Decay length diverges near critical incidence, contradicting the old universal subwavelength claim. Specializing the source TE transfer matrix gives `t = [cosh(κd) − i(kz²−κ²)sinh(κd)/(2kzκ)]⁻¹`; its squared modulus is the stated T. At κ=0 the solution is linear and `T=[1+(kz d/2)²]⁻¹`. Stable field evaluation avoids singular exponential coefficients there. The power axis includes the actual attenuation (below10⁻¹⁹ at a tested corner); no marker is clamped to10⁻⁴. The large-gap asymptote is drawn only for κd≥2, and the incidence control stops short of grazing. |
| 4.11 | The real Stokes reversal statement requires two propagating rays at the bare lossless dielectric boundary. Removed its unsupported extension of the same real-power equations to evanescent reversed incidence; general complex reversal requires conjugation. Both polarization controls and TIR rejection remain explicit. |
| 4.12 | Corrected primary **outer** versus secondary **inner** geometric edges; finite ray tracing is distinct from radiometry/interference. The prism trace now intersects actual finite face segments and identifies base exit/TIR instead of following an extended opposite-face plane through empty space. Missing symmetric minima and absent two-face branches are readable states. Signed refraction and dynamic/full deviation extents include low-apex minima. Drop rays terminate at the viewport boundary. |
| 4.13 | Invalid n2≥n1 is reported and produces no guided output; the selected index is never silently changed. Acceptance saturates at90° when NA>1. Guidance is strict, with critical equality used as a limiting bound. The internal, air-accessible and selected-cone delay bounds are distinguished. Gaussian output integrates equal incident energy per unit launch angle; rejected angles reduce area. Removed arbitrary peak normalization and stride sampling. Zero fibre length and zero cone are supported; input width is exact Gaussian FWHM. Material and waveguide dispersion are both retained as separate effects outside the ray model. |
| 4.14 | Pulse propagation now reaches/refects at the interface for every index and both directions within the animation cycle; previously glass-to-air at n=3 never arrived. The transmitted pulse becomes longer on leaving glass, contrary to the old blanket shortening caption. Field/phase extents and sign conventions include the whole displayed signal. |
| Adjacent4.8/4.15 | Equal-index reflected polarization is explicitly undefined because no reflected beam exists. Beer–Lambert zero concentration has infinite absorption depth and no defined relative absorbance error. Exact stray-light ceiling is `log10((1+s)/s)`, consistent with its stated normalized detector model. The calibration axis now includes A=15 at the maximum path. No new queue reductions are claimed. |

For the fibre integral, if the incident pulse is g and launch half-angle is C, output is `C⁻¹∫₀^min(C,accept) g(t−delay(θ))dθ`. Its time integral is the input time integral multiplied by the accepted angle fraction; the plot preserves that scale. Numerical angle quadrature restricts evaluation to the nonnegligible Gaussian neighborhood. Independent references instead integrate the delay density after `delay=u²`, removing its axial square-root singularity.
