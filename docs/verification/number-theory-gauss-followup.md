## Corrections and proof review

**4.11 — definition, cancellation and geometry.** The two prime-sum expressions agree by counting square roots of each residue and using the sum of all pth roots of unity. The prime-character facts and the fixed positive sign are supported by the cited sources; numerical examples alone do not determine the sign theorem.

The caption formerly said the p−1 all-one arrows close a p-gon although they end at −1. They are the first p−1 edges; the omitted unit vector closes the polygon. It also said only Legendre signs can give length √p. Negating every Legendre sign already gives another such sign pattern. This uniqueness claim is removed. For independent fair signs the exact expected squared endpoint length is p−1, so the reference scale is the root-mean-square √(p−1), not an asserted exact random length. Green/red arrows now mean positive/negative weights in every mode, with the residue interpretation explicitly limited to Legendre mode.

The term slider's maximum follows the selected p−1, its effective count is reported, and the walk layout fits every partial-sum vertex as well as the full endpoint and reference circle. The whole-sum vector remains gold; the final displayed term keeps its actual sign colour. This removes long stretches of a nominal 100-term control that formerly made no change for a small prime.

**4.12 — squared values and composite hypotheses.** The prime proof was checked term by term: substitute b=at in the double sum, evaluate the geometric inner sum separately at t=−1, and use the zero character sum. The character-sum square is ±p independently of the unresolved sign of g itself; this distinction remains explicit.

The composite extension now has its missing argument. For coprime odd a,b, CRT factors the Jacobi-weighted sum with the twisting factors (b/a)(a/b). Squaring removes their signs, so factoring a squarefree n into primes gives gₙ²=(−1/n)n. If ℓ² divides n, the Jacobi weights have period n/ℓ; the ℓ shifted terms with that common weight multiply a vanishing full sum of ℓth roots of unity. Thus gₙ=0 for every odd nonsquarefree n>1. This grouping proof does not assume the prime reciprocity theorem being illustrated next.

The old nonsquarefree readout falsely labelled zero as the prediction of (−1/n)n, although that expression is ±n for every odd n. It now states that the squarefree formula does not apply and that a separate periodic-cancellation argument predicts zero. Floating sums are displayed as numerical evaluations and checked against independent high-precision sums.

**4.13 — distinct primes, the quotient ring and cancellation.** The former collision adjustment `nearOddPrime(q+2)` could return the same prime again. In particular p=q=13, 19, 23 or 31 could leave characteristic q=p, where g²=0 and the asserted unit cancellation is invalid. A dedicated selector now explicitly excludes p while keeping q within its 3…61 range; a computation-level guard rejects equal or nonprime inputs. The caption explains the adjustment and the readout gives the actual p,q used.

The proof explicitly works in Fq[X]/Φp, which need not be a field. Frobenius is valid in this commutative characteristic-q ring. Because p≠q, the scalar p* is invertible and g²=p* makes g invertible as well. Cancelling yields congruent ±1 signs, and q odd turns that into equality. No irreducibility of Φp modulo q or unproved cancellation in a ring with zero divisors is assumed. The coefficient caption now says the **entire vector** matches one signed reference; at zero entries both outlines coincide. The implementation uses multiplication/reduction and binary exponentiation; the independent oracle instead uses BigInt polynomial long division and q successive multiplications.

The mathematical proof is a modern presentation. Its historical connection is supported by Gauss's actual *Summatio* arts. 32–33, rather than by an unverified ordinal/date attribution. The original passage's own reference to his first two book proofs supports the paper summary; this batch does not claim to have independently re-audited every proof in the original book's fourth and fifth sections.

**4.15 — original metadata, constructibility and the table.** The original title verifies the author, publisher, place and 1801 year. Removed the unsupported “written 1798 at age 21” credit, sweeping first-priority claims, discovery anecdotes and later-development claims. The summary now describes the documented content. In particular Gauss explicitly omits the proof of the necessity part of his constructibility characterization in art. 365; the old “full criterion” wording is removed. The display shows sufficiency via quadratic period splitting, not a proof of that omitted necessity claim or a historical ruler-and-compass construction diagram.

The former table used a single orange for both ordered mixed pairs (+,−) and (−,+). That made its rendered colours symmetric even where its caption asserted asymmetry. Four colours now distinguish the ordered pairs, so transposition interchanges the two mixed colours exactly when both primes are 3 modulo4. Diagonal cells are excluded, and the readout reports the actual ordered pair selected by a click.

The 17th-root groups are the exponent classes modulo 2ᴸ in the powers of3. Each child pair sums to its parent, and its product is invariant under the parent's multiplicative subgroup, hence is expressible in the parent-period algebra. The two children are therefore roots of a quadratic over the previous level. The first levels' periods are real because their classes contain conjugate pairs; at level 4 they are individual complex roots, now reported with both components. Filled/hollow markers distinguish the 16 singleton classes when the eight-colour palette repeats. Labels explicitly denote exponents; the black unity point is marked ζ⁰=1. The displayed radical is described in modern form, rather than as a literal facsimile of the original typography or signs.
