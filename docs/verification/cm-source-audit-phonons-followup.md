## Corrections and mathematical meaning

**3.11.** The classical rms approximation underestimates quantum displacement, contrary to the old caption. The original paper derives an Einstein frequency from a known melting point and an assumed collision amplitude; it does not contain this later Debye rms formula. The modern heuristic is now clearly distinguished from a universal melting theorem. Removed9 remembered material data sets/false universal bar range. Model inputs are dimensionlesseta and thresholdc. The actual zero-point rms is `sqrt(eta)/2`; ifc is below it, there is no positive crossing. The former fixed1–3000K bisection incorrectly returned a boundary as a melting prediction. New monotone solve brackets the actual crossing and returns an explicit no-crossing state. Debye integral uses a small-X Bernoulli antiderivative or exponentially convergent tail series, including the exact zero-temperature term. Plots show the full crossing.

**3.12.** Replaces a hidden `max(static,background+.05)` alteration of user input with explicit positive oscillator strength and the identity `static=background+strength`. Removes unsourced NaCl material/frequency claims. The lossless dielectric/polariton panels are separated from the damped reflectivity panel; the real-frequency zero/strict band is not claimed for nonzero damping. Lossless pole plotting is split, and reflectivity at its exact pole has the analytic limit1. Stable product-of-roots calculation avoids cancellation in the lower polariton branch. All LO roots/upper branches/light lines fit their dynamic views; only the explicitly disclosed fixed dielectric vertical window clips the divergent pole. Electronic background, vacuum permittivity, ionic polarization and tensor limitations are explicit. Atk=0 the lower root is zero, rather than two strictly positive roots. The nonpropagating interval excludes the upper endpoint, where the upper branch starts atk=0.

**3.14.** The old transformed energy integrand returned0 at its finite endpoint instead of the correct limit2(after covariance normalization), shifting energies. Dedicated quadrature uses the proper limit and stableexpm1. The right plot now evaluates the physical effective mass from Feynman's Eq.(45), not the trial total mass `(v/w)^2` incorrectly compared to a physical weak series. Both quantities are separately labeled in the readout. The dashed energy approximation is this trial method's Eq.(36), not an unsupported higher-order exact perturbation coefficient. The physical mass comparison uses only leading1+alpha/6. Removed guessed material-coupling markers and sharpalpha6 self-trapping claims. Gold dots/readouts evaluate the actual.05-step coupling, rather than silently rounding it to.25while plotting at a differentx. Enlarged views include fullalpha12energy/mass values. Finite quadrature/local search do not certify a global optimum or a rigorous floating-point bound; the theoretical variational inequality is distinguished from its numerical approximation.

**3.16.** Removes unreviewed historical priority and secondary generic yield-stress data. The third bar is actual current rigid-slip stress magnitude, with zero explicitly labeled instead of a fake log-axis floor. The Peierls curve now states the exact width convention and cited variant; alternative sampling/core models are not treated as interchangeable. Wide-core exponential stresses down to4e−11Gremain visible instead of being clamped at2e−6G. Geometry is explicitly a sampled continuum schematic with nearest-partner cut connections, not an atomistic relaxed extra plane or a bond-force simulation. Width changes the stress estimate only. Core is constrained before computing the displacement so the drawing and handle agree at input boundaries; far-field strains decay rather than a blanket few-percent assertion.
