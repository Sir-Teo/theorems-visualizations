## Mathematical and display corrections

**Exact finite certification.** The old chromatic-number helper could skip a timed-out k-color search and return a later coloring as if optimal. The old cycle finder also stopped after 400 cycles before filtering by length/parity, which could hide a longer or odd cycle behind shorter ones. This module now uses a dedicated subset dynamic program for every induced subgraph of its graphs (at most 14 vertices), with proper optimal coloring witnesses, and exhaustively enumerates induced cycle vertex sets without a shared cap. A candidate cycle set must have degree two at every vertex and be connected. The exact values do not depend on a search budget. The maximum input size is explicit and enforced by existing editors; the dedicated helper rejects larger inputs instead of returning a guessed result.

For χ(S), choose the least vertex v and minimize `1+χ(S−I)` over independent sets I containing v. Every color partition has exactly one such class, so this recurrence is exhaustive. Independent-set flags, clique numbers and independence numbers are independently computed by subset recurrences. The tests compare these values and witness colors with a separate sequential backtracking enumerator and explicit pairwise subset enumeration.

**Perfect/chordal definitions.** Replaced a weak hereditary illustration with a genuine counterexample to testing only the whole graph: C5 disjoint K3 has χ=ω=3 while its induced C5 is imperfect. Minimal imperfect graphs are described by induced-subgraph minimality rather than ambiguously as “the smallest.” Chordal elimination labels and their readout now consistently mean **higher-numbered/still-present neighbors** form a clique. The displayed reverse-MCS order's condition is directly checked; the text does not pretend the next perfect-graph proposition proves an unstated algorithm theorem. The strong-perfect editor caption now correctly states its ten-vertex limit rather than eleven.

**Strong and weak perfect graph theorems.** The easy direction is explicit: an odd hole has (ω,χ)=(2,3), and an odd antihole on 2k+1 vertices has (ω,χ)=(k,k+1). The difficult structural converse is cited and identified as unproved in this short display; random finite comparisons establish no universal result. The complement theorem's equivalent clique-cover statement now consistently refers to α(H) and clique covers of H, rather than switching the complement underneath those quantities. Complement selections are synchronized by vertex index.

**Replication.** The proof now explicitly inducts on graph order. A proper induced subgraph containing only the new twin is **isomorphic** to one in the old graph, rather than literally lying in it. A subgraph containing both twins uses the smaller induction hypothesis. Both ω-increase and ω-unchanged cases are retained; the caption no longer says ω and χ must increase in every replication. They remain equal for perfect starting graphs and can both stay constant. Original imperfect induced subgraphs persist, so replication cannot repair an imperfect start.

**Product characterization.** Replaced “the converse uses replication” with the actual **Gasparian rank proof presented by Diestel**. In a minimal imperfect graph all proper induced subgraphs are perfect; the independent-set/clique incidence matrices have product J−I of order αω+1, with nonzero eigenvalues αω and −1. Rank at most n contradicts n≤αω. The independent tests construct these matrices on minimal imperfect holes and antiholes. Bubble radius is now exactly proportional to the square root of multiplicity, matching the stated area encoding. The selected gold ring is drawn last and hollow, so a large aggregate bubble cannot paint over it.

**χ-boundedness and Mycielski.** The definition now supplies a nondecreasing bound for all clique numbers **at most r**, matching the author's explicit qualification. Jitter is disclosed as display-only; certified integer coordinates determine counts and comparisons. The filled Mycielski points at orders 2, 5 and 11 are now actually generated and exactly computed; the larger hollow points are explicitly theorem-derived. The original infinite construction, not small random samples, supplies unboundedness at ω=2. Scatter ranges expand to retain actual data and its stated jitter.

**Odd-hole-free bound.** The original paper proves `χ≤2^(2^(ω+2))` and constructs Gk with ω=3^k and `χ≥(7/2)^k`, using |Gk|=7^k and α(Gk)=2^k. The old picture plotted (3^k,(7/2)^k) as exact (ω,χ), which is already impossible at k=1 because 3.5 is not an integer. Hollow markers now explicitly encode the **lower bound**, while C7-complement is separately shown at its actual (3,4). The old upper-bound curve was almost entirely outside its plot. A second panel now plots log2 χ and the finite transformed bound `2^(ω+2)` over a matching visible range. The first panel retains logarithmic axes for the samples and lower-bound construction. No infinite or overflowing expression is fed to the plot.

**Induced paths and conjecture.** The proof recurrence is explicitly translated from path edge length to the atlas's vertex count: `g(c,k vertices)=(k−2)(c+1)+1`, then `f_k(1)=1`, `f_k(r)=(k−2)(f_k(r−1)+1)+1`. The verified bound is that recurrence; unverified historical sharper-bound enrichment was removed. The recursive proof's color IDs are compressed to actual classes without merging classes, with distinct display colors beyond the old eight-color cycle and accurate counts of colors used. The nearby unflagged local lemma receives the same distinct-palette correction. The general Gyárfás–Sumner statement remains a conjecture; the forest/tree equivalence and the high-girth counterexample for a cyclic forbidden graph are explicit. Clearing the source-review flag does not mark the mathematical conjecture proved.
