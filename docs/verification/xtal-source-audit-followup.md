## Corrections and derivations

**3.10:** Qualified ordinary **polar**, symmetric rank-two tensors and conventional nonmagnetic symmetry at the reference state. Axial tensors and magnetic time reversal have different transformation laws. Uniaxial symmetry allows two principal coefficients but does not force inequality. The plot r(n)∝nᵀKn is generally **not an ellipsoid**: fordiag(1,2), the45° radial point fails the ellipse equation with semiaxes1,2. Labels now describe radial response and surfaces of revolution. Sliders can generate indefinite tensors; the picture explicitly plots the positive part and labels discarded negative values. Removed the invented minimum0.02radius and enlarged rotated-axis extents. Exact zero invariance is not replaced by an arbitrary10⁻¹⁷ readout floor.

For a finite orthogonal group, averaging its tensor representation is idempotent: multiplying the sum by any group element permutes it. Its image is exactly the invariant subspace. Trace equals dimension for this projection. On Sym²(V), the character is[(trR)²+tr(R²)]/2: diagonalizingR overC, the eigenvalue sum over unordered pairs is half the sum of all ordered products plus the diagonal products. On V⊗Sym²(V), multiply bytrR. This derives both displayed character formulas; the tests instead solve the full component invariance constraints.

**3.12:** A piezoelectric polar rank-three tensor is symmetric in the **last two indices**, not fully symmetric. The direct displayed coefficient couples stress to polarization; the separate spring toy uses strain and does not reproduce measured quartz constants. Removed an unsourced2.3pC/N material number and unnecessary material/priority embellishments. Corrected a physical sign: the negative sublattice is displaced by+u, so its dipole change is **−qu**, not+qu. Both curves, response coefficients, arrow and readout now use−qu/A. At zero imposed strain a zero response does not diagnose inversion symmetry. The toy's deformed bonds/ions/arrow fit adaptive extents; small-response labels use relative offsets, the y-axis reserves space for small numeric ticks, and the inversion caption fits its panel.

Inversion sends a rank-three polar tensor to its negative, so invariance forces zero. For432,180° coordinate rotations eliminate all components except permutations ofxyz; a90° x rotation sendsdxyz to−dxzy, while last-pair symmetry makes them equal, so all remaining components vanish. The full32-group finite component calculation independently gives11centrosymmetric zero spaces and20noncentrosymmetric allowed spaces, with432the sole remaining zero. It does not claim that every allowed material coefficient is necessarily nonzero.

The toy minimizesΣb[b·(εb+u)]² with unit bonds. Its stationarity equation is(Σbbᵀ)u=−Σb(bᵀεb). For the three honeycomb bonds, ux=εxy anduy=(εxx−εyy)/2; opposite checkerboard bonds cancel the forcing exactly. The chosen affine reference is nonpolar; this is a linear internal-displacement illustration, not a complete quantum-polarization theory. An analytic two-sublattice inversion test replaces a finite search masquerading as structural proof: inversion about a positive site preserves negative sites iff2δbelongs to the Bravais lattice. For the square,2δ=a1+a2; for honeycombδ=(0,1), itsa2coefficient is4/3, so no inversion exists. The finite overlay is illustrative, not the proof.

**4.3:** The redundant plane indexi=−h−k is preserved. Corrected the rotation wording:120°cycles basal indices;60°also changes signs, and the actual point group determines plane equivalence. Removed an unsupported Bravais date. Zero quadruple is rejected; inconsistent unlocked indices are rejected even whenh=k=0or an intercept lies at infinity. Infinity does not make collinearity automatically valid. The visible line is now explicitly clipped to the rectangular view rather than supplying huge hidden endpoints. All125three-index inputs are checked algebraically, including basal planes and zero.

**4.5:** Corrected the false claim that arbitrary two integer zone planes generate every zone plane by **integer** combinations. They span the index plane overQ; their integer span can be a proper sublattice. Counterexample:(110),(1−10) share[001], while(100)is half their sum. The coordinate cross product determines a direct-axis index triple; converting it with the direct basis gives the geometric perpendicular to reciprocal normals, including oblique cells. Both input planes must be nonzero and nonparallel; zero is now distinguished from parallel valid orientations.
