## Mathematical and model scope

The dual convention is aᵢ·bⱼ=δᵢⱼ throughout; phases use2πi and reciprocal lengths contain no2π factor. The lattice-plane statement assumes a primitive basis, allows000 only as an explicitly undefined state, and distinguishes gcd-spaced occupied planes from all integer phase levels.

The FCC reciprocal lattice is BCC, with conventional edge2/a. Its first Brillouin polyhedron is computed by plane intersections and all-half-space tests; the24 vertices,36 edges, six square/eight hexagonal faces and volume4/a³ follow from the model. The reversed BCC case gives a14-vertex,12-face rhombic dodecahedron of volume2/a³. The proof also checks that omitted reciprocal vectors cannot cut these cells. This geometry is derived explicitly rather than inferred from an unverified picture.

The zone algorithm includes all vectors with norm≤2R for a radius-R view and uses an inverse-basis index bound to enumerate them. Counts exclude equalities; an explicit tolerance flags ties. Polygon area comes from clipping, separately from raster sampling.

The periodic theorem is mean-square convergence of rectangular Fourier projections, with normalized Parseval. The pulse model uses analytic sinc coefficients and its exact coefficient-energy tail. Color clipping does not change the error calculation. A separate off-lattice wave reports its complex phase defect and receives no periodic Fourier-error claim.

Finite diffraction evaluates a continuous exponential sum on a finite patch, with a direct sum oracle. It is not called an FFT or an infinite comb. Intensity is normalized by point count squared, while1/V is labeled only as the infinite delta weight. Replayable row offsets preserve translation along each row, and are not described as general isotropic disorder or experimental scattering. Rasters are finite-resolution displays; peaks are not distributions on the screen.
