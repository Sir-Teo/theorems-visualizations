For 4.2, log utility gives the household condition `1/(w−s)=beta/s`, so saving is `beta*w/(1+beta)`. Market clearing gives `(1+n)k_next=s`. Writing the map relative to its positive fixed point gives exactly `ln(k_next/k*)=alpha*ln(k/k*)`, proving monotone convergence for every positive initial state. Zero is an additional boundary fixed point and is excluded explicitly. Total consumption is **per young worker**, `c_young+c_old/(1+n)`, rather than a single household's consumption.

The golden rule has `f'(kgold)=1+n`, and overaccumulation is equivalent to `R*=f'(k*)<1+n`; the readout now compares gross returns and cohort growth directly. For an exact feasible resource witness, the helper reduces capital halfway from k* toward kgold. Initially this releases `(1+n)Delta`; at every later date the exact gain is `C(k*−Delta)−C(k*)>0`. Tests use the exact finite difference, not a first-order derivative approximation. Each date's gain can be split between young and old with positive individual gains, proving the existence of a Pareto-improving allocation. The UI expressly leaves the design of transfers outside the simulation and makes no claim about arbitrary government policies.
