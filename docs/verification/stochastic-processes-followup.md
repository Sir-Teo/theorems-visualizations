## Material corrections

**Watson.** The original example is Binomial(5,1/4), with PGF `((3+s)/4)^5` and mean 5/4. Buttons now reproduce it and the paper's uniform 0,1,2 law. Its least fixed point is `0.552718670341240073...`, contradicting the paper's p.143 choice of one. Original printed decimal approximations are not substituted for the exact law. Removed unsupported earlier/later priority claims. Independent 90-digit references reproduce 26 iterates and the limiting root.

The shared solver previously returned one whenever the mean was at most one, including the selectable deterministic one-child law, which never dies. It now handles no-zero-offspring laws first and bisects the monotone factor `(f(s)−s)/(1−s)`; this also avoids extremely slow fixed-point iteration and cancellation near criticality. For finite normalized laws this factor is `p0−sum(k>=2) pk*(s+...+s^(k−1))`.

Families not followed beyond the 4000-parent cap have unknown later outcomes. They now produce an explicit sample censoring range; missing extinctions are not presented as exact sample probabilities. Surname bars show only sizes 1–20 and explain omitted/censored mass. The all-zero weights convention is explicit. A neighboring total-progeny display likewise no longer identifies a 300-individual cutoff or a cropped drawing with infinity. Sampled child counts are retained, exploration stops before unknown offspring, and the slider cannot exceed that prefix. The neighboring extinction cobweb now computes through its full selectable 60 iterations. These are limited supporting corrections, not independent historical reviews of those adjacent entries.

**Pólya.** Removed the unsupported claim about when later decimal calculations were first obtained. Combinatorial return probabilities and the renewal CDF are checked against independent integer lattice-path enumeration, including direct first-hit killing. Curves use integer-time steps. Every current walker fits; artificial one-dimensional jitter and three-dimensional projection are explicit, with full-dimensional return detection. All integer times 1–2000 are selectable. Finite nonreturns are not classified as never returning, and sample proportions retain estimated sampling error.

**Cycles and trees.** The six displayed cycles are examples, not an exhaustive universal check. Their analytic log ratios cancel row normalization; the twisted triangle has `6*theta` and the untwisted triangle zero. Independent enumeration checks all 37 unoriented simple cycles on five vertices. The tree explanation uses a checked probabilistic proof and principal directed cofactors, rather than crediting an all-minors theorem to Kirchhoff. Reversible weights retain the degree normalization factors. In-tree enumeration includes every positive edge, even below the former 1e−12 threshold. Root-weight sums agree with separate principal determinants and balance equations. A root's total weight depends on both counts and transition-product weights, not on “cheap trees.”

**Ehrenfest.** The chain has period two: from a fixed start all mass is in one parity class, while stationarity gives each class mass 1/2. Thus total-variation distance stays at least1/2, despite mean relaxation and occupation convergence. Replaced the claim of reaching equilibrium with the exact first integer time when the mean enters `N/2 ± sqrt(N)` (twice the stationary standard deviation). At N=4 this time is zero, explicitly omitted on the log axis. Both axes now contain every selectable N through 60 and return means through 2^60. Integer-time means are connected only as a rendering aid. The 150-excursion cohorts disclose their one-million-step cap, censoring and standard errors, and stay fixed under path resampling. No physical time-per-step is inferred from the urn model.
