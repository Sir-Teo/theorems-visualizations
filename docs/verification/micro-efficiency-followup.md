## Explicit mathematics

Totals are(10,10), A=(x,y), B=(10−x,10−y), a,b∈[.15,.85]. Utilities are Cobb–Douglas x^a y^(1−a); CES [a x^ρ+(1−a)y^ρ]^(1/ρ), commonρ=−1.5; complements min{(1−a)x,ay}; substitutes ax+(1−a)y. Smooth utilities extend continuously as0 on either axis.

For smooth tastes, MRS = a/(1−a)·(y/x)^(1−ρ), withρ=0 for Cobb–Douglas. Equating the two MRSs gives

`eta=[b(1−a)/(a(1−b))]^(1/(1−rho))`,
`f(x)=10 eta x/[10+(eta−1)x]`.

Both endpoints(0,0),(10,10) are efficient. On the positive interior, the CES Hessian is a positive scalar times `[[-y²,xy],[xy,−x²]]`, where the scalar is `a(1−a)(1−rho)x^(rho−2)y^(rho−2)u^(1−2rho)`. It is negative semidefinite; Cobb–Douglas has the same matrix with scalar a(1−a)u/(x²y²). At a tangency choose positive λ so λ∇uA=(1−λ)∇uB in own coordinates. The corresponding concave weighted utility sum has a global maximum there, ruling out weak-all/strict-one improvement. Off the curve an intermediate exchange rate locally improves both traders. Along the curve, A's utility increases and B's decreases. Two scalar reservation roots enclose a weakly improving interval; its midpoint constructs the button target. Curve drawing samples the explicit formula; its polyline never determines membership or the selected target.

For complements the exact weakly preferred rectangle is
`[uA/(1−a),10−uB/(1−b)] × [uA/a,10−uB/b]`.
If either side is0, one good's combined requirements bind, so increasing either utility would violate feasibility. If both sides are positive, define
`delta=min(slack1/[1/(1−a)+1/(1−b)], slack2/[1/a+1/b])`.
The target A=((uA+delta)/(1−a),(uA+delta)/a), with the complementary B bundle, increases both utilities by delta and makes one resource bind. It is efficient. Equivalently, let fA=(1−a)x−ay and fB=(1−b)(10−x)−b(10−y). The efficient set is the union of fA,fB≥0 and fA,fB≤0. Exact half-plane clipping draws those two closed polygons. At a=b=.5 they collapse to the diagonal. At a=.15,b=.3,A=(5,5), the weak rectangle has a horizontal side longer than6 and zero vertical side; all its points leave both utilities unchanged.

For substitutes, if a>b, efficiency is x=10 or y=0; if a<b it is x=0 or y=10. If a=b, uA+uB=10 everywhere. Off the relevant edges, set q to the midpoint of the two MRSs. Move A by(δ,−qδ) when a>b, or(−δ,qδ) when a<b, choosing the maximal feasible δ until an edge binds. Both utility gains are strictly positive. These targets are exact resource-binding constructions, including boundary cases, rather than clamped interior approximations.

Membership uses a declared 1e−10 numerical residual: y−f(x), distance to the indicated substitute edges, or the smaller complement-rectangle side. The readout says **matches within numerical tolerance**, not exact equality for arbitrary floating-point input. Off-set membership remains separate from the independently computed utility gains. Both gains must exceed1e−12 to show a resolved strict witness. A 1e−8 smooth-curve perturbation is explicitly off-set but may have gains below floating-point resolution; it is never relabeled efficient because a raster misses it or a gain is unresolved.
