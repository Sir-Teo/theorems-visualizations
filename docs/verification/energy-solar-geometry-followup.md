The independent geometric proof rotates the solar vector `(cosδ cosω, cosδ sinω, sinδ)` into an orthonormal local east/north/up basis. Dotting with the vertical gives altitude; dotting with the equator-facing panel normal gives incidence, including the southern-hemisphere sign. The implementation uses atan2 for azimuth. The noon-normal condition is |latitude|−sδ, only when it lies in 0–90° and the Sun is above the horizon. Polar night reports no beam energy rather than presenting an arbitrary zero-energy tilt as an optimum. Negative incidence remains signed in the readout; the panel front receives zero beam from behind.

Writing altitude sine as A+Bcosω and panel incidence as a+bcosω, the intersection of their positive intervals ends at ω*. Without attenuation the daily energy is exactly `I0 ×24/π ×[aω*+b sinω*]/1000` kWh/m². The factor24/π combines dh=12/π dω and the symmetric afternoon. With attenuation, 160 midpoint samples integrate only that daylight/front-facing interval. The reference test independently finds the time-domain boundaries by bisection and applies Simpson quadrature. This avoids the discontinuity at the horizon that a vertical panel would introduce into an unaligned full-day midpoint reference.
