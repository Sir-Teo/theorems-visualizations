## Sources checked

- [Wasserman, 36-705 Lecture20](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~larry/=stat705/Lecture20.pdf): §§1–3 Monte Carlo, conditional bootstrap variance, percentile/basic intervals; §6 mean-root bootstrap consistency and distinction between population substitution and finite-B simulation; §4 parametric bootstrap; §7 uniform-maximum counterexample. Our smooth theorem states a consistency result without a rate, for a continuously differentiable transformation of a mean with finite positive variance and nonzero derivative. It does not reuse the lecture's stronger third-moment Berry–Esseen rate.
- [Shalizi, bootstrap notes](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~cshalizi/402/lectures/08-bootstrap/lecture-08.pdf): model simulation/refitting, studentization, resampling vectors as units, preserving dependence, nonsmooth/extreme-value failures.
- [Efron and Narasimhan (2020), §2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7958418/): exact BCa tail transformation with both occurrences of z0; nonconstant-variance adjustment and bootstrap bias correction. No blanket higher-order coverage guarantee is claimed.
- [Efron (1979), original paper](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176344552), read using its [university-hosted scan](https://blogs.helsinki.fi/bk-club/files/2012/05/Efron_Bootstrap_AS1979.pdf). Rendered and inspected printed pp.2–3 and7. Page3 explicitly defines empirical n-draw resampling with replacement, separates it from permutations, and distinguishes exact conditional laws from Monte Carlo approximation. Page2 introduces the jackknife comparison and §5 connection; page7 treats the sample median, smoothing, and finite-simulation error. The editable five-point visualization is a modern synthetic example, not Efron's numerical dataset.
- [NIST bootstrap guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/bootplot.htm): conditional resampling and limitations for extreme-dependent statistics.
- [Athreya (1987)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176350371), original infinite-variance citation; publisher full-text requests were blocked. The restricted Pareto/stable-law claim was cross-checked against the [International Biometrics Society short course, slides54–55](https://www.biometrische-gesellschaft.de/fileadmin/AG_Daten/Weiterbildung/PDFs/Bootstrap_Methods_for_Short_Course_in_Germany_Part_II.pdf), which explicitly identifies Athreya's Theorem1 for 1<alpha<2. The page claims inconsistency of the ordinary bootstrap law in this stable-attraction case, not that every infinite-variance statistic fails or that every finite simulation must show a particular coverage.
- [Künsch (1989)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176347265): publication verified on the [author's ETH page](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~kuensch/papers/); original author-uploaded abstract states block-length growth and length/n→0 under appropriate conditions. The implemented circular-block variant is labeled explicitly; it is not attributed as an exact reproduction of the original moving-block algorithm. Stationary Gaussian AR(1) variance is derived directly from its finite covariance sum.

All explanatory text is original paraphrase. No external numerical dataset or illustration is embedded.
