## Exact models and repaired claims

Centipede has n2–10 decision nodes, strict own-money preferences and observed history. At index m the mover receives4·2^(m−1), the other2^(m−1). Passing hands over the doubled piles. The all-pass payoff is the **fixed leaf at index n+1**, with the virtual next mover assigned the larger pile; that index is not another choice. All-pass is better for both than(4,1) for all offered n≥2. At the last decision, take gives twice the mover's pass payoff. If the next player takes, this same strict comparison holds at each earlier decision. Thus the unique pure SPE takes at every node, including off-path nodes; its initial outcome is(4,1).

The finite-level family is separately authored: level0 passes everywhere; levelk≥1 best-responds against opponent levelk−1 while optimizing its own later decisions, passing at exact ties. Plans are complete at all owned nodes. The actual selected pair then follows its two plans; its levels may differ from the opponents used in constructing them. There is no claim that the grid is experimental behavior or an incomplete-information equilibrium. For n10, levels(9,0) stop at3. The old silent `min(displayedLevel,n)` is gone: every real level0–10 remains displayed and computed at every game length. A fixed11×11 grid retains every level pair. The subgame inspector shows each take payoff and **prescribed** pass continuation at readable size, including the terminal leaf, rather than crowding11 labels onto a narrow horizontal tree.

For a selected Centipede profile, the report of profitable single-node deviations checks **every** decision node. At each node it compares the prescribed action with changing only that action while later selected choices stay fixed. This is separate from the global best-response construction against the hypothesized preceding-level opponent. The readout gives full owned-node plans, strategy counts, actual terminal index/payoffs and off-path deviation locations. Root/previous/next/outcome navigation keeps the inspected history coherent when n shrinks. Saved state retains the node count and a jointly validated inspected index; slider state retains all three exact control values.

Ultimatum has exactly11 integer offers0–10. A complete responder plan has2^11 possibilities. With only money preferences, positive offers are strictly accepted and zero has two best actions. There are exactly two **pure** SPE: offer0/accept0, or offer1/reject0, accepting all positive offers in both. The zero-tie selector exposes both; no mixed-profile classification is asserted.

Fixed threshold m0–11 accepts x≥m; m11 means never. The authored population is uniform over integers max(0,μ−w)…min(11,μ+w), μ0–11,w0–5; the proposer knows the distribution, not the drawn threshold. For each offer x, the exact accepted-type count Kx and support size D give F=Kx/D and proposer money(10−x)Kx/D.
