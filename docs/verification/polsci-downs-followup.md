## Explicit finite contracts

**4.13.** Three fixed authored integer electorates over positions0–10. Every individual supports the nearer party if its distance is at most the offered cutoff1/2/4/10; equal distances divide expected votes equally; otherwise the person abstains. This distance cutoff is explicitly distinct from the article's insufficiently differentiated-choice discussion. All66 ordered displayed A≤B profiles and all11 unilateral proposals are computed. Free deviations may cross the opponent. Ordered deviations require A'≤B and B'≥A. All weak maximizing proposals are retained, rather than selecting one and connecting it to misleading continuous curves. Green means no profitable *allowed* move in this finite comparison; it is not a continuous-space theorem or convergence certificate. Coupled actual controls keep displayed positions ordered, and source/controls explain that adjusting one past the other moves the other to the same position. All original three-party/unimodality-only convergence claims are removed.

**5.1.** Even m other iid Bernoulli(q) voters and fair random tie resolution. A's existing lead is at least two when nonzero. Voting A instead of abstaining therefore changes A's win probability by exactly t/2, where t is the exact tie probability. With B the full win-versus-loss benefit, expected gain is Bt/2. Equality with c is indifference, rather than a strict vote decision. The central tie coefficient uses a recurrence, and biased log probabilities use the exact multiplicative factor[4q(1−q)]^(m/2). The full2,500 even sizes2–5000 are plotted, with six selectable counts; logs preserve the positive mathematical tails even below binary64 range. The plotted y-coordinate is log10 expected gain with a log10 cost reference, rather than clipped probability zero. No asymptotic formula is treated as an exact finite break-even threshold, and the former unsourced modern-election example is removed.

**5.7.** Already-participating voter, exogenous baseline accuracy a0, independently specified switch-decisiveness p, exponential improvement a(s)=1−(1−a0)e^(−s), linear positive effort cost. Gain over baseline is pB(a(s)−a0)−cs, so K=pB(1−a0) and s*=max(0,log(K/c)). The p here compares switching between candidates, whereas5.1 compares voting with abstaining; in that item's tie setting these are t and t/2 respectively. The complete left curve goes beyond every actual optimum, including s*>20, and the right curve shows optimal effort versus p on the offered1e-9…1 interval. Zero paid effort retains a0.
