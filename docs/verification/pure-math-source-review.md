## Uniform RCF witness and Laurent-field correction

LOGIC5.4 is now **closed**. Independently checked the following explicit cardinal-uniform construction (proposed during this verification). It is our proof, not a claim that Weiss–D’Mello’s p38 remark contains it. Read the complete ordered-field embedding proof, Lemma13 pp42–43, with its claim and ordering argument; also checked the RCF polynomial intermediate-value axiom scheme in the source pp11–12. The algebraic-extension refinement below is supplied explicitly because the printed compactness construction alone does not establish algebraicity.

For every uncountable cardinal κ, put I=κ·ω and J=κ·ω₁, as ordinal products. Both have cardinality κ; their cofinalities are respectively ω and ω₁, even when κ is singular. For λ=ω orω₁, the block boundaries κ·α (α<λ) are cofinal inκ·λ; fewer thanλ indices lie in a bounded collection of blocks sinceλ is regular, so cannot be cofinal. Starting with Q, recursively form F_{α+1}=F_α(t_α), with each new indeterminate positive and larger than every element of F_α; take unions at limit stages. This ordering is defined algebraically: the sign of a nonzero rational function in t_α is the sign of the quotient of its leading coefficients. Products preserve signs, and the sum of two positive rational functions is positive by comparing degrees; in the equal-degree case the leading coefficients add positively. Thus this is an ordered field extension. Every element of F_I or F_J is a rational function in finitely many of the t_α. Both fields have cardinality κ.

Weiss–D’Mello Lemma13 pp42–43 supplies an order-preserving embedding of any ordered field F into a real closed ordered field L. Its compactness proof does NOT by itself assert that L/F is algebraic. Instead take R={x∈L:x is algebraic over F}. This is a subfield: finitely many algebraic elements generate a finite-dimensional extension of F, closed under field operations. If a polynomial over R changes sign between a<b in R, it has a root c between them in L. Its finitely many coefficients generate a finite extension E/F, and c is algebraic over E, so E(c)/F is finite and c is algebraic over F. Thus c belongs to R. Consequently R satisfies every polynomial intermediate-value axiom used to define RCF in Weiss–D’Mello pp11–12, and is an algebraic real closed ordered extension of F. Since an infinite field has only |F| polynomials, each with finitely many roots, |R|=|F|=κ.

Every y∈R is bounded in absolute value by an element of F. For a monic polynomial y^n+Σ_{i<n}a_i y^i=0, set M=max_i|a_i|∈F and B=1+M. If |y|≥B, then |y|>1 unless M=0 (the zero-root case), and Σ|a_i||y|^i≤M(|y|^n−1)/(|y|−1)≤|y|^n−1<|y|^n, contradicting the polynomial equation. Thus |y|<B (or y=0), over any ordered field; no Archimedean assumption is used. This proves F cofinal in R.

For I, a countable cofinal sequence of indices yields a countable cofinal sequence of t_α in F_I and R_I: every base-field element has finite support, and every real-closure element has a base-field bound. An ordered field has no finite cofinal subset, so cf(R_I)=ω.

For J, any countable subset of R_J has countably many base-field bounds. The union of their finite supports is countable and is bounded in J because cf(J)=ω₁. A later t_α dominates all those bounds, so no countable subset is cofinal. A cofinal ω₁-sequence of indices gives a cofinal ω₁-sequence of variables, so cf(R_J)=ω₁. Thus R_I and R_J have the same cardinality κ and are nonisomorphic as ordered fields. The order is also definable in a real closed field by x<y iff y−x is a nonzero square, so their pure field reducts cannot be isomorphic either.
