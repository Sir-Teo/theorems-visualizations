## Sources actually consulted

- [Etingof et al., official MIT author text](https://math.mit.edu/~etingof/reprbook.pdf), downloaded 2026-10-22. Printed pp. 107–111 cover products, induction, character formula and the two reciprocity conventions; pp. 135–147 cover GL₂, Artin induction and abelian semidirect products. The chapter uses the left-coset tensor convention, explicitly related to the book's right-coset function convention. The degree bound is derived from reciprocity and the abelian character theorem.
- [Conrad, Math 210B: Mackey theory and applications](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/210BPage/handouts/mackey.pdf), Proposition 1.1 and §2. The intersection convention is consistently H ∩ tHt⁻¹ with conjugated character h ↦ ψ(t⁻¹ht).
- [Bernstein, Problem assignment 7.5](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~bernstei/courses/2007_Spring/Representations_of_finite_groups/pr/pr7v.pdf), 10 May 2007, actual four-page proof. “Elementary” means a cyclic prime-to-p factor times a p-group, not “elementary abelian.” The displayed S₄ integer certificates are computed separately from explicit D₈ and C₃ line characters.
- [Representation theory of finite groups I, §4.3](https://mathvideos.org/representation-theory-of-finite-groups-i/rtfg-i-notes-4-3/), actual theorem and proof through the character-kernel construction. Counting and conjugation stability alone are explicitly distinguished from the difficult closure assertion.

Source attempts that were not used: the SMF Bolte PDF request timed out; the Bonn Schwede PDF returned 404. Neither body was recovered from another source or treated as read. Serre and Fulton–Harris section pointers in the original plan were replaced with the actual consulted bodies, rather than claiming their numbering was verified. All changes to prerequisites and refs are synchronized in the syllabus. No original-source uncertainty elsewhere was removed.

## Computations and visual design

The shared model enumerates S₃ and S₄ as permutations. A coset map builds monomial induced matrices; traces and weighted character products come from those matrices. S₄ restrictions include both S₃ and A₄, whose two nonreal characters are retained. Dihedral examples cover n=3…12, distinguishing fixed dual characters from two-element orbits. Artin certificates use exact half-integer coefficients on the Klein four-group; Brauer certificates use signed integer combinations for all five S₄ irreducibles. Affine maps over F₃/F₅/F₇ give explicit Frobenius kernels. GL₂(F₃/F₅) enumerates all 48/480 matrices, classifies their conjugacy types and constructs the Borel principal series.

Scenes differ by construction: a branching diagram; complex amplitudes routed through fibres; a clickable multiplicity matrix with signed summands; fixed-coset trace diagrams; double-coset boxes; a tensor table; a degree bound and polygon; character orbits; a signed waterfall; virtual-character cancellation tiles; affine point permutations and kernel multiplication; and a full matrix mosaic. Actual computations drive each live readout. General classification claims are separated from the finite cases: the GL₂ scene does not claim to build cuspidal matrices, and finite examples are not presented as proofs of general induction theorems.
