## Corrections and independent mathematical reasoning

4.1 now states exact finite Bin(n−1,p), mean c=(n−1)p, variance c(1−p), and expected edge count nc/2. The Poisson(c) law is a fixed-c sparse limit: sum of Bernoulli probabilities is c, maximum probability c/(n−1) tends to zero. Degrees are dependent; for distinct vertices the covariance is p(1−p), arising from their shared edge. A finite degree histogram is consequently compared with a marginal law, not treated as independent degree trials.

Conditional on any possible incident-edge configuration with at least two neighbors, a specified pair of those neighbors is linked with probability p by independence. At p0 the conditioning event is impossible. This does not give E(global transitivity)=p. Independent n3 enumeration yields E(transitivity | a wedge exists)=p/(3−2p), while conditional neighbor-pair closure is p. The old unconditional clustering identity and unrestricted logarithmic-distance assertion were removed. Conditional on M with positive probability, all M-edge graphs have the same independent-edge probability, proving the exact conditional G(n,M) relationship; the old broad finite-ensemble equivalence was removed.

4.1 now draws every selected vertex (20/60/120) of the same graph that supplies the complete histogram. Every finite degree0…n−1 is counted. A final explicit k≥n bucket holds the full Poisson overflow, while the finite Binomial law has zero mass there. Expected values and actual mean, variance, triangles, wedges, component sizes and transitivity remain separate. With no wedges the ratio is undefined. Fixed size/seed shares pair uniforms across p, so its graphs nest. The uint32 sample seed is persisted by a scoped state adapter.

4.2 now distinguishes fixed c from finite G(n,c/n) expected degree c(1−1/n); treats c0 exactly; separates subcritical, exactly critical and supercritical limits; and states adjustable-constant critical probability bounds. In the original Erdős–Rényi notation the edge/vertex parameter is c_old and our c=2c_old. Their equation x exp(−x)=2c_old exp(−2c_old) gives q=x/c, S=1−q and S=1−exp(−cS). Frieze–Karoński use the degree parameter directly.

For c>1, F(S)=1−exp(−cS)−S has F(0)=0, F′(0)=c−1>0, F″<0, F(1)<0: there is exactly one positive root. For c≤1 no positive root exists. Dividing the small-S expansion gives S~2(c−1). The PGF extinction theorem motivates the equation but does not turn finite graph exploration into survival forever; the global giant conclusion is attributed to the random-graph theorem.

The finite curves are explicitly authored G(n,M) edge-order samples at n100/1000/10000, not finite G(n,c/n) samples. With ideal independent uniform vertex draws, each unordered nonloop pair has the same probability per attempt; rejecting loops/seen pairs chooses uniformly among remaining edges. Thus the first M accepted edges give uniform G(n,M). The finite PRNG is a reproducible illustration, not a claim of exact iid distribution over seeds. All component-size changes are retained in exact staircase paths. For the declared hundredth-grid c, M=floor(tick*n/200), avoiding a floating floor off-by-one; the dot uses the actual2M/n, which is separately reported. At n100,c1.01, for example, M50 gives realized degree1.00, distinct from c1.01.

The positive-root helper uses the divided equation `(c−1)+c[(1−exp(−cS))/(cS)−1]=0`, with a stable small-argument series and bisection. It avoids accidentally returning the ever-present zero root or relying on slow fixed-point iteration near criticality. The Poisson overflow is summed as a positive series rather than subtracting a nearly-unit CDF. Tiny mathematically positive probabilities can still underflow in binary64; the implementation does not promise rigorous enclosures or positive representability of every tail.
