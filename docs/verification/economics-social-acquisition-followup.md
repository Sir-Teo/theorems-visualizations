## Robinson: complete original, not just the convergence statement

[Julia Robinson, *An Iterative Method of Solving a Game*, Annals of Mathematics54(2),296–301](https://ranger.uta.edu/~weems/NOTES6319/PAPERSONE/robinson.pdf) is a new University of Texas Arlington teaching-copy lead. The seven-page PDF contains the archive cover plus all six original pages. Read pp296–301; the scanned middle pp297–300 were rendered and visually read because text extraction omitted them.

Definition1 and its alternative on pp296–297 permit simultaneous or alternating payoff-vector updates. For zero initial vectors p297 gives the exact lower/upper minimax bounds represented by the card. The Theorem p297 states convergence to the game value. Definition2 p298 defines eligibility over an interval; Lemma2 pp298–299 bounds each vector's coordinate spread by2at when every action is eligible, and Lemma3 p299 bounds the inter-vector gap by4at. Lemma4 pp299–301 deletes an action never eligible during a block, applies induction on proper submatrices, and combines the all-eligible and missing-action blocks to obtain a sublinear accumulated gap. This replaces the card's inaccurate account based on best responses remaining fixed for a stretch. The normalized gap and compactness/continuity give the stated optimal limit-point conclusion.

The final cited exponent t^(-1/(m+n−2)) still depends on the later Shapiro1958 rate analysis. [Wiley's exact original record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1002/cpa.3160110408) redirects the web reader to bibliographic HTML; its explicit epdf viewer was unavailable, and a direct download produced a6154-byte “Enable JavaScript and cookies” challenge, not the seven-page article. [The author/repository index entry](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228010560_Note_on_a_computation_method_in_the_theory_of_games) explicitly has no full text. Later papers quoting the rate are discovery leads only. The flag stays for that precise rate/original gap; Robinson's proof is no longer listed as missing.

## Kirman: verified original working paper and exact model boundary

[Alan Kirman, *On Ants and Markets*, EUI Working Paper88/351](https://cadmus.eui.eu/bitstreams/895f210b-2f8c-59c0-b7b6-2980b5636cb3/download), printed June1988, is a new public EUI Library/Cadmus primary manuscript. The32-page file contains its covers, complete18-page paper and subsequent working-paper catalog. Read all paper pp1–18 and visually inspected Eq1 p4 and Figures1–4 pp7–8,13,15. The browser-facing web reader returned403, but the public archive download yielded the full7MB PDF; no access control was bypassed.

On p4, select a person uniformly, select another distinct person, copy with probability1−ε and otherwise choose a group by a fair coin. The exact upward transition is

`P(k→k+1)=(1−k/N)[ε/2+(1−ε)k/(N−1)]`,

and the downward transition is

`P(k→k−1)=(k/N)[ε/2+(1−ε)(N−k)/(N−1)]`.

These match the existing visualization's conditional sample of the selected agent and its exclusion from the potential copied peer. Its independent mode corresponds to ε=1. Pages5–6 discuss the binomial/absorbing special cases and symmetric-beta limit; Figure1 p7 displays equilibrium shapes and Figure2 p8 displays slow switches for smallε versus central fluctuations for largeε. Those panels were inspected as figures, not inferred from captions alone.

Pages9–11 motivate communication and changes of views in financial markets. Pages11–14/Figure3 give a separate fundamentalist/chartist exchange-rate equation, and pp14–17/Figure4 discuss two fundamentalist views, endogenous switches and the ratio of meetings to price updates. The card's `100 exp[1.2(x−1/2)]` is not that exchange-rate equation: it remains explicitly an independently chosen illustrative price rule. The credit and reference now name this actually read1988 primary paper rather than claiming verification of the inaccessible1993 final *Ants, Rationality, and Recruitment*.
