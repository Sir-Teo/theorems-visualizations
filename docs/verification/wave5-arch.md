## Chosen model decisions

1. Funicular ordinate is−M/H with stated downward sign, not beam displacement. Point loads give polygonal slopes; distributed load gives a curve. Actual reaction and moment equations are used.
2. Rectangular central-load beam uses correct SI conversions, stress and L/250 constraints separately. Width is the maximum of both derived requirements. L/250 and material constants are chosen, not universal span rules. A labelled non-slender alert does not certify applicability.
3. Stress-volume proxies include the triangular truss tie and exclude external cable anchors/arch abutments; buckling, serviceability and construction costs are explicitly excluded. There is no promised order-of-magnitude win or actual steel weight.
4. Historical uniform one-sided pivot moment versus linear-elastic first-fibre threshold: factor3 is conditional, not a real rupture law.
5. Pin-pin Euler and cantilever effective length are separate from a compression cap. Mode amplitude is normalized for visibility; no invented historical material failures.
6. Deck/arch example solves an explicit positive two-coordinate quadratic compatibility model. Transfer and force shares come from the matrix solution; no invented thrust distribution or calibrated Maillart field.
7/10. Exact finite rhombus motion is separate from infinitesimal rigidity. Square, diagonal, redundant and count-passing collinear cases use actual matrix rank. The collinear case can have a first-order mechanism without a corresponding finite flex. Numerical tolerance is displayed and is not an exact near-singular certificate.
8/11. Every actual bar force follows a supported axial solve. The load vector is normalized before solving, and force/reaction coefficients are scaled only at the final products, avoiding intermediate loss of representable subnormal forces. Physical displacements can still underflow; the displayed residual is explicitly the normalized solver residual scaled by load, not a claim that independently quantized output forces balance exactly. Matching lower chord cancels selected diagonals only for the specified three loads; flat chord and extra load change them. Pratt, Howe, Warren and bowstring have genuinely different connectivity/geometry. Warren has staggered upper nodes. Chosen common EA=10⁶ kN, constant section stiffness for the force solve; separate proxy excludes buckling/joints/minimum gauges.
9/15. One connected reciprocal shares four force vertices. Every pair is parallel and its edge length gives force magnitude; all three joint cycles and external cycle use the same coordinates. Zero load collapses the force diagram without fabricating directions. Force and geometry units differ explicitly. Positive-load reciprocal coordinates use the original unit-load geometry before tiny-force rounding; physical P and forces are reported separately.
12. Actual icosahedron faces are midpoint-subdivided and projected onto a sphere, then cut to an upper cap. Full3D coordinates determine rank; projection is labelled orthographic. Open boundary mechanisms remain. A separate supported tetrahedron solves actual axial stiffness; edge removal/flattening exposes rank loss. No load distribution is claimed for the open cap.
13. Authored symmetric double-cantilever levers with equal central reactions; unequal arms require counterweight(P/2)a/b. No historical forces or human-load instructions.
14. Tensile stress positive, upward z and below-centroid tendon eccentricity positive. Effective force is supplied; net sagging moment M−Pe. Linear uncracked stress and first chosen tensile-threshold comparison do not simulate nonlinear cracks. Near-boundary rounded classification is explicitly unresolved.
