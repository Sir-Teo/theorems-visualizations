## Mathematical and visual corrections

**11.19.** The previous Farkas paragraph inferred existence of an equal-value pair merely from impossibility of a positive-gap pair. That implication does not follow. Now, because cᵀx≤p* follows from the nonempty feasible system Ax≤b, −x≤0, the inhomogeneous Farkas lemma gives y,z≥0 with Aᵀy−z=c and bᵀy≤p*. This is dual feasibility; weak duality forces equality. The proof is valid with degeneracy. Unsupported unpublished/first-publication priority metadata is removed. Every finite objective sample fits the number line; the primal drawing window clips the unbounded region and does not classify its status. Zero sampled pairs are reported as no comparison, rather than a vacuous checkmark. A computed zero gap is explicitly numerical evidence.

**11.22.** The exact numerical family's reduced costs, not a generic cube resemblance, explain the path. For bits b_i specifying x_i=0 or upper-row tightness, every assignment is feasible. Indeed the prior-variable weighted sum is at most10·100^(i−2), so the i-th upper bound is at least0.8·100^(i−1)>0. If z_j is the nonbasic x_j or slack, ∂x_j/∂z_j=σ_j=(−1)^b_j. At each later active upper row, the derivative alternates ±2σ_j10^(i−j); inactive x_i derivatives vanish. Summing objective coefficients gives reduced cost (−1)^(b_j+…+b_n)10^(n−j). Therefore the largest positive coefficient flips the first even-suffix bit, producing reflected Gray code. Every one of 2ⁿ vertices is visited, with 2ⁿ−1 strictly increasing pivots. The same algebra bounds the objective by100^(n−1), attained only at x_n=100^(n−1), other coordinates zero. Greatest immediate increase chooses this coordinate initially. Variable/slack units are essential; drawing rescaling does not change the solver's units. No general complexity guarantee is assigned to the other rules, and a nonoptimal solver trace is marked incomplete.

**11.23.** Corrected the barrier gradient: for maximizing μcᵀx+Σlog(slack), it is μc−Σa_i/slack, while the positive matrix used for the Newton ascent direction is Σa_i a_iᵀ/slack². The old implementation added the barrier gradient with the wrong sign. Backtracking now stays inside the domain and reports tolerance or limit status. The current point is solved at the actual displayed μ rather than mislabeled from a nearby cached sample. Actual objective error, exact-center m/μ benchmark and numerical stationarity residual are separate. An optimal **face** can be an edge; a single green optimal vertex is just a reference.

The old ellipsoid representation suffered determinant/Cholesky cancellation near40 iterations. The corrected algorithm maintains a square-root factor B, Q=BBᵀ. In unit-ball coordinates u=Bᵀa/‖Bᵀa‖, the update is B←√(4/3)·B·[I+(1/√3−1)uuᵀ], followed by an orthogonal column rotation restoring triangular form. This preserves the same mathematical ellipsoid while avoiding subtraction of nearly equal Q entries. Area is πB11B22; the exact ratio is4/(3√3). Point containment is independently tested by triangular solves, not the unstable inverse determinant. Views fit all displayed ellipses; optional history and extreme edge directions can compress the polygon because the ellipses genuinely become long and thin. The caption explains this. Rational encoding, degeneracy handling and precision bounds are distinguished from this fixed floating-point demo; an incorrect constant-factor/Newton-iteration complexity explanation was removed.

**11.24.** The proof uses n independent tight rows of **[A;−I]**, not an unspecified square subsystem omitting nonnegativity. The incidence matrix is explicitly unsigned for simple undirected graphs. A minor with a zero column has determinant0; one nonzero entry permits induction; with two entries in every column, the bipartition's signed row sum vanishes. An odd cycle gives determinant±2; assigning half on its cycle and zero outside is a fractional vertex because cycle equations plus outside-zero constraints have full rank. Minors now use exact BigInt Bareiss determinants. The zero-edge polytope has the unique empty matching vertex, and its display has no fictitious edge column. “Add an odd cycle” really ensures a triangle even after Clear or arbitrary edits. Vertex enumeration remains a disclosed finite floating-point calculation. The restored historical credit is supported by the original Hoffman–Kruskal reprint.
