## Exact dating comparison and uncertainty convention

Renne2013 **printed p684**, both complete relevant body columns read and visually inspected (`renne-p684.png`): pooled Beloc impact tektites **66.038 ± .025/.049 Ma**; IrZ coal bentonite, the closest stratigraphic boundary proxy, **66.043 ± .011/.043 Ma**. The direct comparison is a statistically insignificant difference of **5 ± 27 kyr**. Its two-sided interval is −22 to +32 kyr under that convention, explaining the abstract’s32kyr phrasing. It is not a universal hard limit on event separation or a measured extinction duration. **Printed p687 note9**, visually inspected (`renne-p687.png`), specifies **68% confidence** throughout; note11 says X/Y exclude/include systematic sources. The more precise relative comparison should not be obtained by independently adding full absolute systematic errors. These are the paper’s historical calibration results, not a claim to the latest absolute numerical age.

The complete subsequent discussion distinguishes fast terrestrial recovery from much longer marine recovery and notes precursory environmental stress and uncertain Deccan chronology. Temporal coincidence is supporting causal evidence, not a stand-alone proof that no other stresses existed.

Suggested precise wording: “Renne and colleagues’2013 dating found the impact tektites and a boundary-proxy ash indistinguishable, with a relative age difference of5±27 thousand years at68% confidence.” A shorter “within roughly30,000 years of dating uncertainty in that study” is reasonable with the source and convention available nearby.

## Crater identification and 2016 drilling

Hildebrand1991 full pp867–871: **p867 Gravity and Magnetic Data**, Figs2–3, the circular Bouguer anomaly is approximately180km; **p870 Origin** interprets it as an approximately180km peak-ring crater. Petrology on pp868–869 reports shocked quartz in breccias; **p870 Age** explicitly leaves exact age uncertain. Its speculative late greenhouse/seismic discussion is not modern direct evidence for every consequence clause. It does not give a10–15km projectile estimate.

Morgan2016 full pp878–882: **p878 body** uses an approximately200km multiring structure; **p880 body** identifies **April–May2016**, joint IODP/ICDP Expedition364, siteM0077A, core505.7–1334.7mbsf, peak-ring top618mbsf and felsic basement approximately748–1334.7mbsf. **p881 body and Fig3** give fractured/shocked felsic basement with low density/velocity, supporting dynamic uplift and collapse; **p882** says impact energy and pollutants still require better constraints. This fully supports “drilling of its peak ring in2016.” It does not directly measure a projectile size or the entire global extinction mechanism.

**180–200km** is therefore a defensible rounded *impact-structure* range. Collins’s fresh crater rim diameter should retain the existing caution against treating every apparent structural ring as an identical diameter definition.

## Worldwide layer versus site-specific indicators

Schulte2010 full main paper: **p1214 body and pp1215–1216, Figs2–3** synthesize the globally distributed ejecta layer and distinguish four distance regimes. Spherule abundance, shocked-grain size and layer thickness vary with distance and preservation; the distal layer is not a universal list of every indicator at every locality. Its **SOM pp3–4** gives individual target-rock correlations and soot/cenosphere occurrences in North America, Denmark and New Zealand. Relevant complete SOM sections were read; this review does not claim to independently remeasure every entry in its34-page supplement.

Harvey2008 full Methods, Results, Discussion and conclusions pp355–358: thirteen sampled marine/nonmarine sites; **p356 Results** reports aciniform soot in New Zealand, Italy, Denmark and Spain, with carbon cenospheres at specific New Zealand/Denmark/Canada sites. Controls address modern contamination. **p357** explicitly discusses nondetection, site-specific preservation and detection limits. Its proposed fossil-carbon origin is not evidence for global forest fires. The inference of target-hydrocarbon combustion remains distinguishable from observed particle occurrence.

Suggested wording: “A globally distributed boundary ejecta layer contains iridium anomalies, shocked minerals and impact spherules; soot is also documented at widely separated sites.” Avoid implying all four occur in every clay sample or that soot alone proves worldwide wildfires. Neither soot nor a particular microspherule morphology should be confused with the toy iridium profile in3.10.

## Consequences and projectile-size claim

The full **Schulte2010 pp1216–1218** comparison supports a *model-based* account: strong seismic effects, platform collapse and surrounding-ocean tsunamis; possible ejecta-reentry thermal radiation; atmospheric particles/gases reducing light and cooling the surface. The thermal pulse is modelled, its wildfire extent debated; p1217’s cited model does **not** imply automatic ignition of all woody biomass. Fine dust alone was considered insufficient in the cited contemporary estimate; carbonate dust and soot can add effects. Sulfur-aerosol cooling is estimated over **years to decades**, with uncertain mixture and magnitude. Thus “dust, sulfate aerosols and soot cutting light for months to years” should be qualified as inferred/modelled effects and should not assign a single confident duration to every component. A source-scoped replacement is: “Regional blast and tsunamis, a possible thermal pulse from re-entering ejecta, and atmospheric dust, soot and sulfate aerosols provide modelled mechanisms for darkness and cooling; their relative importance and durations remain uncertain, with sulfate-cooling estimates extending for years to decades.”

**Collins2005 pp830–831, full Air Blast section, Eqs54–64 and Table4** supports the generic regional airblast mechanism. Its nuclear-explosion scaling becomes less reliable for very large impacts, so do not present a precise Chicxulub damage radius from this simplified law. **p832** expressly excludes tsunami prediction from its program. **p833 Global Effects** describes possible months-long dust/photosynthesis effects while not computing them. Keep tsunami evidence attributed to the geological synthesis, not to the crater-only interactive calculator.

The exact optional **10–15km** estimate appears in the **complete relevant official [Lunar and Planetary Institute2010 coauthor-institution statement](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/features/chicxulub/)**, paragraph beginning “The Chicxulub Crater was produced…”, rather than in the three original papers requested. It is an institutional reported estimate, not a confidence interval inferred by the current solid-rock calculator. Either cite it as “LPI’s2010 summary gives roughly10–15km” or omit that optional numerical range while retaining Alvarez’s separately sourced10±4km historical inference and the explicitly model-dependent interactive inversion.

The non-avian dinosaur/ammonite extinction clause is supported by Schulte **p1217 Fossil Record**; the rough three-quarters-of-species figure is already covered by root’s separately reviewed Barnosky2011 Table1, not newly independently derived here. **66Ma** is an appropriate rounded era for this teaching item; do not restore Schulte2010’s older65.5Ma calibration as a modern date.
