## 4.6 — Toba

Read the complete original [Rampino–Self1992 NASA scan](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/pubs/docs/1992/1992_Rampino_ra00200p.pdf), Nature359 pp.50–52, all three pages using single-thread OCR. The paper models short-lived volcanic cooling, extrapolates aerosol loading, discusses regional precipitation decreases, and proposes amplification of an already developing glacial transition. It expressly warns that mixed GCM results do not support firm conclusions about ice-growth conditions. These are proposed climatic effects, not an observed human census.

Read the complete relevant text of the original 29-page [Ambrose1998 primary scan transcription](https://www.scribd.com/doc/81826970/Bottlenecks-Volcanic-Winter), JHE34 pp.623–651: especially pp.626–627 (wide range of prior effective-size estimates),631–636 (causes/timing/duration),638–640 (refugia and release),641–644 (subdivision, drift and conclusions), Fig4 and Fig5. The original hypothesizes **bottlenecks in subdivided populations**, with differential survival in tropical refuges, and alternative release times including a millennium or an isotope-stage-length event. Replaced the card's oversimplified claim that a single short event reduced all humans to a measured few thousand and left an individually detectable mark in everyone. The display's panmictic effective-size model is explicitly a simplification.

Read [Storey–Roberts–Saidin2012 complete original main article](https://www.pnas.org/content/109/46/18684.full), PNAS109 pp.18684–18688, Table1, Results/Discussion, Materials/Methods and Figs1–3. Verified **73.88±0.32ka,1σ full external errors**:45 aliquots,41 in the main population,36 after normalized-median-absolute-deviation filtering; inverse-isochron73.6±.5ka agrees. Calibration uses astronomically dated A1 tephra via Alder Creek/Fish Canyon standards. The main text directly supports the displayed date; this is not an independent audit of the supplementary aliquot spreadsheet. Removed “the date is not in doubt” in favor of the actual uncertainty.

Read [Lane–Chorn–Johnson2013 complete original main text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3657767/#sec1), PNAS110 pp.8025–8029, Results, Table1, Figs2–4, Discussion and Materials/Methods. The original identifies YTT cryptotephra by morphology/electron-microprobe chemistry in two Lake Malawi cores, reports undisturbed sediment, no major XRF disturbance/overturn evidence, and a roughly1.5°C TEX86 depression that is smaller than some other variations. Its chosen age model uses **75.0±.9ka**, explicitly discussing the calibration difference from Storey's73.88±.32ka. About1cm sediment represents30years; a regional proxy result cannot simply become a genome-wide census or a demonstration of no climatic effect anywhere. PMC/publisher routes intermittently returned challenge/cookie pages; the anchored PMC primary text eventually supplied the entire relevant article, rather than just indexed snippets. The Manchester repository PDF was separately attempted but returned403/octet-stream failure.

## 4.8 — regional disappearance windows

Read the original [Roberts et al.2001 author-uploaded primary text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11941538_New_Ages_for_the_Last_Australian_Megafauna_Continent-Wide_Extinction_About_46000_Years_Ago), Science292 pp.1888–1892, especially articulated-remains selection and optical/U-series dating, Table2, the extinction likelihood on p.1891, and p.1892's causal limitations. The **46.4ka** MLE has68%CI43.6–48.9 and95%CI39.8–51.2; their model assumes a constant-intensity Poisson burial process plus Gaussian age errors. The existing40–50bar is a schematic simplification, not that95%CI. A Washington course PDF named `prinn2001.pdf` contains only the first Roberts page after another article and was rejected as a complete copy.

Read the complete [Faith–Surovell2009 main original](https://www.pnas.org/doi/full/10.1073/pnas.0908153106), PNAS106 pp.20641–20645, Testing for Simultaneity, Tables1–2, continental/biogeographic simulation methods, Discussion and Materials/Methods. The stated **12,000–10,000 radiocarbonBP≈13,800–11,400calendarBP** is verified. Their31-genera/1,955-occurrence/66-terminal-date data and sampling model distinguish observed last dates from inferred extinction. This paper does not prove overkill rather than other synchronous causes; removed causal-exclusion language from the idea as well as the readout.

Read complete [Cooper et al.2015 original](https://conservationbytes.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/cooper-et-al-2015-science.pdf), Science349 pp.602–606,31transition events, Greenland/Cariaco synchronization, Fig1, randomization tests/Table1 and concluding interaction hypothesis. The transitions include clade replacement and invasion, not merely global species extinction. The study concerns many abrupt interstadials across56kyr. A single **14.7–11.7ka** rectangle includes both interstadial and stadial phases and cannot stand in for all those warmings. The visible caption now says so. No new numerical claim is drawn from uninspected supplementary event tables.

Read complete [Saltré et al.2016 original main article](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/2a7d5444-1bc2-4fee-b34b-0cc8a162111b/content), Nature Communications7:10511, pp.1–7, Results, Figs1–2/Table1, Discussion and Methods. It quality-filters659fossil ages, combines six extinction estimators, gives a42.1ka peak with36.7–48.1interquartile model range, estimates first human presence55.6ka and median coexistence13.5kyr, and tests six continent-scale climate proxies with temporal lags. Its absence of a detected continental linear relationship **allows local and synergistic climate effects**. It does not establish no climate event or derive the display's50–65/40–50bounds.

Read the complete original main article [Dillehay et al.2008 primary scan transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/845699296/MonteVerde), Science320 pp.784–786. Monte VerdeII wood/charcoal dates14.6–14.2calendar kaBP and directly dated seaweeds14.220–13.980kaBP support a site-level occupation claim, not a14.5–16ka continent-arrival confidence interval. No original source for the display's South American8–13ka disappearance endpoints was identified.

Read complete original main text [Wilmshurst et al.2008](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0801507105), PNAS105 pp.7676–7680, all Results/Discussion, Conclusions and Materials/Methods, Figs1–3. Settlement≈1280CE is supported, but **BP=1950** is explicit in Methods. Thus1280CE→0.670calendar kaBP, outside the former0.70–0.78range. Its oldest pooled seed result is about700**radiocarbon**BP, calibrated1290–1380CE; treating700radiocarbon years as700calendar years would also be invalid. Its Fig1 archaeology1σ1280–1382CE converts to0.568–0.670calendar kaBP. With explicit root authorization to edit the New Zealand REG object, replaced its blue range with the checked **[0.568,0.670]calendar kaBP1950** interval. The row note explicitly identifies this as the oldest archaeological site’s calibrated1σ interval, **not a confidence interval for first human arrival**. The moa range remains schematic and unverified.

The full [Kirchner–Weil2000 original](https://seismo.berkeley.edu/~kirchner/reprints/2000_34_delayed_recovery.pdf), Nature404 pp.177–180, p.179 discussion and methods, supports the Signor–Lipps direction: incomplete sampling shifts observed last appearances **earlier** than true extinction. The root reviewer independently read and tested this caveat; the source is now named explicitly in4.8.
