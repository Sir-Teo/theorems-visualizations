## Shieber proof obligations

The printed Claim2 prose says dative before accusative, but its displayed lexical schema and the pp336–337 formulas use the reverse ordering: d'chind (accusative), then em Hans (dative), then laa (accusative-subcategorizing), then hälfe (dative-subcategorizing). The correct formal target is a^m b^n c^m d^n, matching the displayed schema; do not propagate the prose reversal.

The formal implication must state the empirical premises separately from the theorem about a language satisfying them. Arbitrarily large total embedding alone does not guarantee every pair of independent counts. More seriously, footnote4 restricts the needed case-count claim to equal-total noun/verb strings, and that equal-total restriction is not regular. It cannot be silently used as an additional regular filter. The peer's initial inequality direction was reversed for optional object omissions; this was corrected before any prose/source acceptance. No final proof is accepted on the weaker premises alone.

A concrete counterexample demonstrates the logical gap: M={a^i b^j c^i d^l} is context-free (S→AD, A→aAc|B, B→bB|ε, D→dD|ε), contains every a^m b^n c^m d^n, and all its equal-total noun/verb words have both case counts matched, because i+j=i+l forces j=l. Thus a nonregular equal-total restriction cannot repair the proof, and no marked-tree argument can derive non-context-freeness from just those premises.

A sufficient conditional theorem that permits optional omission uses a stronger, explicit empirical licensing premise: every overt selected NP must be licensed by a distinct compatible selected verb, each selected verb has at most one such slot, and fixed-frame slots are separately discharged. Omitted objects are permitted; unmatched extra overt NPs are not. This gives K⊆{a^i b^j c^k d^l : i≤k, j≤l}, where K is the homomorphic image of the regular lexical restriction. This premise is not attributed verbatim to footnote4 and must be disclosed as an empirical assumption. If K also contains arbitrarily large balanced diagonal words a^N b^N c^N d^N, a direct pumping proof suffices, without claiming exact equality with the four-block copy language.

Suppose K is context-free with pumping length p and choose N≥p. In any pumping split uvxyz with |vxy|≤p and |vy|>0, the total letter-increment vector (α,β,γ,δ) of v and y is nonzero and supported on at most two adjacent blocks. Therefore (γ−α,δ−β) cannot be (0,0). If either coordinate is negative, pumping twice violates i≤k or j≤l (or already violates block order). Otherwise at least one coordinate is positive, and pumping zero times makes the corresponding capacity difference negative. Every split has a bad pump. This proves the conditional non-context-freeness result while allowing optional omissions. On equal-total words, the two capacity inequalities automatically force exact case matching. An alternative exact-subfamily-equality premise is sufficient but stronger still, and must likewise be explicit.

For a completely literal word-alphabet proof, first intersect L with the exact regular lexical schema R. Then apply a genuine wordwise homomorphism: d'chind→a, em→b, Hans→ε, laa→c, hälfe→d, and all fixed frame words→ε. On R this yields the selected four-block language K; its capacity bounds and balanced-word supply must be separately assumed. This avoids silently treating multiword phrases as alphabet letters or admitting arbitrary extra words erased before the regular restriction. The coordinator was sent this precise construction.

Actual written TOC5.8 proves union/concatenation/star closure, not context-free intersection with a regular language or arbitrary homomorphic images. Future TOC6.6/6.9 cannot be used as written proof inputs. Supply the two short constructions directly: annotate a CNF variable with incoming/outgoing DFA states, composing through a split state; replace terminal grammar symbols by their finite homomorphic images, including ε. The sufficient capacity-language pumping proof is supplied above; exact equality with the copy language is not inferred from footnote4. The pumping lemma itself can be supplied from the already written marked-tree argument or a short repeated-variable CNF-tree proof.

The formal conclusion is weak non-context-freeness under the stated idealized competence-language premises. Crossing arcs in a finite example are not that proof. Other permitted word orders do not invalidate a regular-intersection argument; a non-context-free subset alone would not prove anything about its superset. The original source's pp341–342 performance and parsing cautions remain material: finite observed examples cannot establish the unbounded premise, bounded languages are finite, and non-context-freeness does not imply difficult parsing or establish that every natural language occupies one exact hierarchy level.
