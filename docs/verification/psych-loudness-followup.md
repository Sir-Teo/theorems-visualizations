## Prerequisites and mathematical scope

Read `AUTHORING.md`, the everywhere/social-science guidance in `docs/fields.md`, and the actual written bodies of **psych-3.2**, **music-1.8** and **music-1.9**. The first supplies the descriptive power-law form. The second supplies the actual decibel, pressure and intensity conversion used in the derivation. The equal-loudness-contour item is related context and moves to `see`; its ISO contour algorithm is not an input to this widget. No dependency is added to an unwritten or unused idea.

SPL is a physical RMS-pressure level; phons use matching to a 1-kHz reference; sones describe a subjective loudness ratio scale. Their meanings are separated. For a progressive plane wave at the same acoustic impedance, intensity is proportional to squared RMS pressure. Thus an intensity ratio is `10^(Δ/10)` and the pressure ratio is `10^(Δ/20)`. Pressure values displayed are calculated from `p=20e−6*10^(L/20)` Pa, never measured device pressures.

The declared approximation is `N_m(L)=10^(m(L−40)/10)`, normalized to 1 sone at 40. It yields ratio `10^(mΔ/10)` and doubling interval `10 log10(2)/m`. Exact 10-dB doubling **inside this nominal formula** uses `m=log10(2)=0.3010299956639812`. The rounded 0.3 form gives `1.9952623149688795` per 10 dB and doubles in `10.034333188799373` dB. The pressure exponent is `2m`. Neither 0.3 nor the rounded 0.301 is exactly `log10(2)`.

The nominal and rounded models are separately selectable. Custom m in [0.2,0.5] is explicitly an authored mathematical exploration, not a fitted observer or a source-reported human range. Curves are calculated formulas; there are no new listening measurements, source data points, full Cambridge/ISO implementation or unsupported extrapolation below 40/above 80.
