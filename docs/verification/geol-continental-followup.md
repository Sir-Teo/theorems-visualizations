## Corrections and derivation

4.1 explicitly distinguishes vector Fourier heat flux from an upward scalar flux with depth positive downward. Local isotropic conduction, source-free steady conservation and ideal thermal contact give constant flux, continuous temperature and inverse-conductivity gradient jumps. The lithologies/conductivities are declared chosen examples. The old fixed40K/km gradient axis clipped the legitimate120mW/m²,1.8W/m/K corner (66.67K/km); its range now follows the actual maximum. The chapter summary and scope use the dated47±2TW primary estimate with its model/coverage limitations.

4.2 requires common source-profile shape/scale and deep flux before inferring a line across sites. The same surface law fits uniform-layer and exponential profiles; it does not identify their subsurface shapes. The reduced flux is not uniquely mantle heat. For the exponential, the source extends below the chosen35km datum, so the actual flux there is `qm+A0*h*exp(-35/h)`, not simplyqm. The temperature and flux readout now shows this distinction. The horizontal35km line is a chosen datum, and no unprovided solidus or blanket melting claim is inferred.

Every source and source-free temperature curve, scatter point and fitted line determines its actual plotting range; the former1400°C/130mW/m² limits clipped valid selected models. Volumetric heat production now has its **own physical axis**, replacing a red curve scaled into temperature units. Uniform-source discontinuity is drawn as an exact step, exponential as a profile. Least-squares line sets the unconstrained optimum for the fixed synthetic dataset; the displayed residual is checked against it. Synthetic points retain their chosen seed/noise and are not field measurements.
