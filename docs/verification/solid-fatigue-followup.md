## Primary engineering source actually read

Read COMSOL's complete relevant authored model documentation in the official [Fatigue Module User's Guide 6.3 PDF](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.fatigue/FatigueModuleUsersGuide.pdf): printed **p52** (cycle cutoff), **pp60–63** (stress life/Basquin/approximate curves), **pp79–84** (cumulative damage and evaluation limits), and **pp103–104** (mean-stress interactions). Downloaded the complete 108-page PDF and rendered those sections; visually inspected the actual Basquin equation on printed p62, Miner equation(3-3) on p81, and all three interaction equations on pp103–104. Also read the complete official HTML sections [Stress Life Models](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.fatigue/fatigue_ug_sme.4.20.html), [Cumulative Damage Model](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.fatigue/fatigue_ug_sme.4.25.html) and [Mean Stress Correction](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.fatigue/fatigue_ug_sme.4.28.html).

The source defines full cycles versus reversals, a power-law stress-life segment, out-of-range high stresses, linear usage from counted bins and its absence of sequence effects, and linear/parabolic tensile-mean interaction shapes. Its mean-stress formulas describe allowed curve amplitude, rather than multiplication of the applied stress to produce an equivalent amplitude. The atlas uses those mathematical forms. Chosen strengths, scatter and the negative-mean extension are explicitly illustrative. This is primary documentation of an implemented engineering model; no claim is made to have reread the historical Wöhler/Basquin/Palmgren/Miner/Goodman originals or validated a named alloy. Unverified historical dates, mechanism explanations, numerical empirical scatter/ranking claims and a claim of literally indefinite measured survival were removed.

Also read the complete official [NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook §8.1.3.1, Censoring](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/apr/section1/apr131.htm), including Type I/II, interval/multiple censoring and failure-mode separation. Its fixed-stop Type I definition supports the distinction between an observed failure time and survival through the stop, used for both artificial specimen sets. No statistical estimation or reliability claim is inferred from the ten chosen specimens.

## Corrections

**4.7:** No unsupported low-cycle extrapolation above the supplied curve's top stress. The aluminium-like 300–440 MPa controls now explicitly give undefined life. Test-stop censoring applies to both parameter sets, fixing the former aluminium failure dots that silently clamped late lives to the stopping time. Run-outs represent survival only until the stated stop; the artificial log-life scatter and every material-like parameter are identified as chosen. Plot bounds contain all specimen dots and run-out arrows, and the curve colour matches its legend for either set.

**4.8:** Replaced the former generic nonlinear model and the two different cycle allocations masquerading as a permutation with a direct test of Miner's rule. Both orders now have identical amplitude/count bins, including when the amplitude controls cross or are equal. Their final damage and duration agree. Their trajectories and first threshold crossings can differ once the planned usage exceeds 1; the remaining history is explicitly an accounting extension beyond the model's failure threshold. This also removes the prior silent plot-only adjustment of the high amplitude, which disagreed with the numerical readout. Zero usage and allocations of 0 or 1 are supported.

**4.9:** Corrected the yield check to include compression, using the largest absolute stress over the complete cycle. For example, steel-like mean −200 MPa/amplitude 200 MPa satisfies its Goodman condition but exceeds the chosen compressive yield strength. Gerber's positive ray root is rationalised to avoid cancellation, and zero load has unbounded factors. The negative-mean flat extension is a stated choice. The complete waveform fits at mean 560 MPa/amplitude 300 MPa (peak 860 MPa), both yield limits are shown, finite yield/ray segments fit the diagram, and the draggable point itself now communicates the admitted/exceeded condition.
