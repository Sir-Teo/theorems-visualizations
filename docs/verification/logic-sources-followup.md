## Completed mathematical and interaction corrections

**Deduction theorem (4.2).** Its three cases and hypotheses agree with Enderton's second proof. The transformation produces at most three output lines per input line. The old claim of linear running time ignored formula size, tautology checks and proof-line searches; only the supported line-count bound remains. An unchecked Herbrand-priority attribution is removed. All three displayed examples and free-variable/duplicate-hypothesis edge cases produce valid transformed proofs. The tests independently evaluate every resulting formula over finite interpretations in addition to running the existing syntactic checker.

**Enumerability (4.8).** Effective presentation is essential: the symbol supply is effectively enumerable with decidable symbol classes/arities, making proof enumeration meaningful. The statement no longer implies undecidability for every restricted first-order language. The Herbrand route is explicitly equality-free; its Skolemization is equisatisfiability of the negation, and a finite propositionally inconsistent expansion is a validity certificate. Finite satisfiable levels do not prove invalidity. Work units advance levels whose computational costs differ. The scope now records the 400-term generator budget, 2,500-instance cap and SAT-work cap. A silent term truncation at 401 terms previously escaped the instance cap when only one universal variable was present; it is now marked capped. A contradictory subset still certifies unsatisfiability even if generation was truncated. Blank or malformed editable input no longer substitutes the default valid sentence and prints it under the user's sentence number. Invalid input has no first-row proof search. The last preset’s countermodel must be infinite: a finite strict transitive relation cannot give every vertex a successor (repeated successor choices would form a cycle); the natural-number strict order supplies an infinite countermodel. Unsupported publication-priority dates are replaced by the inspected proof references.

**Connectedness (4.10).** The compactness argument works relative to the axioms for simple undirected graphs. Each δn includes **a≠b**, as well as denying walks of lengths 1 through n; otherwise the degenerate a=b interpretation could defeat the stated disconnection conclusion. At stage N the path has N+2 vertices and endpoint distance N+1. The proof concerns arbitrary graphs including infinite ones; it makes no finite-only definability claim. The former broad assertion that every formula agrees on all larger cycles was not established by the displayed finite comparisons. The text now states precisely that these are finite comparisons for the selected formula, independent of the compactness proof. User input with many nested quantifiers could previously freeze the evaluator; each structure now has a disclosed 250,000-recursive-step budget. Exhaustion returns unknown/orange and is excluded from agreement counts. Exact successful evaluations agree with a separate evaluator, including the triangle distinction between C6 and two triangles.

**Neighboring finiteness display (4.9).** Finding models at sizes 1–8 no longer invokes compactness to assert an infinite model. The proof, caption, scope and live readout distinguish eight finite observations from the arbitrarily-large-finite-model hypothesis. Failed searches are inconclusive rather than a proven upper bound. The independent regression constructs a first-order sentence requiring every element to equal one of eight constants: it has models of every size 1–8, but no model with nine or more elements. This is a counterexample to the former inference. Browser input verifies that the live all-eight-found path now gives only finite evidence.

**Upward Löwenheim–Skolem (5.2).** The proof retains the entire elementary diagram, then applies downward LS while retaining **both** the embedded original A and all κ distinct new-constant values. Merely retaining A could lose the lower cardinality bound. The consequence for a theory first shrinks an oversized starting model if needed. The diagram is fixed but not drawn; displayed inequalities are only part of it. The infinite A option is explicitly countably infinite, ensuring its size does not exceed either displayed infinite target. Finite targets 4 and 8 compare inequality families and draw no infinite-cardinal conclusion. The batch slider's maximum follows the selected finite family and clamps to four, and the finite-size control is actually hidden under the infinite option. Finite pigeonhole failures and the distinction between the current batch and every batch are computed separately.

**Categoricity (5.4).** All eight rows are retained, with correct countable/uncountable patterns. A theory *with an infinite model* cannot single out one isomorphism type across all cardinalities; the missing qualification was added. Red cells now assert at least two nonisomorphic models, rather than exact model counts. In particular the original claim of only ℵ0 countable real closed fields was false. Exact-count metadata is removed from the dedicated catalogue as well. The successor row explicitly means **Th(ℕ,0,S)**, not merely an unspecified set of injective-successor axioms. An unchecked Veblen term-priority claim is removed. Explicit witnesses replace vague claims applying only at the continuum or invoking classification without supplying a pair.

The eight categoricity patterns are justified as follows, with independent arguments where elementary cardinal arithmetic suffices:

1. **Infinite pure sets:** any bijection is an isomorphism at every infinite cardinal.
2. **DLO without endpoints:** countable uniqueness is the primary back-and-forth theorem. For every uncountable κ, let D be a lexicographic sum of κ copies of ℚ followed by ω1 copies of ℚ. D and D+ℚ have size κ and are DLOs without endpoints, but cofinalities ℵ1 and ℵ0 differ. This works even when κ is singular.
3. **Nonzero ℚ-vector spaces:** dimension 1 versus 2 gives countable nonisomorphic models. An uncountable size κ forces dimension κ, since a vector uses only finitely many basis vectors over a countable field. Bases of equal size yield an isomorphism.
4. **ACF of fixed characteristic:** algebraic closures of the prime field and of its one-variable rational function field have different transcendence degree and are countable. At uncountable size κ, transcendence degree is κ; Steinitz's theorem gives uniqueness, exactly as Enderton's proof states.
5. **Equivalence relation with infinitely many infinite classes:** countable models have countably many countable classes and are matched classwise. At any uncountable κ, use κ countably infinite classes versus countably many classes of size κ; number of classes distinguishes the models.
6. **Th(ℕ,0,S):** Enderton's classification gives the standard ℕ-chain plus d ℤ-chains. d=0 and d=1 distinguish countable models; uncountable size κ forces d=κ.
7. **RCF:** countable real algebraic numbers are Archimedean; the real closure of ℚ(t), ordered with t above all rationals, is a countable non-Archimedean model. For every uncountable κ, begin with a real closed field of size κ. Its elementary diagram plus x>a for every old element a is finitely satisfiable. Compactness and downward LS retaining the field and x give an elementary extension still of size κ with a point above the old field. Repeat along ω or ω1, taking elementary unions at limit stages. Both final unions have size κ. The ω union has a countable cofinal sequence. The ω1 union has an ω1 cofinal sequence, while every countable subset lies in one bounded stage and is bounded by the next added point; its cofinality is ω1. Thus they are not isomorphic as ordered fields. This uses the primary diagram/chain theorems, not a false exact model count.
8. **Th(Rado graph):** Cameron's extension property and back-and-forth give countable categoricity. For an arbitrary uncountable κ, start once with an independent set of size κ and once with a κ-clique. At each of ω stages, add an independent layer containing one new vertex for every finite neighbor set A of the previous graph, adjacent exactly to A among old vertices. There are κ such finite sets, so size stays κ. Every finite disjoint adjacency/nonadjacency pattern A,B is in some stage and gets a witness in the next; both unions satisfy the extension axioms. These axioms have only infinite models and a unique countable model, so Łoś–Vaught gives the complete theory of the Rado graph. The first union is a countable union of independent layers and hence has no uncountable clique, while the second retains its original κ-clique. They are not isomorphic. Finite closure tests verify the construction step; a finite stage is not claimed to be a model of all extension axioms.

None of these incomplete responses is treated as proof evidence. **The original paper's reduction lemma, exact derivation details and historical claims remain unverified, and logic-4.12 remains flagged.** Enderton's modern expansion proof supports the reconstruction's contemporary finite method but does not replace that historical check.

Within that explicitly finite reconstruction, substantive corrections are nevertheless complete:

- The old SAT encoder expected negation-normal form but received implication and equivalence nodes, silently treating them as disjunctions. Each ground instance now passes through NNF before CNF encoding. The contradictory matrix P(x)∧(P(x)→¬P(x)) is now correctly refuted at N=1.
- Each bound variable is renamed uniquely **before** prenex prefix removal and numeric grounding. Without this, repeated/shadowed variable names could be substituted twice and change a satisfiable sentence into a contradiction. The satisfiable `(∀x ∃x P(x)) ∧ ∃y ¬P(y)` now has satisfiable finite prefixes and checked assignments.
- A satisfiable current prefix no longer asserts all twelve prefixes are satisfiable: the supplied delayed contradiction first fails at N=5, even when the slider is at N=1. If twelve prefixes all pass, the text still makes no infinite-model conclusion. Infinitary compactness/selection is conditional on **every** Dn being satisfiable.
- Greedy witness queries preserve the solver's third state, unknown; exhaustion is not interpreted as false. Partial-assignment tree nodes are orange when unresolved. The structure panel previews at most seven coordinates of the current ground assignment and is labelled as such, rather than as a model of the quantified sentence. Displayed unary/binary relation symbols come from the actual input signature, including Q. Unsupported quantifier alternation, empty input and malformed input are rejected without applying a different default formula.
