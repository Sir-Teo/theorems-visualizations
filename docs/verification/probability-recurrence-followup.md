## Corrections and independent reasoning

### Symmetric return probabilities and neighborhood recurrence

The Pólya display retains the exact combinatorial return probabilities in dimensions 1–3 and the periodic local-limit asymptote. The constant 2(d/(4πn))^(d/2) is consistent with the two equal Fourier maxima for a period-2 nearest-neighbor walk and the quadratic characteristic-function expansion with covariance I/d. In dimension 2 the exact probability is the square of its one-dimensional counterpart, as in the source. Independent spatial dynamic programming checks every time through 12 steps in all three dimensions, and the 2,000-step ratios approach the displayed asymptote.

The displayed walk is thinned, but its bounds now include all sampled coordinates rather than only the thinned vertices. The final vertex is included. The caption distinguishes all returns counted in the readout from the first 400 gold markers, and distinguishes true three-dimensional returns from incidental visits of the projection to the planar origin. Neither a finite trajectory nor a finite partial sum establishes recurrence.

For Lemma 5.4.5, an infinite expected number of visits does not by itself imply infinitely many visits almost surely. The new argument fixes k, bounds by k the number of ε-visits followed by no ε-visit after a gap k, and uses independent increments plus the triangle inequality to bound that sum below by ρ₂ε,k times the divergent visit-probability series. It follows that every avoidance probability ρ₂ε,k vanishes. This is the actual source mechanism. The Monte Carlo plot is labelled finite evidence, and the Gaussian zero/nonzero-drift classification is justified separately by the recurrence results and strong law. The readout includes an estimated standard error of the finite visit-count mean.

### One-dimensional Chung–Fuchs examples

A vanishing median is only a quantile statement, not convergence in probability. The demo now adds an adjustable-threshold estimate of P(|Sₙ/n|>ε), with exact theoretical curves for the Gaussian, drifted Gaussian, and Cauchy cases and an estimated pointwise standard error of the final estimate. The median remains as an illustration. All five displayed signed-log paths, all medians, and the reference level 1 now lie in the plot windows. The unbounded Pareto inverse sampler uses 1−U, avoiding an inverse-power evaluation at a possible machine-generated zero.

The recurrence/transience labels do not come from sample frequencies. Integrable symmetric Pareto steps of index 1.5 have mean zero, so the theorem applies. For symmetric Pareto density α/(2|x|^(1+α)) on |x|≥1,

`1−φ(t) = α |t|^α ∫_[|t|,∞) (1−cos u) u^(−1−α) du ∼ c |t|^α`.

The positive limiting integral is finite for 0<α<2. Thus α=0.7 has an integrable one-dimensional resolvent and is transient by Theorem 5.4.10. Cauchy steps have φ(t)=exp(−|t|), a logarithmically divergent resolvent, and are recurrent even though Sₙ/n remains Cauchy. Nonzero Gaussian drift gives transience by the strong law. Removed an unqualified assertion that the absolute normalized Pareto sum tends to infinity, which did not specify a convergence mode.

### Characteristic-function integrals

The regularized display previously called a walk recurrent only when a finite ratio J(s)/J(10s) exceeded 1.5. That can incorrectly label the recurrent planar symmetric walk transient as its logarithmic growth slows; the clamped endpoint s=0.5 is another immediate failure of this rule. Both displays now use the mathematically known classification of the five selected walks and explicitly state that finite ratios do not prove recurrence or transience. The reference point is labelled s′=min(10s,0.5), matching the actual computation.

The dedicated helper evaluates `1−rφ` as `(1−r)+r Σ p·2sin²(step·y/2)` plus its imaginary part, avoiding cancellation in 1−cos at small arguments. All selected laws have the reflection symmetries needed by the helper. Plain one-dimensional integrals are analytic. The higher-dimensional radial grid uses true bin edges and log-midpoint integration; its angular rule is adaptive on a quadrant in the plane and uses a fixed spherical rule in dimension 3. The adaptive rule resolves the biased planar walk's narrow peaks near the transverse direction. For example, its I(10⁻⁴) changes from approximately 26.29 with the former fixed angular rule to approximately 27.02. The regularized integral includes a small central-ball contribution instead of silently omitting the ball below radius 10⁻⁶. All such multidimensional values remain labelled approximate.

The code's unit-ball computations concern the same recurrence test as the cube theorem: balls and cubes contain each other after rescaling, and the theorem holds at every positive size. No assertion that an arbitrary characteristic function has only one singular point is made; that observation is restricted to the five listed laws inside this unit ball. The zero integral at outer radius 1 is omitted from the positive logarithmic plot rather than replaced by a fabricated positive observation. Monte Carlo return-count means have estimated standard errors.

Twelve independent 65-digit reference values are stored in [probability-recurrence-references.json](probability-recurrence-references.json). They were generated by mpmath adaptive integration of the literal real-axis cosine/sine resolvent, independently of the production stable-loss formulation, analytic plain integral and logarithmic radial quadrature. The one-dimensional plain integrals agree within floating-point precision; the regularized log-quadrature values agree within relative tolerance 1.1×10⁻⁴. Planar regularized values also agree with a separate Cartesian midpoint integration within 5×10⁻⁴, and three-dimensional regularized values agree with an independent Cartesian ball integral within 2×10⁻³, and refinement plus the known symmetric logarithmic increment checks the angular/radial calculation. Finite quadrature convergence is not used as a proof of an infinite integral.

### Period, coupling and the initial law of a tail event

The period definition matches the source's recurrent-state formulation. For the finite ring/chord graph, all closed paths are concatenations of cycles of lengths L, effective M when M<L, and 1 when a self-loop is enabled. Those cycle lengths occur within the displayed 40 steps, so their gcd is the actual period rather than an uncertified finite-prefix guess. Inputs M≥L now explicitly say that there is no additional chord. Unit tests exhaust every ring length 2–9, chord parameter 1–9 and loop setting.

The convergence theorem now explicitly concerns a countable state space. The old orange curve was a Monte Carlo coupling tail yet its caption and readout asserted it always bounded the exact total variation. A finite estimate need not do so. Orange now comes from the finite killed product-chain recursion: start at (0,Y₀), remove diagonal mass as soon as the chains meet, and propagate the remaining mass with P⊗P. Its total mass is P(T>n). Gold points retain the independent 1,500-run estimate, with a pointwise standard error in the readout. The logarithmic floor is disclosed. The illustrated pair occupies the same state coordinate after meeting; the former unexplained vertical offset was removed. The periodic comparison keeps a positive coupling tail when the two initial phases differ, as it should.

For Theorem 5.7.3, the old conclusion “tail trivial iff aperiodic” omitted the initial law. With a deterministic initial state, or a law confined to one cyclic class, the tail is trivial even when d>1. The corrected statement gives the generated cyclic-class σ-field modulo the actual initial law. Its proof uses recurrent i.i.d. excursions and Hewitt–Savage, then transfers the zero-one value within a cyclic class, following the source. It does not use the positive-recurrent convergence theorem to justify a null-recurrent result.

The demo now offers three initial laws: uniform over every state, uniform inside class 0, and a fixed state 0. With no shortcut, true cyclic labels remain constant, but only the all-class initial law has a nontrivial label σ-field. The fixed-class examples visibly demonstrate the corrected trivial-tail conclusion despite periodicity. With a shortcut, an exact finite-graph period calculation gives 1; the wandering labels of the old partition are explicitly no longer called tail variables. Their finite-time wandering does not prove tail triviality or exact independence from the start. The right-panel range includes a class frequency of 1, and each paired bar is aligned with its actual class index rather than shifted one tick to the right.
