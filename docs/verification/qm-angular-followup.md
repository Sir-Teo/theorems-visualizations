## Corrected content and implementation

**7.2.** The orbital commutators act on a common invariant domain, such as smooth compactly supported functions. If `Jx psi=a psi` and `Jy psi=b psi`, the first commutator forces `Jz psi=0`; applying the other two then gives `a=b=0`. All three components annihilate that state. This supports the zero-angular-momentum exception without mistaking zero means for zero uncertainty. Pairing the anticommutator terms in `[J²,Ji]` proves the Casimir identity.

The active finite rotations are `RA=Ry(b)Rx(a)` and `RB=Rx(a)Ry(b)`. Expanding their relative product gives `RA RB⁻¹ = I - ab Az + O(a²b + ab²)`; the order of the factors determines the sign. Its finite axis generally has x/y components. For positive small angles it approaches **negative z**, while the positive principal angle approaches `|ab|`. The old caption asserted an exact z-axis difference. The new gold arrow and readout use the actual axis. Negative and zero controls are supported; an identity rotation has no unique axis or angle/product ratio. Quaternion composition extracts small angles stably instead of losing them to an `acos(trace)` cancellation. Frame endpoints still come from the actual SO(3) matrices. The original algebra check remains the l=1 finite representation, explicitly described as an illustration.

**7.5.** The overlap chart now includes all25 complex harmonics through l'=4, including every selectable l=4 state. It uses the actual selected complex or real function for both coefficients and normalization. A nonzero-m real mode has two complex-basis coefficients of magnitude `1/sqrt(2)`, rather than the old false single-unit-bar claim. The signed/imaginary coefficients include the realY convention's `(-1)^|m|` factor. The readout checks the full coefficient vector and squared coefficient sum, instead of calling one real-mode partner an erroneous overlap with an “other” mode. The m-control bounds follow l; unchanged values no longer trigger a perpetual invalidation from inside render.

Interior nodal latitude circles are distinct from nodal great circles through z (two meridian halves each). Sine modes vanish on the complete xz section; that empty section is explained rather than replaced with an invented lobe. Complex phase includes the polar factor's sign, and winding is asserted only on nonzero latitudes. The polar plot explicitly states its sampled-amplitude scaling.

Normalization/projection uses12 Gauss–Legendre nodes in cos(theta) and24 equally spaced azimuths. For the displayed l≤4 harmonics, azimuth sums eliminate unequal m and the remaining equal-m products are polynomials of degree≤8. Thus this rule integrates the displayed inner products to floating-point rounding; it is not an infinite-dimensional completeness test. An independent Rodrigues implementation verifies the basis functions. Completeness itself follows from harmonic decomposition of restricted polynomials and density of coordinate polynomials on the compact sphere. The decomposition uses `Delta(r^(2k) H_l)=2k(2l+2k+1)r^(2k-2)H_l` in three dimensions, with nonzero coefficients for k>0; the resulting harmonic restrictions span all polynomial restrictions. These polynomials contain constants and separate points, so are dense in continuous functions and hence in L².

**Adjacent7.1.** Two populated distinct modes are a superposition; a pure second-mode endpoint or two identical modes are still eigenfunctions. The readout now identifies those cases without claiming all w>0 states fail. Its finite-difference curve now has bounds containing the actual values; the old ±3.4 axis clipped the allowed equal-m=3, w=1/2 amplitude `3sqrt(2)`. No additional source-queue closure is claimed.
