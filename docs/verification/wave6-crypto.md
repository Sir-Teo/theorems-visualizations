## Mathematical and interaction contracts

- Inversion accepts any valid preimage. Image probabilities are weighted by fiber size under a uniform input, not a uniform distinct output. Candidate factor, discrete-log and subset-sum scenes count named elementary operations, not wall times, bit complexity or best-known attacks.
- The NP prefix relation includes the input length in unary. Exact finite oracle queries recover the lexicographically first preimage or reject an absent image; this finite exhaustive oracle does not establish P=NP.
- Hard-core prediction distinguishes computational difficulty from information-theoretic ambiguity. A permutation has a perfect unlimited inverse-table predictor. Goldreich–Levin actually uses nonempty XOR shifts, cached indexed predictor calls, all guessed basis inner products, majority decoding and final verification. Pairwise independence and Chebyshev give the stated bound, which can be vacuous in small cases.
- The permutation-to-generator reduction retains the signed acceptance gap and the complement orientation. Sequential hybrids retain terminal state, conserve mass and telescope signed gaps; averaging absolute gaps is not substituted for a valid uniform-index reduction.
- The general OWF-to-PRG theorem is explained through the modular HRV construction, with its deep inputs identified. The scene exactly enumerates finite uniform-fiber hashing and proves its leftover-hashing bound. It does not turn ordinary entropy into computational pseudoentropy or claim a finite proof of HILL.
- GGM uses fixed-length queries with a consistent prefix cache; repeated queries agree. Variable-length queries are rejected and their security problem explained. The scene never constructs an exponential full tree to claim the polynomial-query proof.
- Feistel uses no final swap and is invertible even for constant round functions. Three independent PRF rounds give forward-query security with explicit collision terms; inverse access is outside that theorem. The tiny-domain forward comparison is1/4 versus4/15, advantage1/60. The adaptive inverse relation has ideal-permutation probability11/35, not zero.
- Primitive equivalence concerns reusable schemes with polynomial key lengths and growing domains, including an exponentially large MAC message space. One-time and polynomial-domain information-theoretic exceptions are excluded. MAC/CPA converse arrows identify distributional inversion as a deep input. The exact eight-key posterior is an illustrative attack, not its general proof.
- Five worlds use an explicit randomized-heuristic/samplable-distribution convention and key agreement for the final boundary. Public-key encryption is not asserted equivalent to arbitrary key agreement. A finite distribution-weighted search cost does not determine the actual complexity world.
- Empty or malformed active inputs and sparse arrays are rejected. Hidden inactive candidate/round controls do not affect a computation. Continuous probability state retains unsnapped values, including10⁻¹⁰⁰; only actual indices/counts use integer steps.
