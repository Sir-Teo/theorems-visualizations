## Substantive corrections

**Tychonoff3.12.** The old text asserted faster-than-every-Gaussian growth at each fixed positive time. That does not follow from uniqueness and is false for this series: Cauchy's derivative bounds give Gaussian bounds with constants depending on the fixed time. What fails is one pair of constants uniform on a time strip touching zero. The general1<α<2 construction is justified directly: choose ρ with αasinρ<π/2, bound the holomorphic g on |z−t|=ρt by exp(−ct⁻ᵅ), then optimize k! (ρt)⁻ᵏ exp(−ct⁻ᵅ) over t. This gives a uniform Gevrey bound of order1+1/α<2 and permits all mixed derivatives termwise, including flat zero initial data. This derivation repairs the mathematics but does not substitute for an unread historical original.

The old numerical “rounding-error bound” used exp(−((1+ρ)t)⁻ᵅ) as a maximum modulus on a complex circle, ignoring its argument; large circles could even enter a sector of exponential growth. It also treated one omitted term and twelve recent terms as a certified tail. The replacement restricts circles to a safe sector and bounds their log maximum by −cos(αasinρ)/((1+ρ)t)ᵅ. It bounds Cauchy-DFT aliasing using a larger safe circle and bounds the series tail from B_k=M k!(x²/R)^k/(2k)!, whose successive ratios decrease. Floating-point evaluation still uses ordinary libm, so that component is explicitly an **estimate**, not a certified interval bound. Values and finite-difference checks are withheld where their diagnostics fail. The table-capacity guard also rejects an unsupported derivative count.

**Robin4.3 / shared solver.** Nonnegative Robin coefficients imply nonnegative spectrum; pure Neumann has a constant zero mode. LeftDirichlet/rightRobin has a zero linear mode at h=−1/L and a negative mode iff h<−1/L; leftNeumann has a negative mode for every h<0. The old root scan began away from zero and silently omitted small eigenvalues on either side. Scanning now begins at zero (with an explicit exact-zero branch), and a stable local power series evaluates the fundamental solutions near λ=0. Tested transitions ±10⁻⁷ that the old cutoffs missed.

**Orthogonality4.5.** A bilinear boundary form alone is insufficient for reality in a complex domain; the statement now uses the Hermitian form and real Robin coefficients. The energy identity includes both outward Robin signs. Removed the false implication that the displayed nonselfadjoint, nonorthogonal system is incomplete. The counterexample shows failure of orthogonality only. Zero-Neumann exceptions are explicit.

**Other scope.** The wave model states that a triangular pluck gives an energy solution with propagating kinks, rather than an everywhere classical C² solution. Parseval's squared-coefficient formulation explicitly assumes real-valued f. PiecewiseC¹ means a finite partition with finite one-sided derivative limits. The syllabus carries the same source references, corrected growth statement and semantic dependency.
