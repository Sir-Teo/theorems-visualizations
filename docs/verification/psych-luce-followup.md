## Theorem and independent derivation

Let T be finite and nonempty, with a probability distribution P_S on every nonempty S⊆T and strictly positive masses for offered alternatives. This allows a singleton's unique mass1. For larger menus every mass is below1 by positivity of the others. Empty menus have no distribution. The positive single-alternative choice axiom is `P_U(i)=P_S(i)P_U(S)` for i∈S⊆U⊆T, where `P_U(S)=sum_{j∈S}P_U(j)`. Finite sums give the corresponding event form for any nonempty R⊆S. Taking ratios cancels the shared factor and yields IIA. Conversely IIA plus the unit sum forces `P_S(i)=P_U(i)/P_U(S)` and hence the axiom.

Set v_i=P_T(i)>0. IIA and normalization give `P_S(i)=v_i/sum_S(v)`. Conversely the common denominator cancels in every pair's odds. If v and w represent the full family, full-menu equality gives `w_i=v_i(sum_T(w)/sum_T(v))`; a common positive multiplier is the only freedom. With theta_i=log(v_i), binary probabilities equal `1/(1+exp(theta_j−theta_i))`, and adding one common constant to all theta changes nothing. No absolute-strength or incomplete-menu identification is claimed. An independently specified positive cyclic set of binary odds3,3,3 has product27 and cannot share a weight vector, illustrating that pairwise positivity alone is insufficient.
