The new `source-audit-radiative.shared.js` integrates the full photon occupation `1/(exp((E−μ)/kT)−1)` over a sharp spectral window. For source fraction F=C(R/d)²≤1, net generation is `A=F[N(Tsun,0)−N(Tcell,0)]`. The photon current is `A−m[N(Tcell,V)−N(Tcell,0)]`, where m is the number of exposed emitting faces. Sunlight replaces the same fraction of ambient front radiation; other front directions and the exposed rear face receive ambient blackbody radiation at the cell temperature. A perfect rear mirror leaves one emitting face. These optical assumptions are stated publicly.

The photon tail is integrated analytically using Li1/Li2/Li3. Li1 retains the logarithmic lower-gap singularity; Li2/Li3 use the convergent Bernoulli expansion through B14 for x≤1 and their rapidly converging series otherwise. The independent quadrature caught cancellation in `−log(−expm1(−x))` at large x; that branch now uses `−log1p(−exp(−x))`. The derivative follows by differentiation of the same full distribution. Inverse emission is solved in `log((Eg−μ)/kT)`, retaining a positive open-circuit gap even when a displayed voltage rounds to Eg at extreme concentration. For a nonzero ideal absorbing step, photon emission diverges as μ approaches the gap, so finite illumination reaches open circuit strictly below Eg.

Single-junction maximum power solves `J−V mN'=0` by bisection. Since N'>0 and N''>0, electrical power has negative second derivative in the forward photovoltaic range. The 4.5 plot now normalizes current by Jsc so the full concentration range remains readable; it includes the true MPP rectangle and open-circuit endpoint. Its sunlight budget uses gross incoming solar photons for the absorbed gap energy, separating unabsorbed power, thermalization and remaining conversion loss. The purple remainder is not presented as a pure radiative-voltage fraction. The peak ring explicitly samples a 0.025 eV grid.

For 4.6, each junction absorbs **and emits** only in its assigned spectral window. This is a declared ideal spectrum-splitter/filter model, not a generic unfiltered tandem: there is no interjunction luminescence exchange. Series operation restricts every junction to nonnegative voltage and one shared current 0≤j≤minA. Since inverse voltage has V'<0,V''<0, the shared-current power has negative second derivative, so bisection solves its sole forward maximum. Independently loaded junctions provide an upper comparison for the same gaps and optics because the shared-current choices form a subset of independent choices. A three-junction independent device has six terminals, correcting the former universal four-terminal label.

The dark spectral density now includes the actual collected fraction: its per-photon work is `part.P/(q×gross solar photon flux)`, replacing the former use of voltage alone. Integrating that density over a complete assigned window gives the computed electrical power. The 0.3–4.2 eV displayed crop is disclosed; complete energy windows and their high-energy tails are integrated for all electrical calculations. Equal adjacent gaps give a zero-width window and zero series output without NaN.
