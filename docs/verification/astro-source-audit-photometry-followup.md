Recommended bolometric magnitude zero points, Eqs.(1)–(4),10pc standard reference and note4's **exact parsec=(648000/π)au**. Subtracting same-band logarithmic magnitudes eliminates the zero point, and applying inverse-square flux gives the displayed distance modulus.
- [Bailer-Jones2015, Estimating distances from parallaxes, author preprint v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.02105): complete **§§1–3 pp1–3** read, including all §2.1–2.2 likelihood/reciprocal example and §3 Bayes setup. SHA256 **8f86ef1b3cc7e9257ce6541e4d1620e7102bb1b74677137a27bf0fcd3184f14d**. The true geometric reciprocal is a very accurate small-angle relation; a noisy parallax and a probabilistic distance estimate are different. The first-order uncertainty formula becomes poor with increasing fractional error and negative measured parallaxes are legitimate measurements, not negative distances. No inference implementation is falsely claimed here.

## Corrections and mathematics

**4.1:** The finite triangle gives tan(p)=au/d; the modern parsec is exactlyau divided by one arcsecond **in radians**, rather thanau/tan(1arcsec). The small-angle relation is labelled approximate. Projecting the observer's circular orbit onto an orthonormal tangent-plane basis yields semi-axes1/d and|sinβ|/d; current controls cover0≤β≤90°. The red circle is an assumed-parallax-noise size comparison, not a fitted position likelihood. Its full extent is now within the adaptive field, including whenp≈σ (the old1.7max(p,σ) clipped a circle centred on a displaced star). Noise options10,1,.03mas are explicitly illustrative and no longer masquerade as current instrument performance. The threshold100/σmaspc is labelled nominal **linearized**10%precision. A lower signal-to-noise ratio is not falsely described as an ellipse inside the circle, and nonlinear distance uncertainty is not asserted from first-order propagation.

**4.3:** For positive same-band fluxes, m1−m2=−2.5log10(F1/F2), so five magnitudes give exactly100 and m−M=5log10(d/10pc), with extinction added only when modelled. Replaced memory-based named-star values with synthetic magnitude presets. Thresholds6/30mag are illustrative, not universal naked-eye or telescope capabilities. Absolute and apparent magnitudes must refer to consistent band/bolometric conventions; the cited IAU document provides the explicit bolometric example.

**4.6:** Removed unsupported catalogue star colours and placed explicitly synthetic blackbodies at known temperatures. Separate band zero points, real-atmosphere departures and the temperature/extinction ambiguity are explicit. The new scoped synthetic model integrates **energy** through the displayed Gaussian responses over **250–900nm**, using Simpson quadrature with1nmintervals; it is not a measured Johnson throughput or a photon-counting prescription. Its (5772K,.65) point is a chosen calibration, not a new solar-colour measurement. Spectrum and both colour curves now fit their actual raw extents, including the large250nm flux at30000K and900nm flux at2500K. The approximate Ballesteros curve is displayed only for positive temperatures within the plot domain, and is not claimed to invert this toy calibration.

For the toy bands, d/dλlog(RB/RV)=−(λ−440)/σB²+(λ−550)/σV² is negative across250–900nm. The Planck logarithmic temperature derivative x/[T(1−exp(−x))],x=hc/(λkT), also decreases withλ. Thus the derivative of FB/FV is the positive weighted covariance of these two decreasing functions under the V-band weight. This supports monotonic temperature versus colour for this fixed blackbody/response family, not arbitrary reddened stellar spectra or arbitrary filters.
