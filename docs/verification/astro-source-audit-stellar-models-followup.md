## Corrections and mathematical evidence

**4.10:** Replaced the inconsistent product of a ground-state Saha neutral fraction and a ten-level Boltzmann excited fraction. For excitation weightswn=n²exp(−En/kT), Z=Σwn, ground-only ratioR0, the consistent neutral fraction isZ/(Z+R0), and the total n2 fraction isw2/(Z+R0). This follows by replacing neutralUI=2 withUI=2Z in Saha balance. Orange is normalized by its actual maximum, since the neutral excited fraction is not monotone over the displayed range; its maximum is around32525K. The blue maximum moves from8238.266K at0.5Pa to9872.415K at20Pa and12962.030K at2000Pa. It is a model absorber-population proxy, not equivalent width, line strength or an actual MK classifier. The right bars and temperature bins are explicitly schematic. Unneeded unsupported original-priority and universal A0-peak claims removed.

Independent peak reference: differentiatingw2/(Z+R0) gives a root condition proportional to E2(Z+R0)−ΣwnEn−R0(χ+2.5kT)=0. The reference generator solves this analytic equation at65digits, while production numerically maximizes the population in log temperature. This finite-domain calculation does not validate a stellar-atmosphere approximation outside its assumptions.

**4.11:** Replaced all named-star values “from memory” with explicitly invented temperature/radius points, plus the solar reference. No catalogue accuracy or population frequency is asserted. Luminosities are constructed fromR²(T/5772)⁴. Removed the arbitrary radius thresholds that labelled any dragged point a white dwarf, giant or supergiant. Dragging now reports only its geometric radius; its evolutionary state is not determined. Constant-radius segments are analytically intersected with the plot rectangle rather than drawing clipped raw endpoints. Removed uncalibrated spectral-class temperature ticks. Actual hover reads the chosen synthetic model.

**4.12:** Replaced an unidentified memory fit with the actual six Eker Table4 calibrations over0.179–31solar masses, plotted as separate segments with no artificial bridges across independent-fit jumps. Synthetic dots are not the paper's observations: Gaussian log-mass and log-luminosity scatter is correctly labelled by its chosen standard deviations, rather than an impossible bounded “±” guarantee. Plot bounds include the actual noisy sample. Both window handles retain identity, clamp to the calibration range, and can pass one another without collapsing. Zero/small/degenerate windows give no fitted slope. OLS standard error is identified as formal and does not account for noisy mass, selection or model mismatch. The centre-domain exponent does not predict a slope fitted across several domains. The right curve is a constant-fuel M/L proxy, normalized to10Gyr at one solar mass, not an evolutionary lifetime prediction or a claim about40solar-mass stars beyond calibration.
