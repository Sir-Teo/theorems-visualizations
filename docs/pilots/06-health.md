# P06 · What can a counted breath tell you?

**Research brief · 2026-10-22 · D09 / D07 · researched, not authored or accepted.**

Connect a simplified explanation of quiet breathing with an observation task using a synthetic recording. Readers learn what is being counted and why a rate alone does not describe all aspects of breathing. This is conceptual education, not diagnosis, triage or professional certification.

## Sources and a recorded conflict

- **`nhlbi-breathing`:** [NHLBI, How the Lungs Work](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/lungs/breathing-benefits), inhalation and exhalation.
- **`openstax-breathing`:** [OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, 22.3](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-3-the-process-of-breathing), pulmonary ventilation. These sources agree that diaphragm contraction accompanies inspiration; quiet expiration includes relaxation and elastic recoil. Forced expiration needs a different explanation.
- **`openstax-respiration`:** [OpenStax Clinical Nursing Skills, 15.6](https://openstax.org/books/clinical-nursing-skills/pages/15-6-respiration), observation discussion. Its introductory mechanism paragraph reverses diaphragm contraction/relaxation relative to the two sources above. **Do not use that paragraph as physiological authority.** Observation instructions still require clinician review.
- An NCBI Nursing Skills candidate stopped at a CAPTCHA; no chapter body was verified and no alternate copy is used.

The conflict is a source-quality finding, not a correction we have made to the publisher. No clinical rate threshold, patient recommendation or diagnostic interpretation is adopted by this brief.

## Reader task and exploration

Audience: general readers, with a simple distinction between air movement, a complete respiratory cycle and gas exchange.

An original schematic pairs diaphragm/chest motion with a labeled inhalation–exhalation sequence. It is a qualitative model; an open, compliant lung is not an exact sealed Boyle's-law container. A synthetic event trace then presents complete cycles over a stated observation window. Readers count using an explicit event-boundary convention, compute breaths per minute as `60 × counted cycles / seconds`, and compare two traces with the same average but different timing.

State: paused frame, event markers, window length, counted cycles and notes about rhythm. Deterministic traces and event times make the answer inspectable. The screen must say the trace is synthetic; it is not a real patient record.

A future supervised-observation rubric may record what was observable, the window used, uncertainty, and what was not inferred. It must be approved by a clinician educator before use. This pilot neither collects patient data nor asks readers to provoke symptoms or alter breathing.

## Acceptance checks and access

1. A clinician checks each phase label, contraction/relaxation relationship and limits of the schematic.
2. Independent fixtures verify complete-cycle counting, observation boundaries and unit conversion; an incomplete boundary cycle must not be double-counted.
3. Readers can explain why equal rates need not mean equal rhythms or establish health.
4. The source conflict, unreviewed rubric and synthetic status remain visible until resolved.
5. Formative observation tests identification and interpretation, not diagnosis or clinical skill.

Pause, replay, step and reset controls must work by keyboard; no timed answer is required. Supply the event-time table and a full textual sequence. Motion respects reduced-motion preferences and is not necessary to answer. Any audio is optional and user-started.
