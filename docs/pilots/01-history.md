# P01 · What can two documents establish about the Zimmermann telegram?

**Research brief · 2026-10-22 · D20 / D28 / D15 · working prototype; independent acceptance pending.**

The first history pilot compares a decoded diplomatic message with its use in a presidential speech. It tests whether readers can distinguish a document's contents, a speaker's interpretation, and a later historical inference. This is a bounded United States–Germany–Mexico case from 1917; it does not stand for world history, archaeology, or all causes of US entry into the war.

## Sources and boundaries

- **S1, `telegram`:** [National Archives transcript](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/zimmermann-telegram), with RG 59 file references 862.20212/82A and /69. The proposal is conditional. These documents do not establish that Mexico accepted it. The decoded English transcript was read; scan pixels and the German original were not inspected.
- **S2, `wilson1917`:** [Wilson's April 2, 1917 address](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/address-to-congress-declaration-of-war-against-germany), RG 46, especially the passage using the intercepted note as evidence of hostility. Read it as advocacy addressed to Congress.
- **Method, `archive-analysis`:** [National Archives document-analysis materials](https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/worksheets). Use provenance, audience, purpose, and what remains unanswered.

S2 depends on the telegram it discusses. Two records are not two independent confirmations. Neither selected source gives Mexico's own account, the entire intelligence chain, or a complete causal explanation of the war decision. Those omissions must stay visible.

## Reader task and exploration

Audience: general readers, with an inline explanation of diplomatic messages and wartime neutrality. No existing collection is a prerequisite.

A source viewer offers four claims: a conditional alliance was proposed; Mexico accepted; Wilson used the note to frame a threat; the proposal alone caused the US decision. Readers select a passage, classify its support as direct statement, inference, or unsupported by this pair, and explain one limitation. The current prototype supplies source-dependence explanations and a short dated context list. A richer provenance diagram remains a possible extension; it must not invent dates or actors absent from verified evidence.

State consists of selected document/passages, claim, support category, and reader annotation. Every state has a reset and a stable textual description. No generated “historical certainty” percentage. Feedback checks evidence use and explains why more than one interpretation can remain defensible.

## Acceptance checks and access

1. A reader can identify the proposal's conditionality and reject the unsupported inference of Mexican acceptance.
2. A reader can explain why the speech is evidence of Wilson's framing but not independent confirmation of the telegram.
3. The answer key separates what the texts establish from broader causal interpretations; a historian checks context and dates.
4. Every displayed passage resolves to an edition, locator, creator, audience, and archival reference.

Provide semantic text, keyboard passage selection, buttons instead of required dragging and non-color evidence labels. If a provenance diagram is added, retain its linear textual equivalent. Images are optional enhancements; the task must work without them. Pilot observations should record evidence-selection errors and whether the limitation transfers to a new claim, without claiming learning efficacy.
