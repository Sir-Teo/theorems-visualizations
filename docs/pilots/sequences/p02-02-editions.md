# P02-L02 · Which Frankenstein edition?

Research brief, 2026-10-22. Parent: P02 literature. Audience: readers of the first translation-comparison activity; no knowledge of the whole novel assumed. Planned only. Sources: `seq-frankenstein-1818` and `seq-frankenstein-1831` in [sources.json](sources.json).

## Question and evidence

How can an edition change the evidence for a reading? Compare Victor's account of Elizabeth entering the family in [the 1818 witness](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41445/pg41445-images.html), volume I, chapter I, and [the 1831 witness](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/42324/pg42324-images.html), chapter I. These are Gutenberg transcriptions, not inspected first-edition page images or manuscripts.

In the first, Elizabeth is the child of Victor's father's sister; in the second, her parents and transfer from foster carers are described differently. Preserve the distinction between what Victor narrates and what an editor or reader concludes. The 1831 narrator's continued use of “cousin” in chapter I does not erase the changed family account. Comparing these passages supports a local textual observation, not a complete account of the revision or a demonstrated authorial motive.

## Planned exploration

A second prompt asks whether the evidence justifies a claim about Victor's perspective, the fictional family or Shelley's intention. Original editorial labels must be visually and semantically distinct from quoted text.

State: edition, comparison lens, passage locator, observation and interpretation. Save both edition IDs so an annotation cannot silently migrate to the other text. Provide stacked reading, semantic headings, keyboard selection and persistent labels; no drag-only alignment. Do not use text-diff volume as a measure of literary importance.

Success means accurate edition attribution, a defensible word-based interpretation and an explicit limit. A transfer prompt offers a statement citing the wrong edition and asks readers to repair its evidence. Feedback can accept multiple interpretations without inventing a single correct theme. Include a brief context note that the narrator's class and appearance judgements are being examined, not endorsed.
