# P08-L02 · A staging choice and an audience report

Research brief, 2026-10-22. Parent: P08 performance. Audience: readers comparing an authored staging plan with what people actually notice. Planned only; no performed scene or measured audience effect. Sources `seq-rsc-performing` and `seq-rsc-kyle` are recorded in [sources.json](sources.json).

## Question and evidence

Which decisions belong to a staging plan, and which conclusions require an observer? The Royal Shakespeare Company's [Performing Measure for Measure](https://www.rsc.org.uk/shakespeare-learning-zone/measure-for-measure/staging/performing) asks about setting, relationships, aims, positioning and status. These are prompts for work on a particular play, not a universal dictionary of gesture, emotion or body language.

The RSC's [account of Barry Kyle's 1978 production](https://www.rsc.org.uk/measure-for-measure/past-productions/in-focus-barry-kyle-1978) supplies a named production, designer Christopher Morley and descriptions of interpretive choices. Its critical quotations are reported judgments, not direct audience measurements made by this project. The page contains inconsistent role captions: its rehearsal caption assigns Richard Griffiths to Lucio while prose and cast list assign him to Pompey. Do not use that caption as a verified identification. Photos, prompt-book images and the performance itself were not inspected. Their licensing labels do not clear reuse.

## Planned exploration

Use an original three-line exchange, unrelated to the play's plot: A asks, “Is this for me?” B replies, “I left it here.” A says, “Then I'll wait.” The prop is an unmarked envelope. Prepare two staging scores with the same words, proposed timing and prop location, changing the performers' position/orientation relative to each other and the audience. These are intended constraints, not proof that actual performances differ in only one variable.

First let readers predict what they might notice and why. Then, only when real consented performances and observations exist, compare the plan with event descriptions and participants' reports. Distinguish performer intention, visible/audible action and audience interpretation. Ask what was noticed first and what supports a relationship inference; allow disagreement and an unknown response. Eye contact, mobility, hearing and culturally familiar gestures must not become mandatory assumptions or scored personality traits.

State: score version, selected event, prediction, evidence and alternative interpretation. Supply a numbered event list, text transcript, stage map and, for eventual recorded material, reviewed captions and descriptions. The text/map route permits analysis without compulsory acting. It does not prove equivalent perception of every performance feature or performance competence.

Record actual deviations, observer position, presentation order and access mode. A small comparison can reveal different reports; it cannot establish a universal or isolated causal effect of gesture. No fabricated participant responses, eye-tracking heatmaps or automatic emotion labels are planned.

## Preparation gates and release

A performer/theatre educator must review the complete original scores and observation task. Real performance, participant observations, access review and exact recording/transcript permissions remain open. Freeze the question and analysis rubric before collecting observations; do not convert intended staging into an observed effect.

The older play's status does not license modern production material.
