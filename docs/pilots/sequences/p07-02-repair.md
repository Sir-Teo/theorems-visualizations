# P07-L02 · What makes a patch attach?

Research brief, 2026-10-22. Parent: P07 craft. Audience: beginners comparing a repair plan with physical evidence. Planned only; no completed sample or practical competence claimed. Sources `seq-ifixit-patch` and `seq-sca-basting` are recorded in [sources.json](sources.json).

## Question and evidence

Does a drawn stitch path establish that a patch is attached? Eli Hardy and contributors' [iFixit student guide](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/How+to+Hand+Stitch+a+Patch+to+Clothing/172554), updated 25 August 2025, describes covering a hole with overlapping fabric, stitching the edges and avoiding excessive tension. Its text was read; photographs were not inspected and the repair was not reproduced. A student guide is not evidence of professional approval or durable performance of our design.

The Sewing & Craft Alliance's [Running Stitch: Basting, guideline 15.105](https://www.sewing.org/files/guidelines/15_105_running_stitch_basting.pdf) distinguishes temporary basting from other uses and limits its seam example to work without strain. Its instructions address catching the intended layers and avoiding bunching. Both pages and four figures were inspected. The page-2 code is 3/06; the page-1 rights notice is dated 2008. Neither establishes a current technical standard.

## Planned exploration

Design an original low-load scrap-swatch exercise, not a repair to safety equipment or a promise of garment durability. A schematic shows base fabric, a hole, an overlapping patch and a proposed stitch path. Readers inspect three deliberately different plans: a path through both intended layers in intact material; one that misses the base fabric; and one that fails to cover part of the hole. These are diagram facts. Material fraying, thread tension and actual attachment require inspection of a real sample.

State: visible layer, patch position, proposed path, claimed observation and missing evidence. A cross-section and numbered text sequence must expose hidden layers without requiring colour perception or fine pointer movement. A non-sewing route offers the same evidence-comparison task; it does not certify hand skill. Any hand-work option needs practitioner-selected materials, tools, stitch spacing and accessible handling guidance before authoring.

Prepare a physical reproduction packet: record materials and dimensions, intended layers, actual deviations and an agreed inspection method; photograph both sides and the relevant edge with permission; have a second person use the instructions and report where they fail. Record observed puckering or missed layers only if actually observed. A digital path check is never a physical pass, and a sample that survives inspection does not establish long-term wash or load performance.

## Preparation gates and release

A sewing practitioner must review the proposed construction and reproduction protocol before authoring the practical instructions. The sample, second-person reproduction, handling/access alternatives and learner evidence remain open. Freeze the limited claim and record failures rather than treating a neat drawing as a successful repair.

Use original diagrams, prose and later consented sample photographs. The Alliance notice permits specified educators/retailers to reproduce unchanged educational handouts and restricts other uses; it does not clear adapted figures or general website redistribution. No such source assets are bundled. No iFixit image licence was established by this reading. Exact assets and release terms require their own check. The P01/P02 first-lesson human-check removal does not extend to this brief.
