# P07-L03 · Locating a kente teaching account

Research brief, 2026-10-22. Parent: P07 craft. Audience: readers examining how a textile practice is represented and taught. Planned only; no weaving instruction, community endorsement or authorised pattern collection. Sources `seq-asare-context` and `seq-asare-workshop` are recorded in [sources.json](sources.json).

## Question and evidence

What can one practitioner's account establish, and what would require a particular object, maker or teaching session? [Kwasi Asare's website](https://kwasiasare.com/) presents him as a mathematics teacher and kente weaver and places his practice in an Ashanti tradition connected with his father's Dento Mills Kente Studio in Nsawam, Ghana. This is a self-presentation, not independent verification of every lineage or promotional claim.

His [handloom workshop page](https://kwasiasare.com/kwasi-asare-kente-weaving-handloom-workshop/) describes a teaching handloom developed in 2013 and an introductory workshop connecting design with geometry. The page is undated. Its advertised duration/group size is an offer recorded on a website, not evidence of current availability, observed learning or equivalence to other looms and apprenticeship. Promotional health, uniqueness and universal-popularity claims are excluded. These two pages are one provider's perspective, not two independent cultural authorities.

The selected research context is therefore Asare/Dento Mills in Nsawam, rather than an undifferentiated account of all Ghanaian kente. No course, video, textile object or loom was inspected. The UNESCO kente page presented CAPTCHA, an institutional inventory PDF timed out and a UN gift page returned 403; none supplies a verified inscription, object attribution or pattern meaning here.

## Planned exploration

Use source cards to separate a named maker's account, a teaching offer, a documented object and an observer's inference. Only the first two are currently populated. Readers identify the evidence missing from a claim about a particular cloth: maker and place, date, materials, structure, stated meaning, intended use and whose explanation is being quoted. Unknown fields remain visibly unknown.

For the eventual second stage, select one object or teaching demonstration with the practitioner's participation and asset permission. Record the selected terminology and language, the limits of any translation, and whether a statement concerns that object, this workshop or a broader tradition. Do not extract named patterns from photographs or assign fixed cultural meanings from colour/geometry. Any original generic interlacing diagram must be labelled as a construction model; it is not authenticated kente or evidence of competence in the selected practice.

State: source card, claim, scope, attribution and unresolved field. Provide a linear evidence table and text descriptions alongside any cloth/process image. A transfer question asks whether a successful generic paper weave establishes authorship, cultural meaning or ability to produce the selected cloth; each needs different evidence.

## Preparation gates and release

Before authoring the situated lesson, obtain a current practitioner/context check, choose the actual object or process evidence, verify the exact terminology and settle the scope of participation and reuse. No outreach or agreement has occurred. A public biography does not supply consent to represent a whole community. Differences among makers, places and traditions must remain expressible, not graded against a universal pattern dictionary.

The current brief uses links and original commentary. No website photograph, course content, video, pattern or loom design is copied or cleared. Physical making, learner/access observations and any new media require their own evidence; the P01/P02 first-lesson check removal does not apply.
