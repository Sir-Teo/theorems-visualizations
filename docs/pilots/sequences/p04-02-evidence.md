## Question and evidence

Which fact does a legal presumption address, and which remedy conditions still need checking? [Consumer Rights Act 2015 s19(14)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/19) expressly refers to s19(3)(b), (c) and (4), rather than the short-term rejection route in (3)(a). Its six-month rule concerns conformity at delivery, subject to the exceptions in (15). It is not a six-month unconditional refund entitlement. A later malfunction, its cause, the relevant conformity requirement and the available remedy are distinct questions.

[Section 23](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/23) imposes conditions on repair/replacement, including reasonableness, inconvenience and impossibility/disproportion. [Section 24(5)–(7)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/24) separately controls progression to price reduction/final rejection. Its refund-deduction provisions have their own conditions and time origin. Do not merge those clocks or equate a repair request with a completed repair.
