# P02-L03 · Who knows what in The Cabuliwallah?

Research brief, 2026-10-22. Parent: P02 literature. Audience: general readers; this extends close reading to Bengali literature through a specifically bounded English witness. It is not authored or accepted. Source: `seq-tagore-2518` in [sources.json](sources.json).

## Question and evidence

How does a first-person narrator move between seeing, hearing, imagining and attributing thoughts? Read “The Cabuliwallah” in [Gutenberg ebook 2518, The Hungry Stones and Other Stories](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2518/2518-h/2518-h.htm). Mini's father narrates the story. His account of the street incident is partly learned from others; later passages report Rahmun's imagined expectations and close with the father's thought of reunion. Those are different kinds of narrative access, not a single independently verified record.

The [catalog](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2518) attributes the stories to Rabindranath Tagore and says the volume was translated by several hands. The HTML header credits Tagore and C. F. Andrews generally; its preface lists several contributors without identifying this story's translator. Do not assign a familiar translator from memory or another version. The print edition underlying this digital witness and individual attribution remain preparation gaps. No Bengali original has been inspected.

## Planned exploration

Present a short ordered passage sequence. Readers label the narrator's direct report, quoted speech, secondhand account, imagined scene and attributed interior state. These are editorial reading categories, not exhaustive or infallible rules. Readers may justify a contested category with words in the passage. The final task distinguishes a wished-for or imagined outcome from an event actually narrated as witnessed.

State: passage locator, selected category, supporting words and qualification. Supply a linear text alternative to any viewpoint diagram, keyboard-operable categories and persistent speaker labels. Avoid a “reliability score” that treats viewpoint as an error rate. Do not turn fictional descriptions of Afghan traders, Bengali families or women into factual group characteristics. Contextual claims need separate evidence and review.

Success means naming who supplies the information, acknowledging mediated access and supporting a limited interpretation. A transfer prompt offers a statement about Rahmun's feelings and asks whether it is his quoted testimony or the father's narration. No reading of English diction is presented as a claim about Tagore's Bengali wording.
