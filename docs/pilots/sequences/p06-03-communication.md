# P06-L03 · Care communication and teach-back

Research brief, 2026-10-22. Parent: P06 health and care. Audience: general readers learning to inspect a communication exchange. Planned only; no patient encounter or professional certification. Sources `seq-ahrq-tool5` and `seq-ahrq-tool9` are recorded in [sources.json](sources.json).

## Question and evidence

What does a reply show about an explanation, and what remains unknown? AHRQ's **Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit, third edition**, [Tool 5](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool5.html), reviewed April 2024, frames teach-back as checking the explanation through a person's own account. A yes/no acknowledgement or verbatim repetition is insufficient. It supports small segments, clarification and checking again; this is not a test of unaided memory.

[Tool 9](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool9.html), reviewed September 2024, distinguishes language preferences, qualified language support and written translation. Those are separate requirements, not problems solved by repeating English more loudly or classifying a person as inattentive. Its legal-compliance statements are not adopted here as a current legal opinion.

The toolkit's practice anecdotes and general benefit claims do not demonstrate learning or clinical outcomes for this proposed artifact. No linked training video, external observation instrument or full toolkit PDF was independently assessed in this reading.

## Planned exploration

Create an original nonurgent, fictional scheduling exchange for a routine discussion visit, with an authored appointment card: Tuesday at 10:00, Room B; bring a written list of questions; contact the fictional reception desk if the appointment cannot be attended. These are scenario facts, not real clinic instructions. Include no fasting, medication change, diagnosis or emergency advice.

Readers choose an opening that takes responsibility for clarity, then inspect scripted replies. One reply acknowledges without explaining; one confuses time and room; another repeats the words but leaves a practical uncertainty; another accurately restates the plan. The next move is an explanation or question addressing the specific gap, not a score assigned to the fictional patient's intelligence. A new reply is revealed only after that revision.

State: explanation segment, reply card, identified uncertainty, revised wording and next check. A branch requests another spoken or written language; the task should recognise the need for qualified support without generating a purportedly verified medical translation. Provide full text, keyboard selection, optional note entry and no requirement to speak into a microphone. No personal health information is collected.

The transfer task asks whether a correct scripted reply establishes future attendance, understanding of treatment, consent or a real patient's comprehension. It does not. Success means choosing a non-shaming check, responding to the actual uncertainty and identifying the limits of the observed exchange. Keep automated feedback restricted to the authored dialogue; open-ended humane communication is not reducible to keyword matching.

## Preparation gates and release

A qualified health educator/clinician must review the complete dialogue, language-support branch, non-shaming rubric and use context before authoring. Actual learner/participant observations remain necessary under the eventual contract. If a later version includes treatment, consent, interpretation or patient scenarios, it requires new domain/participant evidence rather than inheriting approval from scheduling arithmetic.
