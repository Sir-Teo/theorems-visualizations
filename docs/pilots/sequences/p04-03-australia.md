# P04-L03 · A separately verified Australian consumer case

Research brief, 2026-10-22. Parent: P04 law. Audience: readers comparing how two systems organise a consumer-goods question. Planned only. Sources `seq-acl-c167` and `seq-accc-remedies` are recorded in [sources.json](sources.json).

## Question and evidence

What changes when the authority changes? Use the [Competition and Consumer Act 2010, compilation 167](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/2026-09-16/2026-09-16/text/original/pdf/4), dated **16 September 2026**, authorised version C2026C00405 registered 24 September. The Register's latest-version label matched that compilation when read. The PDF excludes uncommenced amendments and warns about modifications/transitional provisions; this is not certification of every legal effect on the retrieval date.

Read Schedule 2 ss3(1)–(2), 54 and 259–263, plus [Act s131](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/2026-09-16/2026-09-16/text/original/pdf/3). The proposed domestic, non-auction kettle purchase from a corporation uses the household-goods and corporate-conduct routes, pending specialist verification. The quality guarantee and remedy provisions are separate. Major failure includes alternative grounds and a multiple-failures provision. Rejection remains subject to s262; its period is contextual, not an imported UK thirty-day rule. Section 263 governs the consequences of valid rejection.

[ACCC guidance](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel) explains major/minor problems, supplier responsibility and practical remedies. It is guidance rather than the statute. The ACCC expressly says it does not resolve individual disputes or provide case-specific legal advice.

## Planned exploration

Create an original fictional transaction in New South Wales: a household buys a new kettle from an incorporated retailer for domestic use, with no resale/manufacturing purpose, finance or auction. Use clearly stipulated variants: an undisclosed dangerous fault, a repairable minor fault, and several failures considered together. The final specialist-approved matrix must identify the actual statutory ground; merely counting faults is not the legal test.

State: jurisdiction/date card, facts, guarantee question, failure classification, remedy conditions and missing evidence. Have readers connect each conclusion to a provision and explain what would change if a relevant fact were disputed. For the repairable minor variant, preserve the supplier-remedy stage and the consequences of failure/refusal within a reasonable time. Do not present every fault as an immediate consumer choice of refund.

The transfer task presents a UK timing card inside the Australian file and asks why it cannot supply authority. Then ask what fact is needed to assess the local rejection period, rather than requesting a universal deadline. Comparison should expose different rule structures without declaring one system better or merging their terminology into a single algorithm.

Supply an ordered facts/rules table, persistent jurisdiction and compilation labels, keyboard controls and a printable reading route. Do not accept personal transactions, calculate entitlements or direct users through a real claim.
