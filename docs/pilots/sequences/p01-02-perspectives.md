## Question and evidence

How does a claim change when it moves from a diplomatic draft to a transmitted proposal and then to later historical writing? Read the [German archive's description](https://archiv.diplo.de/arc-de/das-politische-archiv/das-besondere-dokument/zimmermann-telegramm-1433690) alongside [Mendoza Sánchez's Mexican foreign-policy history](https://www.inehrm.gob.mx/recursos/Libros/Cien_Interiores_Final.pdf), §2.3, pp. 64–66, and the first pilot's NARA records.

The archive identifies a corrected draft dated 13 January 1917 and expressly distinguishes a California/Japan marginal note from the transmitted text. The archive reference is PA AA, RZ 201, R 16919, pp. 1–3. This draft date must not replace transmission dates. Its modern description is evidence about the draft; the facsimile has not been read.

Mendoza discusses Mexican neutrality and attributes a gap in documentation of Carranza's reaction to Katz. This is a named author's later interpretation, not a contemporaneous Mexican response or national consensus. His printed p. 66 gives an erroneous May 1917 entry date in a sentence about Lusitania; the page image confirms it is not an extraction artefact. Exclude that sentence from the factual timeline. Retain the source as an object of criticism, not as an unqualified chronology authority.

## Planned exploration

Offer a linear provenance table with rows for draft, transmitted message, presidential use and later account. Readers assign each claim to its actual witness, distinguish a source's statement from their own inference, and record a missing document that would change their assessment. Include the editorial challenge: does later commentary count as a second contemporary voice?

State: selected witness, claim category, supporting locator and note. A reset restores the original task. Give text buttons and a list equivalent to any diagram. Preserve German/Spanish titles and explain language limits; any future translated quotation must identify translator and source span. Do not generate confidence percentages or invent a Mexican acceptance/rejection document.

Success means separating draft from transmission, recognising retrospective authorship, and withholding an unsupported reaction claim. Feedback should admit justified alternative readings while rejecting evidence that belongs to a different document. A transfer prompt introduces a marginal note and asks whether it belongs in the sent message.
