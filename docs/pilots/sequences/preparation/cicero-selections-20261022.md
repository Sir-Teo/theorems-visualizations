## Witness and selection

Both source HTML and [catalog](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/47001) are pinned by exact byte hashes.

Section/page markers and side notes are excluded from quotation text and retained as separate locator information where applicable. ## Argument and voice

Each paragraph identifies its narrative layer. Cicero presents the views of Diogenes of Babylonia and Antipater; one reply belongs to a hypothetical merchant, and some exchanges are framed as possible or prospective replies. These are not independently observed debate transcripts. The attribution of “the other” depends on the preceding exchange and is labeled accordingly.

The selection includes Cicero's explanation that the dispute concerns whether the act is wrong, followed by his own answer. His distinction between silence and concealment must remain alongside that answer. Readers may disagree with him while accurately explaining his position. The house discussion also permits a question about the relationship between the earlier no-misrepresentation condition and the later advertisement example; the packet does not silently reconcile them or turn them into current law.

Three proposed bridges are explicitly editorial, with source references and challenges. The Diogenes-side bridge is conditional: the relevant disclosure and misrepresentation premises need defense. Neither a normal market price nor a buyer's choice automatically supplies that defense. The Antipater-side bridge asks the reader to justify scope and relevance, while the Cicero bridge requires attention to purpose and the other party's interests.

Four activities cover attribution, an exposed bridge and objection, the retained authorial resolution, and transfer to defects. Future feedback may check attribution and labeling; it must not score moral agreement or turn a reconstruction into a universal disclosure rule. These are preparation cards for the required final philosophy/classics review, not an accepted teaching rubric.
