# P03-L03 · Reading accounts of langar in context

Research brief, 2026-10-22. Parent: P03 philosophy and religion. Audience: general readers; no participation in religious practice is required. Planned only. Sources `seq-sikh-faq` and `seq-sgpc-reht` are recorded in [sources.json](sources.json).

## Question and evidence

How do a stated purpose, a rule and an observed practice differ? Compare the Sikh Coalition's [“What is langar?” FAQ](https://www.sikhcoalition.org/about-sikhs/faq/) with the SGPC-hosted [English Code of Sikh Conduct and Conventions](https://sgpc.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Sikh-Rehat-Maryada-English-n.pdf), article XXI, printed p. 49. These are public accounts from named Sikh institutions; they are not this project's observations of every gurdwara.

The FAQ connects communal preparation and eating with equality, and qualifies floor sitting by ability. Article XXI links service and the rejection of caste distinctions, permits participation across backgrounds, and separately restricts eating from one plate to baptised Sikhs. Preserve the distinction between access to the meal and the shared-plate clause. Do not erase a qualification to make the sources appear identical.

The code's English preface is signed Kulraj Singh, 31 August 1994; that is a preface date, not proof of the uploaded file's publication year. Punjabi text has not been inspected. The two sources' institutional positions and English wording remain visible; neither certifies universal present-day compliance.

## Planned exploration

Offer short attributed source cards beside three claim types: stated purpose, normative instruction and reported observation. Readers connect a claim to its actual source and scope, or leave it unsupported. Example challenges ask whether welcoming all backgrounds entails permission to share any plate, and whether the account requires every visitor to sit on the floor. Feedback must retain the qualifications rather than judge anyone's religious authenticity.

State: source, claim category, evidence locator and a question for a future practitioner conversation. Supply a keyboard-operated ordered list and a full text route; do not require kneeling, a visit, eating or imitation of a ceremony. Use original text diagrams only. No sacred audio, religious image or simulated ritual is needed.

Success means distinguishing institutional explanation from lived observation, using qualifications accurately, and stating what a local account would still need to establish. A transfer task asks what evidence would support a claim about a particular gurdwara on a particular occasion. The answer cannot be a general FAQ alone. Comparing this with the previous argument lesson is a method connection, not evidence of historical influence or a claim that traditions are interchangeable.
