# P01-L03 · What can charcoal date?

Research brief, 2026-10-22. Parent: P01 history. Audience: general readers who can distinguish observation and inference. Planned only; no runtime lesson or accepted connection. Sources: `seq-pompeii-excavation` and `seq-pompeii-experiment` in [sources.json](sources.json).

## Question and evidence

What must be assumed before a dated mark on a wall can date a destruction event? The [Regio V excavation account](https://pompeiisites.org/wp-content/uploads/REGIO-V-EXCAVATIONS-ENG.pdf), p. 11, describes a charcoal inscription with a day/month but no year. It offers competing readings of its other words and an autumn-eruption inference. A [later park research paper](https://pompeiisites.org/wp-content/uploads/27_E-Journal_La-data-della-distruzione-di-Pompei_2.pdf), dated 12 December 2024, tests the argument that charcoal cannot remain legible for long.

The later paper reports a new inscription made in October 2023 and monitored through August 2024. Its experimental setting is specified, not an exact reconstruction of antiquity. Printed p. 8 reports retained legibility; figure 5's right image is labelled 17 August, while the prose's final observation is 24 August. Keep these locators distinct. This challenges one preservation assumption; it does not establish an eruption date. The paper also disputes treating 24 October as a manuscript-supported alternative. No independent collation of Pliny manuscripts has been performed here.

## Planned exploration

Provide four linked cards: visible inscription/transcription, inferred year, preservation assumption and eruption-date inference. Readers mark each as reported observation, interpretation or additional assumption. Toggling an assumption changes which inference is supported, not the underlying evidence. A separate panel compares the two publications' claims and dates. Avoid a probability slider or a countdown that invents a survival distribution.

State: selected source, premise switches, evidence category and explanation. Supply an ordered text table, descriptive labels and keyboard controls. Use original diagrams only; the activity must work without photographs. Do not ask readers to mark an archaeological surface or equate an at-home charcoal trial with the park experiment.

Success means recognising that day/month is not a year, a preservation counterexample weakens an inference without proving its opposite, and publications from one institution can revise earlier claims. A transfer task asks what new evidence could distinguish competing years. The answer key must never turn “not ruled out” into “established.”
