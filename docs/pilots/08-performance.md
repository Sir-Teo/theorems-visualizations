# P08 · One eight-beat phrase, two interpretations

**Research brief · 2026-10-22 · D26 / D27 · researched, not authored or accepted.**

Create one original short percussion phrase and compare two deliberately different interpretations. Readers identify an audible or visible choice, locate evidence in time, and explain a possible expressive effect. This is a narrow music-and-movement exercise, not coverage of theatre, dance, film or global performance traditions.

## Source and design boundary

**`carnegie-expression`:** [Carnegie Hall, “Explore Expressive Qualities in Music and Movement”](https://www.carnegiehall.org/Education/Programs/Link-Up/New-York-City/Explore-Expressive-Qualities-in-Music-and-Movement), on comparing tempo, dynamics, articulation and movement.

The resource supports a teaching approach. It does not establish a universal correspondence between an acoustic feature and an emotion. A licence on a separate worksheet must not be generalized to the whole website, orchestral recordings or scores. No existing musical excerpt is selected for reuse.

The first phrase uses a simple eight-beat grid within an explicitly named Western notation exercise. Other rhythmic systems and performance traditions require their own sources and practitioners.

## Reader task and exploration

Audience: readers without notation fluency; introduce beat, onset, loudness and duration using an event list as well as a score.

Begin with two versions that change one property while preserving the underlying onset pattern in beat units. For example, change articulation while keeping tempo fixed. A later comparison may change tempo; align by beat position, not misleadingly identical clock times. The reader marks one difference, cites a beat/time region, and offers two possible interpretations with reasons.

State: selected version, beat/event focus, playback position, comparison parameter and annotation. A bounded synthesis engine can make timing reproducible, but its playback must be labeled generated. It is not a recorded human performance. A subsequent original human recording can test phrasing beyond the control model after rights and performer review are in place.

An optional gesture sequence or tapping task explores embodied interpretation. Readers can instead annotate the event timeline. Neither path claims to measure dance skill or musical expression objectively.

## Acceptance checks and access

1. Independently verify the event sequence, tempo conversions, duration and articulation differences. Declare which variables are held constant.
2. Preserve beat alignment when tempos differ; annotations show both beat and elapsed time where relevant.
3. A performer/educator checks that the contrast is perceptible and pedagogically meaningful.
4. The rubric rewards a located observation and a supported interpretation, not agreement with one mood label.
5. A transfer task asks the reader to distinguish an observed timing change from an inferred expressive effect.

Playback is user-started, with keyboard play/pause/stop, volume, replay region and visible state. There is no autoplay or mandatory response within a time window. Provide event tables, score descriptions and timed captions/visual cues for structural comparisons; do not claim these fully reproduce the perceptual experience of hearing. If video is later added, check captions and relevant audio-description needs. Reduced-motion and no-audio paths must retain the evidence-and-interpretation task.

## Rights, review and next steps

Compose the phrase and create its notation and generated sound specifically for the atlas. Record author, licence, synthesizer/sample provenance and any performer releases. Prefer synthesis without third-party samples. No audio/video assets currently exist; acquisition and verification are explicit U2 work.

**Review roles:** performer/music educator, accessibility reviewer with media experience, and movement specialist if a movement assessment is added; all unassigned. Before authoring: freeze the phrase, variables, event representation and asset licence. Before acceptance: native playback checks, timing checks, independent performance review and reader observation.

**Existing connections:** `music`, `waves` and `art` are optional related routes. Do not edit existing music lessons or source-audit files to launch this pilot.

**Three-lesson sequence:** original phrase interpretations → original staging or gesture comparison with a performer → a separately sourced performance tradition with authorized contextual explanation. The follow-ups must broaden practice, not just add parameter controls.
