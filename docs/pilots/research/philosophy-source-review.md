## Two defensible reconstructions and objections

All below are ORIGINAL EDITORIAL reconstructions. Neither is an assertion that the source itself mentions modern neighbors, headphones, consultation, consent, sound transmission, or a fair schedule.

Reconstruction A — restraint using the Legge passage:
1. SOURCE: selected negative-form directive, with original exchange context above.
2. INTERPRETATION: consider whether imposing an unwanted interruption on another person would be unacceptable in the relevant reversed position.
3. SCENARIO FACT: one neighbor enjoys music while working; the other says they need quiet. These are stipulated facts, not a sourced empirical generalization.
4. ADDED NORMATIVE PREMISE: relevant comparison should take the affected person’s stated need seriously rather than assume both share the actor’s taste.
5. EMPIRICAL ASSUMPTION: headphones or moving the music can prevent disturbance without blocking the other’s work; feasibility must be checked.
6. PROPOSED CONCLUSION: use headphones or another feasible non-disturbing arrangement while discussing future terms.
Objection: the excerpt alone does not establish how to compare competing preferences, what counts as relevant reversal, or whether all unwanted effects should be prohibited. Quiet also limits the music listener’s use of the space. Reply/revision: make the stated premise explicit and revise arrangements after learning feasibility; the quote alone does not compute a unique policy.

Reconstruction B — responsive assistance using Matthew7:12:
1. SOURCE: full unchanged verse, with asking/good-gifts/Father/law-and-prophets context.
2. INTERPRETATION: treat another person with the kind of attentive consideration one would want in their place.
3. SCENARIO FACT: preferences differ, and the quiet-seeking neighbor communicates that need.
4. ADDED NORMATIVE PREMISE: attentive consideration includes asking about the recipient’s actual needs and seeking terms both can accept; consent/fair terms are editorial additions, not verbatim scripture.
5. EMPIRICAL ASSUMPTION: consultation and a quiet-hours/music-times arrangement are feasible.
6. PROPOSED CONCLUSION: ask, listen and negotiate a workable schedule, possibly combined with headphones.
Objection: “whatever you desire” can be read as projection of the actor’s own preference; one music lover’s preferred treatment may be unwanted by the other. Asking alone also cannot justify an unfair agreement or solve conflicting nonnegotiable needs. Reply/revision: distinguish an uninformed own-taste application from an informed role-reversal interpretation; add and contest the premise that the latter is appropriate. Mark that premise an interpretation/addition, never source wording.

These reconstructions can converge on an action while remaining differently situated. Do not assign each tradition one exclusive solution, label one negative/passive and the other positive/superior, claim text-equivalence, or infer influence. Both are contestable teaching reconstructions and other defensible arguments should be allowed. Swap who likes music; the tool should expose changed assumptions rather than award a moral correctness score.
