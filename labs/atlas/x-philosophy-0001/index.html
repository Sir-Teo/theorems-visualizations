Does reciprocity settle a disagreement?

Two passages, different preferences, and the premises between them.

Build a reasoned response to a disagreement over music and quiet. Separate the source words from assumptions you add, then test what changes when the roles reverse.

Local review covers source fidelity, evidence labeling and bounded prototype reuse. No professional specialist, screen-reader or learner session has been performed.

Two English textual witnesses and an original hypothetical. This does not survey either tradition or establish historical influence.

01

Read closely

Confucius as represented in the Analects; James Legge, translator

Reciprocity in an exchange

Quotation

What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others.

Editorial context: Tsze-kung asks for a rule to practise throughout life; the Master names reciprocity. These are Legge’s translated words in a transmitted dialogue, not an independently authenticated recording of a historical utterance.

A directive in a teaching sequence

Quotation

Therefore, whatever you desire for men to do to you, you shall also do to them; for this is the law and the prophets.

Editorial context: Verses 7–11 concern asking, seeking and knocking, parental bread/fish comparisons and the Father in heaven giving good things. Verse 12 follows with “Therefore”; the chapter closes by presenting Jesus as teacher. The exercise does not require religious assent.

  • The selected Legge ebook labels the exchange XV.XXIII. The corresponding exchange is 15.24 in Eno’s 2015 English teaching translation, p.85. This resolves this pair of locators only; no reason for the discrepancy or universal modern numbering is asserted.
  • No Chinese or Greek original has been verified. Translation choices can affect this comparison.
  • The SEP discussion cautions that removing dialogue context can distort comparison. Neither short passage exhausts Confucian or Christian ethics.

02

Explore the evidence

Original hypothetical: one neighbor enjoys music while working; another needs quiet to concentrate. They share a wall. For this exercise these preferences are stipulated facts, not measured evidence. The available arrangements and their effects remain assumptions.

Interpretation: a reading of the source. Empirical assumption: a testable claim about the situation. Normative addition: a claim about what people ought to do. Proposed conclusion: an action supported by the selected reasons; no automatic moral score is assigned.

Build your ordered premise list

Add a premise, then use Move up, Move down or Remove. Every premise below is editorial, separate from the quotation and stipulated facts.

    Read both complete editorial reconstructions

    An editorial reconstruction of restraint

    Source: Reciprocity in an exchange. The scenario facts above are stipulated.

    1. Interpretation: Ask whether imposing an unwanted interruption would be acceptable in the relevant reversed position.
    2. Normative addition: Give the affected person’s actual need for concentration weight, rather than projecting the actor’s musical taste.
    3. Empirical assumption: Headphones or another room are available and would reduce the disturbance without creating a comparable burden.

    Proposed conclusion: On these added premises, choose a feasible arrangement that avoids imposing the unwanted interruption.

    Objection: What counts as the relevant reversed position, and how competing needs should be weighed, remains contestable.

    An editorial reconstruction of responsive attention

    Source: A directive in a teaching sequence. The scenario facts above are stipulated.

    1. Interpretation: Read the directive as a reason to attend to how the other person would want to be treated in this setting.
    2. Normative addition: Ask about the other person’s needs and seek terms both can reasonably accept.
    3. Empirical assumption: A conversation and a workable quiet/music schedule are feasible here.

    Proposed conclusion: On these added premises, ask about needs and negotiate an acceptable arrangement.

    Objection: The actor can still project personal preferences, and consultation alone may leave unfair terms.

    These examples are not exclusive to either tradition. A source quotation does not automatically validate the added premises or proposed conclusion.

    03

    Try the idea elsewhere

    Apply one reconstruction to a shared office where one person prefers a bright light and another is sensitive to glare. Identify what you would need to learn before recommending an action.

    What a careful explanation includes
    • Identify the selected textual witness and its setting.
    • Label source wording, stipulated facts, interpretation, empirical assumptions and normative additions separately.
    • Explain a proposed action and an objection; swapping roles must not silently swap the other person’s preferences.
    • Allow another defensible reconstruction. Neither example belongs exclusively to one tradition.

    There is no automatic score for your written interpretation. Use the guide to inspect your reasoning.
