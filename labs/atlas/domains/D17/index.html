D17 · Orientation

Sociology, anthropology and cultural life

Social inquiry studies institutions, relationships and meanings in context. Anthropology adds comparative and situated approaches to human lives across places and time.

What this field asks

  • Whose category is being used, and what does it mean locally?
  • How does the observer’s position shape the account?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Institutions, inequality, population and social change
  • Culture, kinship, work and everyday life
  • Ethnographic, historical, comparative and quantitative methods

Ways to explore

  • Compare a statistical pattern with the setting in which it was recorded.
  • Read an ethnographic account with attention to voice and method.
  • Trace how a social category changes across institutions or periods.

Boundaries and gaps

  • The AAA introduction explicitly locates its four-field model in the United States. It is not a universal map of anthropology or a sociology syllabus.

Still to build in this atlas
