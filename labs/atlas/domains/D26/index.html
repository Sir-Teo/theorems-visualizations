D26 · Orientation

Music and sound

Music and sound inquiry joins listening, structure, making and cultural practice. Interpretation includes choices that a score or numerical description may leave open.

What this field asks

  • Which audible choice changed, and what did you actually hear?
  • Which convention makes this interpretation meaningful in its setting?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Rhythm, pitch, timbre and musical organization
  • Listening, performance, composition and recording
  • Musical histories, instruments and social contexts

Ways to explore

  • Compare timing and articulation while holding other choices fixed.
  • Relate a description or score to a listening experience.
  • Trace a recording’s performers, production and context.

Boundaries and gaps

  • The Carnegie lesson is an orchestral-school perspective. Generated audio does not demonstrate human performance or universal emotional effects.

Still to build in this atlas

  • Performance, composition, ethnomusicology, music history, recording, instrument making and listening practices
