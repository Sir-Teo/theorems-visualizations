D22 · Orientation

Religion, belief and worldview traditions

Religion and worldview inquiry studies texts, practices, institutions and lived commitments in context. It distinguishes a community’s normative claims from generalizations about everyone within a tradition.

What this field asks

  • Which community, place and period does this account describe?
  • Is the claim devotional, historical, interpretive or comparative?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Texts, interpretation and theology in context
  • Ritual, material culture and lived practice
  • Historical change, diversity and secular worldviews

Ways to explore

  • Compare interpretations without flattening their differences.
  • Read an object or practice alongside its historical setting.
  • Trace who makes a normative claim and who contests it.

Boundaries and gaps

  • The Harvard principles offer a nonsectarian study approach, not a theological ruling or permission to represent a tradition.

Still to build in this atlas

Core Principles

Harvard Divinity School, Religion and Public Life · Page inspected 2026-10-22 · source language: en

The approach distinguishes devotional claims from the contextual study of religion and emphasizes diversity, historical change and cultural embedding.

Reading scope: Differentiations; three central assertions; diversity and change · checked 2026-10-22

Source limit: A religious-studies approach, not a theological ruling or authorization to speak for any tradition.
