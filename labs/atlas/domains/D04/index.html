D04 · Orientation

Physics, astronomy and space

Physics asks how measurable phenomena can be explained and predicted. Astronomy extends that inquiry to objects and histories often known through remote signals.

What this field asks

  • What observation could distinguish these models?
  • How do instrument limits shape what can be inferred?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Motion, fields, matter and energy
  • Waves, thermal and quantum phenomena
  • Astronomy, cosmology and instruments

Ways to explore

  • Compare a prediction with a measured quantity and its units.
  • Vary an assumption and examine the regime where a model fails.
  • Trace an astronomical inference from detected signal to physical interpretation.

Boundaries and gaps

  • The linked physics preface outlines a calculus-based sequence; it does not verify every model or provide a complete astronomy course.

Still to build in this atlas

  • Experimental measurement, nuclear/particle/plasma physics, cosmology, planetary science, space observation
Place
Observation sites, instrument access and histories of physical knowledge need geographically varied cases.
Language
Scientific terms, notation and instrument conventions need explicit explanation for multilingual readers.
Practice
Measurement, calibration and experimental technique remain distinct from manipulating a simulation.
