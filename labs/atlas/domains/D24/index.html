D24 · Orientation

Literature, philology and storytelling

Literary inquiry follows how words, forms and storytelling invite and resist interpretation. A defensible reading accounts for textual detail and the history of the text being read.

What this field asks

  • Which words or formal choices support this reading?
  • How does another edition, translation or interpretive lens alter it?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Poetry, drama, prose and oral narrative
  • Literary history, criticism and theory
  • Textual witnesses, editions, translation and philology

Ways to explore

  • Annotate a bounded passage and explain the evidence for a reading.
  • Compare interpretive approaches and their blind spots.
  • Trace a text’s witness, editor and translator before treating it as an original.

Boundaries and gaps

  • The course foregrounds selected twentieth-century theory; the local haiku comparison is also bounded. Neither is a world-literature survey.
