D15 · Orientation

Politics, governance and public affairs

Political inquiry asks how collective decisions gain authority, distribute power and face contestation. Institutions can be studied through arguments, records and comparison.

What this field asks

  • What justifies a decision-making rule, and what evidence shows its effects?
  • Which voices and alternatives were excluded from the comparison?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Political ideas, authority and participation
  • Comparative institutions and public administration
  • Policy, international relations and conflict

Ways to explore

  • Compare institutions while naming differences in context.
  • Separate a normative argument from an empirical claim.
  • Trace a policy from proposal through implementation and evaluation.

Boundaries and gaps

  • The course syllabus illustrates inquiry; it does not rank all political systems or endorse a partisan program.

Still to build in this atlas
