D30 · Orientation

Sport, movement, play and recreation

Play and recreation join rules, bodies, environments and shared meanings. Sport and movement can be studied through participation, history, institutions and access.

What this field asks

  • How do rules and access conditions change who can participate?
  • What evidence distinguishes an intended benefit from an observed one?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Games, rules, skill and strategy
  • Sport, movement and physical education
  • Outdoor recreation, leisure, tourism and inclusion

Ways to explore

  • Compare rule variants and the choices they allow.
  • Inspect a participation barrier without assuming one body or ability.
  • Read a recreation practice in its environmental and cultural setting.

Boundaries and gaps

  • The UNESCO overview offers policy principles, not exercise prescriptions, clinical benefit estimates or sport-specific coaching.

International Charter of Physical Education, Physical Activity and Sport

UNESCO · 2015 charter; overview updated 2026-01-19 · source language: en

The overview frames sport through inclusion, evidence, qualified personnel, suitable facilities and ethical responsibilities.

Reading scope: Overview, revision context and The Charter in a glimpse · checked 2026-10-22

Source limit: The overview is policy guidance, not a training prescription, clinical evidence of benefit or every sport’s rules. Linked charter PDFs were not inspected.

Reuse scope

Navigation citation and original summary only; no source passage, image, recording, score or assessment is republished. Linked material retains its own terms.
