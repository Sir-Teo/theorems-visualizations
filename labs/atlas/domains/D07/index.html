D07 · Orientation

Life sciences and organisms

Life sciences investigate organisms, their processes and their relationships through time. Useful explanations connect levels of organization without treating one scale as the whole story.

What this field asks

  • At which level is this mechanism being explained?
  • What evidence could separate two accounts of a biological pattern?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Cells, molecules and inherited information
  • Development, physiology and organism diversity
  • Evolution, populations and ecological relationships

Ways to explore

  • Trace a process while labeling what is observed and inferred.
  • Compare organisms or sequences with their sampling context.
  • Connect an experimental result to the limits of its study system.

Boundaries and gaps

  • The Biology 2e preface supplies a course map, not a validation of every chapter or a clinical interpretation.

Still to build in this atlas

  • Cell/molecular biology, biochemistry, microbiology, immunology, development, botany, zoology, taxonomy
Place
Species and ecosystems absent from well-studied model systems need attention.
Language
Taxonomic names do not replace local names or community knowledge of organisms.
Practice
Microscopy, specimen work, experimental controls and biological data stewardship remain practical gaps.
