D28 · Orientation

Media, journalism, information and archives

Information work shapes which records can be found, trusted, interpreted and preserved. Journalism, archives and libraries share questions about evidence while using different practices.

What this field asks

  • Who created this information, through which process and for which audience?
  • What does the search system make easy or difficult to find?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Journalism, editing, publishing and media systems
  • Libraries, archives, records and preservation
  • Information literacy, search and source evaluation

Ways to explore

  • Trace a claim to the record that supports it.
  • Compare search strategies and inspect what each omits.
  • Distinguish source authority from popularity, format or availability.

Boundaries and gaps

  • The ACRL introduction is a higher-education framework, not a credibility score for every source or permission to republish records.

Framework for Information Literacy for Higher Education

Association of College and Research Libraries · Adopted 2016; revisions approved 2026-09-02 · source language: en

The framework connects inquiry with contextual authority, information production and value, scholarly conversation and strategic searching.

Reading scope: Version header, frame list and Introduction · checked 2026-10-22
