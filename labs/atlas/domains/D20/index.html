D20 · Orientation

History, archaeology and historical methods

History builds and revises accounts of the past from surviving traces. Archaeology makes material context central to questions about lives not fully represented in written records.

What this field asks

  • Why did this trace survive, and what is missing?
  • How could another source change the interpretation?

Territory to explore

An editorial outline, with overlapping boundaries.

  • Chronology, change and historical explanation
  • Archives, oral histories and material evidence
  • Regional histories, archaeology and public memory

Ways to explore

  • Compare accounts with their creators, dates and audiences.
  • Separate a surviving object from its reconstruction or later interpretation.
  • Test a causal account against chronology and alternative explanations.

Boundaries and gaps

  • A source-comparison pilot is a limited case. The AHA framework is not a complete world-history or archaeological field curriculum.

Still to build in this atlas
