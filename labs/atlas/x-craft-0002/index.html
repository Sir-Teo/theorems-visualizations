02

Explore the evidence

Read all three comparison guides

Compare covered area

The hole has area 20 × 20 = 400 square units.

Which patch covers all of that rectangle?

  • Through-layers and miss-base both cover 400; partial-cover covers 10 × 20 = 200.
  • Coverage alone cannot tell whether any proposed location passes through intact base fabric.

Drawing units are not material dimensions.

Read the missing base

The miss-base plan places every marked point inside the hole.

Why can a patch cover the hole while its proposed locations miss the base?

  • Patch material exists at those points, but the base has a hole there.
  • The other plans mark locations in both intended layers, even though partial-cover still leaves half the hole exposed.

A location predicate does not describe the whole thread path, fastening or tension.

Ask what a specimen could show

All physical verdicts in the preparation packet are unfilled.

What would need observation before calling it a successful attachment?

  • Inspect actual materials, fastening, missed layers and tension on both sides.
  • Record deviations and a reproducible inspection; later washing/load performance is a different claim.

No physical pass is inferred from a neat drawing or a correct calculation.

Try the idea elsewhere

Design a fourth rectangle-and-point plan that passes one drawing predicate but fails the other. Explain its coordinates, then name two physical properties your drawing still cannot establish.

What a careful explanation includes
  • Separate area coverage from layer presence.
  • Treat the hole as absent base fabric.
  • Distinguish a proposed location from a finished stitch.
  • Keep physical outcomes unknown until observed.
