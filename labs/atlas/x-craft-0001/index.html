Read closely

Hellenic Centre for Research and Conservation of Archaeological Textiles

A structure does not determine every material property

Editorial paraphrase

In plain weave, the weft alternates over and under individual warp threads. The appearance of a textile also depends on the material and the properties of its threads.

Editorial context: Original paraphrase of the technical description. It supports the generic interlacing idea, not this paper procedure, a unique cultural origin or a claim to represent all Greek textile practice.

What this exploration can establish

  • In this diagram, warp strips W1–W6 form columns and weft strips F1–F6 form rows. These chosen axes are not a universal orientation for every woven object.
  • Front convention: the weft is above the warp when the row and column numbers sum to an even number. The bit string stores that front relation only.
  • Back view means turning the specimen over about a vertical axis: top stays top, warp labels reverse order, and the visible top strip at every crossing changes. It is not a rotation within the plane.
  • A six-column checkerboard may look the same after reversing columns and swapping visible strips. Trace the labels to establish that the view changed.
  • The error control changes a symbolic crossing only. It can produce a run of three repeated crossings in an interior row. The repair control models withdrawing and reweaving the whole selected weft strip.
  • A real repair requires accessible, unsealed ends and support for neighboring strips. Those strips may move; physical feasibility and instruction clarity have not been observed.

Read all schematic steps and their limits

0 · Lay out the warp plan

Plan six pre-cut warp strips with labels W1–W6 and a front/top marker. Keep their ends accessible. A practitioner must first verify the real materials and temporary support.

1 · First weft

Plan F1 across W1–W6 as over, under, over, under, over, under when viewed from the front. The diagram is a schematic, not a photograph.

2 · Reverse the phase

Plan F2 as under, over, under, over, under, over. Adjacent rows now alternate at the same warp crossing.

3 · Continue the first phase

Plan F3 with the same front sequence as F1. Check canonical labels before comparing the opposite side.

4 · Continue the second phase

Plan F4 with the same front sequence as F2. In a real sample, record any displacement or assistance rather than assuming the diagram proves stability.

5 · Add the fifth weft

Plan F5 like F1. Keep the real strip ends unsealed if a later whole-strip repair is to be demonstrated.

6 · Inspect both sides and plan a repair

Plan F6 like F2. For a real demonstration, photograph the labelled front, turn about the vertical axis and photograph the back. A proposed repair withdraws the entire chosen weft, supports its neighbors and reweaves the required sequence. This still needs physical verification.

03

Try the idea elsewhere

On a new labelled four-by-four plan, explain which physical crossing a back-view cell represents. Then list what evidence a real paper sample would add that the grid cannot supply.

What a careful explanation includes
  • Name canonical warp/weft IDs and the declared flip axis.
  • Separate front-relative data from the top strip visible on the chosen side.
  • Describe the actual repeated crossings and a whole-strip repair, not an impossible isolated physical crossing change.
  • Distinguish symbolic consistency, physical completion and observed handling.
  • State which photographs, material details and independent observations remain missing.

There is no automatic score for your written interpretation. Use the guide to inspect your reasoning.
