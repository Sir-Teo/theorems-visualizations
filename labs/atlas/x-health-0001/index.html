What can a counted cycle tell you?

Equal averages can hide different timing.

Inspect two synthetic timing tables, crop the observation window and compare complete-cycle and onset counts. Explain what those counts cannot tell you.

National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, NIH

Air movement and a qualitative phase map

Editorial paraphrase

During quiet inhalation the diaphragm contracts and moves downward, increasing chest space as air enters. During quiet exhalation the diaphragm relaxes and air leaves. Air movement and gas exchange are different processes; a timing table does not measure oxygenation.

National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, NIH

Define what is counted before counting

Original explanation

For this exercise, a complete cycle is a supplied start–phase-change–end row wholly inside the chosen window. A separate rule counts start landmarks in a window that includes its left endpoint and excludes its right endpoint. These conventions can give different counts when the window cuts through cycles.

Editorial context: Original mathematical definitions for synthetic tables, not a counting protocol supplied or approved by NHLBI.

What this exploration can establish

  • Solid diagram segments mark an authored in-phase label; outlined segments mark an authored out-phase label. Segment widths represent time only.
  • The regular table has five four-second cycles. The varied table has durations 2, 6, 3, 6 and 3 seconds. Equal average counts do not make the sequences identical.
  • Complete cycles must lie wholly inside the closed window. Onsets use [start, end), so a landmark shared by adjacent windows is not counted twice.
  • A short window can contain part of a cycle and zero complete cycles. That is not absence of breathing. Outside the supplied interval the result is unknown, not zero.
  • All times are original fixtures. Phase lengths, pressures, volumes, effort and clinical interpretation have not been calibrated or measured.

03

Try the idea elsewhere

Crop the regular trace to 2–18 seconds. Explain why it contains three complete cycles but four onsets. Then inspect a 1–2 second crop: explain why zero complete cycles is not evidence of no breathing.

What a careful explanation includes
  • Name the counting convention and window before giving a number.
  • For 2–18 seconds, identify complete cycles 2, 3, 4 and onset landmarks 2, 3, 4, 5.
  • Distinguish equal counts from equal intervals, and partial information from absence.
  • Do not infer normality, illness, depth, effort or gas exchange from the synthetic table.

There is no automatic score for your written interpretation. Use the guide to inspect your reasoning.
