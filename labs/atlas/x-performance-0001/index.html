What this exploration can establish

  • The span is eight quarter-note beats, shown as two groups of four in this Western beat-grid exercise. There are nine tone events; eight beats does not mean eight sounds. Beat offsets start at zero; the first event is displayed as beat 1.
  • The original onset offsets are 0, 1, 1.5, 2.5, 3, 4, 5.5, 6 and 7 beats. Short tones last 0.12 beat and sustained tones 0.42 beat. Neither version connects every adjacent tone, so sustained is not labeled legato.
  • For a beat offset b and tempo T, elapsed seconds = b × 60 / T. Tone lengths scale with beats when tempo changes; oscillator frequency stays 440 Hz. Attack and release remain 5 ms and 15 ms.
  • Equal oscillator settings and peak gain are controlled signal parameters, not equal perceived loudness, timbre or signal energy. The master-volume control is not loudness normalization.
  • The sound is mono, generated from a sine formula with smooth attack/release and no third-party samples. The arrangement and diagrams are Codex-assisted original work for this pilot; no human composer or performer is invented.
  • Select a replay region and start playback yourself. Version, tempo or region changes stop the current sound. Pause preserves an approximate beat cursor; shared cursors do not establish what another person heard.
  • This is a local draft. A performer/educator, actual learner/accessibility observations and final release terms remain open. The no-audio event task supports structure and interpretation, not an identical sensory experience.

03

Try the idea elsewhere

Keep the onset pattern but compare a slower tempo with longer tones. Cite an event and its beat/second values, identify which variables changed, then offer two possible listener responses and explain what actual observation would test them.

What a careful explanation includes
  • Locate evidence by event ID and beat offset; include seconds at the chosen tempo.
  • Separate onset, tone length, gap, frequency and peak gain from perceived loudness or expressive character.
  • Give two reasoned interpretations without treating a mood label as an answer key.
  • Distinguish generated signal, human performance and listener evidence.
  • Explain what the visual/no-audio route can establish and what it cannot.

There is no automatic score for your written interpretation. Use the guide to inspect your reasoning.
