One pond, two translations

Reading choices in Aston and Hearn

Compare two historical English versions of Bashō’s poem. Locate a difference, distinguish observation from interpretation, and support a reading without declaring one final meaning.

What this exploration can establish

  • A translation is a particular writer’s rendering, not a transparent replacement for every feature of the source language.
  • The Japanese panel preserves a named institutional display text for context. The first task compares English; it does not automatically grade Japanese grammar or fidelity.
  • Hearn’s wider essay makes historical and linguistic claims that are not adopted here. This activity uses the selected rendition only.
02

Explore the evidence

Read all three comparison guides

Number

Aston writes “the frog”; Hearn writes “frogs”.

How might one pictured creature differ from a less individually focused scene?

  • The singular can invite attention to one movement.
  • The plural may invite a wider scene, but does not prove simultaneous jumps.

The English wording alone cannot determine how many frogs the Japanese poem denotes.

Movement and sound

Aston names sound before the plunging phrase; Hearn puts jumping before sound.

Does the order alter what you imagine noticing first?

  • One reader may hear the event before reconstructing the movement.
  • Another may experience the movement and its sound as one compact event.

Reading order is observable; the reader’s imagined sequence is an interpretation.

Punctuation and pace

The selected Aston display uses an exclamation mark and three lines; the Hearn display uses dashes without authored line breaks.

What pauses do you hear or imagine, and which marks support them?

  • The exclamation can emphasize an opening perception.
  • The dashes can suggest cuts or connections; they do not force one emotional response.

These are features of the selected English web transcriptions. This activity does not establish the original books’ page layouts or reconstruct Bashō’s punctuation.

03

Try the idea elsewhere

Someone says Hearn’s plural proves that Bashō saw several frogs. Which part is observable in the English text, and which part needs evidence beyond it?

What a careful explanation includes
  • Point to exact wording in the named version.
  • Separate observation, possible effect and original-language claim.
  • Allow a different reading when it accounts for the same evidence.

There is no automatic score for your written interpretation. Use the guide to inspect your reasoning.
