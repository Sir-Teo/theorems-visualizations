What can two documents establish?

The Zimmermann telegram and Wilson’s use of it

Compare a conditional diplomatic proposal with a public argument. Decide what the documents say, what you infer, and what this pair cannot establish.

Screen-reader and learner sessions have not been performed.

What this exploration can establish

  • The decoded message is presented in English by the National Archives. Its German original and the entire interception/decoding chain are not part of this exercise.
  • The speech refers to the intercepted message. It is dependent evidence for the message, while being direct evidence of Wilson’s public argument.
  • Neither source supplies Mexico’s reply. Neither isolates the telegram from the other causes of the US war decision.

02

Explore the evidence

Read every claim and its evidence guide

The message makes the proposed cooperation conditional.

Direct statement. The quoted conditional phrase directly supports this classification. The editorial context explains what the condition refers to. An offer under a condition does not establish a completed agreement.

Evidence: A condition, not an accomplished alliance

Mexico accepted the proposed alliance.

Not established by this pair. Neither selected document supplies a Mexican acceptance. Missing evidence does not prove rejection either.

Evidence: Neither selected document establishes this claim.

The border-threat wording could help frame war as a response to danger.

Interpretation / inference. This is a defensible interpretation of the rhetoric. The wording alone does not measure its effect on Congress or the public.

Evidence: A public argument

The telegram alone caused the US declaration of war.

Not established by this pair. This pair cannot isolate a single cause. A speech’s use of one document is not a controlled account of all causes.

Evidence: Neither selected document establishes this claim.

Try the idea elsewhere

What a careful explanation includes
  • Distinguish a record from an independent source of its information.
  • Name one missing perspective or link in provenance.
  • Attach the limitation to a specific claim.
