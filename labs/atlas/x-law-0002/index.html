02

Explore the evidence

Read all three comparison guides

Identify the disputed proposition

The May recording shows the stipulated malfunction; delivery condition and cause remain separate.

Which proposition does each card support?

  • Build a row for the recorded failure, a row for delivery condition and a row for cause; locate each source.
  • Ask what inspection or provenance evidence would distinguish the competing explanations.

Do not assign a numerical probability of winning or invent an inspection result.

Read the route before using a presumption

The named cross-references and exceptions limit the presumption.

Why is selecting a remedy route necessary before invoking s19(14)?

  • Trace the actual cross-references and keep their exclusions visible.
  • Explain how contrary delivery/cause evidence would require further analysis instead of an automatic result.

Elapsed time alone does not establish a complete entitlement.

Find the missing event

A request appears in the file but a completed repair does not.

What changes if a dated return-after-repair record is later supplied?

  • It can supply evidence of an event missing from the current file.
  • It still does not automatically establish every condition of the chosen remedy.

No universal deadline or procedural advice is supplied.

03

Try the idea elsewhere

Write a three-column note: proposition, supporting evidence, unresolved question. Then identify the exact provision needed for one selected remedy route. Explain why the video alone is insufficient.

What a careful explanation includes
  • Separate the observed failure from its cause and delivery condition.
  • Read the presumption’s named routes and exceptions.
  • Keep remedy conditions separate from factual evidence.
  • Treat an unrecorded completion as unknown, not as a completed repair.

There is no automatic score for your written interpretation. Use the guide to inspect your reasoning.
