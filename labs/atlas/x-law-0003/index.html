Australian Competition and Consumer Commission

Guidance has a different role

Editorial paraphrase

ACCC guidance explains major/minor problems and remedies. The agency states that it does not resolve individual disputes or give case-specific advice. Guidance is distinct from the statute and from a ruling.

Explore the evidence

Read all three comparison guides

Name the authority

The transaction and compilation are explicit, while the inserted timing card has no Australian basis.

What would be needed to justify a local rejection-period conclusion?

  • Identify the applicable provision and the facts it makes relevant.
  • Keep a separate list of unverified state, procedure and legal-effect questions.

Do not calculate an Australian deadline from the UK example.

Compare the stipulated variants

The variants alter the failure description, not the governing country.

Which condition still needs analysis in each variant?

  • For B, inspect the supplier-remedy stage and consequences of refusal or failure within reasonable time.
  • For C, examine the statutory combined-failures test; counting failures alone is insufficient.

The exercise supplies branch-reading questions, not a resolved personal claim.

Keep guidance and adjudication separate

A regulator explanation is available; a ruling on this fictional file is not.

What claim would require evidence beyond the public guidance?

  • An outcome for an individual dispute needs its facts and applicable legal analysis.
  • A current legal-effect claim needs the relevant amendments and application rules.

An official source is not automatically a complete answer to every case.

Try the idea elsewhere

Remove the foreign timing card. Replace it with the jurisdiction, pinned compilation, provision to inspect and two missing facts. Explain why a structurally similar problem can need a different legal analysis.

What a careful explanation includes
  • Identify authority before importing a rule.
  • Separate a guarantee, failure classification and remedy restrictions.
  • Do not turn several faults into an automatic major-failure finding.
  • Preserve the boundary between guidance and a case decision.

There is no automatic score for your written interpretation. Use the guide to inspect your reasoning.
