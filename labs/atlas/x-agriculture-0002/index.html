What this exploration can establish

  • Fixed profile and illustrative wilting baseline; no capillary rise, root growth, salinity or spatial soil variation.
  • Daily netting does not resolve order within a day, peak stress or transient drainage. Instant surplus removal is explicitly artificial.
  • An end deficit greater than capacity ends the model with no valid final storage or continuing unstressed-use trajectory.
  • Unknown rain does not become zero. Forecasts remain separate from observation records.
  • Equal final deficits do not establish equal paths or equally acceptable decisions. No trigger, crop-loss function or optimal schedule is selected.
  • The uniform field calculation is only an average toy budget. The two-sector counterexample exposes why that average cannot represent the last sector.
  • The deficit balance is start + crop use − rain − incoming irrigation. Negative raw deficit becomes an artificial surplus outflow; deficit above capacity terminates the model.
  • The four paths retain separate forecast and observation labels. Both are fictional; neither label turns the numbers into an actual weather record.

Delivery and evidence boundaries

  • A 0.50-inch gross request takes 20 hours; 0.60 takes 24. A 0.75-inch request takes 30 hours and cannot be silently delivered in one day.
  • A fictional boundary gauge already reporting 0.40 inches yields deficit 1.00 + 0.25 − 0.10 − 0.40 = 0.75 inches. Multiplying that incoming value by 0.80 again would produce an incorrect 0.83.
  • With starting deficit 0.20, crop use 0.10, rain 1.00 and gross irrigation 0.50, delivery loss is 0.10 and raw deficit is −1.10. The separate surplus is 1.10; ending deficit is zero. Delivery loss and surplus are not the same quantity.
  • Starting deficit 3.10 plus crop use 0.25 without input exceeds the assumed 3.24 capacity. Do not continue an unstressed-use trajectory or invent negative storage.
  • Unknown rain remains unknown. A later hypothetical estimate changing deficit from 1.40 to 1.10 is a recorded −0.30 reconciliation, not invented rainfall. With next-day use 0.25 and incoming irrigation 0.40, the revised branch ends at 0.95.
  • Two equal-area sectors ending at deficits 0.85 and 1.25 have mean 1.05, which describes neither sector. Delivering 0.50 inches to one half is 0.25 field-equivalent inches and takes 10 hours at the assumed field-equivalent rate.

Try the idea elsewhere

Compare forecast-early and forecast-late: explain their equal 0.55-inch ending deficit and different daily maxima. Then replace the forecast rain with the fictional observed value. Identify what still prevents a field recommendation.

What a careful explanation includes
  • State units, boundaries and which inputs are assumptions.
  • Keep forecast and observation histories distinct.
  • Respect finite delivery hours and retain missing or out-of-range results.
  • Do not treat an estimation correction as a water flux or a field average as every location.
